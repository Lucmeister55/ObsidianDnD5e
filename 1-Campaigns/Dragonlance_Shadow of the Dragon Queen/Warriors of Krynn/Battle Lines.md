# Battle Lines

*Back to [[Warriors of Krynn]]*

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. Facts from the chapter notes, linked per scenario.

Short lines to say between turns in a *Warriors of Krynn* game. They are written plainly, the way a real battle went: long waits, short bursts of fighting, tired men, ammunition running out, orders arriving late, and most of the dying done by the side that ran. The point is to make the board feel like a place with logistics, not to make anyone feel anything.

**How to use them.**
- Say **one line at the start of each round**, and one whenever something happens on the board.
- **[Brackets]** are for you to fill in: a unit on the board, an NPC who's there, a PC.
- Each round, **ask one player** what their character sees from where they stand. It keeps the PCs on the field instead of above it.
- Use **one or two campaign threads** per game, and the scenario's own lines for the opening, the turn and the ending.

## How Battles Actually Went

Keep these in mind and most of the lines write themselves.

- **Most of a battle is waiting.** Armies spent hours forming up, shouting, and shooting at each other from a distance. Melee came in short bursts, because nobody can fight at full strength for more than a few minutes in armour.
- **Only the front rank fights.** Everyone behind it pushes, waits, or shoots. A unit is as strong as its front.
- **Units rotate.** Tired front ranks pull back and fresh ones step up. A line that can't rotate collapses even without losses.
- **Breaking is catastrophic, fighting isn't.** Most casualties on the losing side happened during the rout, when men with their backs turned were ridden down. The fight itself was often brief.
- **Orders are late.** Horns, banners and runners carried them, and dust, smoke and noise blocked all three. By the time an order arrived, the situation had changed.
- **Ammunition runs out.** An archer carried a few dozen arrows, and a unit shooting steadily emptied its quivers in minutes. Carts resupplied them, if they could get through.
- **Water and heat decide long battles.** Armoured men in the sun without water are done in a few hours.
- **Terrain does most of the work.** A river, a slope, a wood or a wall anchors a flank. A flank in the open is a flank that gets turned.
- **Reserves win battles.** Whoever still has a fresh unit at the end usually wins.
- **Looting stalls pursuit.** A victorious army that finds the baggage train stops chasing.
- **The wounded are a burden.** They need carrying, and every man carrying one isn't fighting.

## Generic

### Opening

1. Both sides spend the first hour forming up. Neither wants to be the one to advance uphill.
2. The front rank is checking its footing. The ground is churned from the night's rain.
3. Officers ride along the line, repeating the same order to every unit. Hold until the horn.
4. Skirmishers go out in front to throw insults and the occasional javelin. Nothing is decided yet.
5. [NPC] counts the enemy's banners and says the number out loud. It is bigger than the briefing said.
6. Carts of spare arrows and water are being parked behind the line, where they can be reached.

### Our Line Holds

1. The front rank has been in contact for ten minutes. A sergeant calls the rotation: the second rank steps forward, the first falls back to breathe.
2. The line has held, but the men in the front are slowing. The enemy's front is slowing too.
3. The enemy charge hits the shields and stops. For a while it's just pushing.
4. Wounded are being dragged out through the back. Each one takes two men away from the line.

### Our Unit Breaks

1. [Unit] breaks when the unit beside it does. Nobody ordered it. They simply saw their neighbours go.
2. The line has a gap, and the enemy's reserves are already moving into it.
3. The men who run are not fleeing a fight they were losing. They are fleeing because the ones behind them ran first.
4. The rout will cost more than the fight did. Horse is already going after the stragglers.

### Enemy Unit Breaks

1. Their front rank drops back, and the unit behind doesn't step up. They are out of men who will.
2. Dragon Army soldiers drop their shields to run faster. Their officers ride after them, and some of them stop running.
3. A brass falls and turns to stone. The corpse is now a lump the next rank has to climb over, which slows them.
4. A copper falls and its body dissolves into acid. The men next to it have to give ground while it pools.
5. A bronze falls and its bones go off. The shrapnel hurts the units around it more than the blast itself.
6. Their banner is down. Without it, the units that were taking orders from it have no idea where to go.

### Enemy Comes On

1. A second line is moving up behind the first. They are clean, dry, and have not fought today.
2. A horn, off to the flank. A fresh unit is taking position.
3. Ogres arrive at the front. They are slow to turn, and the line has had to widen to let them through.
4. Their baggage train is behind the hill. They have enough bolts for another hour.

### From Above

1. A dragonnel makes one pass, drops its load, and climbs. It will need to land and rearm before it can come again.
2. Fire from the air burns the grass between the lines. Neither side can advance through it for a while.
3. Mounts panic when the shadow crosses. Cavalry are the first to lose control.
4. *(Only with a dragon on the field.)* The dragon is the one thing both lines are watching, and neither is watching the ground in front of them.

### Weather and Ground

1. The rain makes the ground slick and the bowstrings slack. Archers are shooting short.
2. The sun is low behind the enemy. Looking that way is painful, and they know it.
3. Smoke from the burning grass is blowing across our line. Nobody can read a banner at fifty paces.
4. The ground in front of the line is a ploughed field. The charge will arrive tired.

### A Hero Does Something

1. The unit next to [PC] sees what they did, and holds when it otherwise wouldn't have.
2. The enemy unit facing [PC] has stopped advancing. Their sergeant is looking for another way round.
3. [NPC] notes where [PC] is standing and sends a runner to tell the commander.

### Lull

1. Both lines pull back to rest. Men drink and sit down in their ranks. Officers count their units.
2. Water carts are going down the line. They run dry about halfway.
3. The wounded are carried to the rear, where the surgeons set up under a tarpaulin. The rest of the army can hear them.
4. Quivers are refilled, if the carts reached them.
5. The enemy is also resting. Whoever starts next has the advantage of fresher men.

### Endings

- **Win:** The enemy line goes, and our men chase until they find the baggage train. Then they stop, because that's worth more.
- **Win:** The field is ours, and nobody has the strength to pursue. Tomorrow's count will say what it cost.
- **Hold:** Neither side broke. Both pulled back at dusk, to bury their dead and count their ammunition.
- **Hold:** The horns sound the withdrawal. The army is intact. The ground is lost.
- **Loss:** The line is gone. What's left is running, and the cavalry is going after them.
- **Loss:** The commanders gather what's left and fall back to the next defensible ground, with the baggage train if they can.

## When This Happens

Small things to say when something happens on the board, so a destroyed unit has consequences. Swap in the real unit's name.

### When a unit is destroyed, say what changes

- **Infantry:** The line is gone, and so is the cover the archers had. They are now the front.
- **Archers:** The volleys stop. The enemy's infantry no longer has to advance under fire.
- **Cavalry or knights:** The flank they were guarding is open. The horses scatter, and the infantry beside them have nothing to stop a charge.
- **Siege engine:** The engine and its crew are lost. The enemy loses its ranged threat. Reloading would have taken minutes anyway.
- **Mages:** The wards they held go down. Whatever they were covering is exposed.
- **Draconians:** A brass turns to stone and blocks the way. A copper leaves a pool of acid. A bronze scatters bone that hurts anyone adjacent. A silver takes the shape of the one who killed it, and for a moment nobody knows who's who.
- **Dragonnel:** It crashes where it falls, and the rider is lost. The aerial threat is gone for this turn.
- **Ogres:** It takes the whole unit to bring one down, and the gap it leaves is wide enough to charge through.
- **A commander:** The unit nearest them doesn't know what to do. For the next few minutes, nobody gives orders.

### When one unit threatens another

- **Infantry goes down, the archers behind it are charged.** Archers have no formation to fight in melee. They drop their bows and run, or die where they stand.
- **Archers fall, the infantry advances with no cover.** Now only the other side is shooting, and the advance across open ground is slow.
- **Cavalry goes round the flank.** The line has to turn to face it, and a line that's turning isn't fighting.
- **A unit is surrounded.** It can't rotate, can't retreat, and can't get water. It will break in a few minutes whatever the fighting looks like.
- **Engines are reached.** The crews are not soldiers. They drop their tools and run.
- **A unit of militia meets draconians.** Untrained men don't hold against something that big. Half will break before contact.
- **A dragonnel attack lands on a unit.** The fire kills a few. The unit's discipline breaks the rest.

### When your side is winning or losing

- **First unit lost on our side:** The commander sends a reserve to fill the gap, and the reserve is now committed.
- **Half our units are gone:** The commander has to choose between holding and withdrawing in good order. Wait too long and it becomes a rout.
- **Our last reserve goes in:** There is nothing left to commit. If this fails, the battle is lost.
- **First enemy unit lost:** The enemy commander shifts a reserve to cover it.
- **Enemy commander falls:** Their orders stop. Units hold position, waiting to be told what to do.
- **A unit holds against the odds:** The neighbouring units stay because it stays. That is why it matters.

### When a hero acts

- **A PC joins a unit:** The sergeant notes it and sends a runner to the commander. The unit fights a little longer.
- **A PC leaves a unit to fight a monster:** The unit has to hold without them, and the sergeant knows it.
- **A PC saves a unit:** The soldiers fall in around the PC. They'll follow their orders for the rest of the day.
- **A PC fails:** Say it plainly and move on. The battle doesn't wait.

## Campaign Threads

Lines that put the rest of the campaign on the board. Use one when that faction's units are on the field, or when the party stands well or badly with it ([[Faction Rules]]).

- **Military:** A runner arrives with Vendri's orders, word for word, exactly as written. They don't allow for what's happened since.
- **Knights:** The knights insist on their own ground. It's the best ground on the field, and they will not give it up.
- **Mages:** The mages are spent. They can do one more thing today, and it has to be chosen now.
- **Guilds:** A cart of fresh bolts is ready, but it can only reach the line by the road the enemy is watching.
- **Smallfolk:** The militia is full of men who have never held a pike. They hold anyway, because the wall is their home.
- **Firefishers:** A dockside crew has turned up with a handcart of knives, grappling hooks and rope. Nobody asked. Before Soth's massacre, it's Marta Dace's doing. After it, Mister Fettuccini's.
- **Home:** The militia from Vogler know the ground and the people. They are better fighters than their equipment says.
- **The cost:** Somewhere in the line is a face the party knows, from a mission, a tavern or a downtime. Tell them where.
- **The season:** Look at the calendar on the table and say the weather out loud: the cold, the light, how long until dark.

## Scenario Lines

### Scenario 0: Introduction

A practice game with no ties to the story. Use the generic lines.

### Scenario 1: Battle of Vogler

*[[2.02 Preparing for the Worst#Scenario 1: Battle of Vogler|2.02]]*

- **Opening:** The pass is narrow, between the sea and the cliffs. A hundred and fifty of Cudgel's mercenaries hold it, half of them in borrowed armour.
- The point of the pass is that it forces the enemy into a bottleneck. It doesn't matter how many they have if only a few can fight at once.
- Behind the line, carts leave Vogler by the inland road. Each hour held is a few more carts clear.
- Becklin is behind the line, relaying orders through runners. Jeyev stays close to her.
- **If the mercenaries' line buckles:** Cudgel rides into the gap himself.
- **If an evacuation cart is in reach:** a wheel has come off on the inland road, and the family is pulling the cart by hand.
- **The turn:** Smoke, banners, and a warhorn from the hills. A second force, many times larger, is coming.
- **Ending:** Becklin orders everyone back to Vogler. The pass is no longer worth holding.

### Scenario 2: Marshal's Gambit

*[[3.05 Mission 2 - Ambushing the Enemy#Scenario 2: Marshal's Gambit|3.05]]*

- **Opening:** Six hundred soldiers lie in the long grass, waiting for a column of eight hundred to walk into them. An ambush is only as good as the discipline of the men who wait.
- The column is on the road and strung out, as columns are. The head and the tail are a long way apart.
- **The choice:** A column of farmers is on the road between the armies. *(See the faction choice in 3.05.)*
- **If an ambushing unit is spotted early:** a horn sounds too soon, from somewhere down the line. The plan is over, and the generals know it.
- **If the column's rear guard is destroyed:** the farmers' carts are unprotected.
- A runner from Vendri: "The Marshal says, now."
- **Ending:** Vendri will want a written report by morning.

### Scenario 3: Engines of War

*[[3.07 Mission 3 - Draconian Blockade#Scenario 3: Engines of War|3.07]]*

- **Opening:** The enemy engines are slow. Six of them, each pulled by a team of oxen, and they have to be unlimbered and ranged before they can fire.
- The first shots are ranging shots, and they miss. The second round will not.
- The crews are exposed whenever they reload. That's the opening.
- A Mason from the guild rode out with you and is sketching the nearest engine. He can't be stopped.
- **If an engine is destroyed:** it burns slowly and comes apart one beam at a time.
- **If the oxen are killed:** the engine can't move. It's now a fixed position.
- **The choice:** Burn them, or take one home for Uri Stonegrey. *(See the faction choice in 3.07.)*
- **Ending:** On a win, Tatina will want to see it before anyone else touches it.

### Scenario 4: Catastrophe

*[[3.12 Steel Springs#Scenario 4: Catastrophe|3.12]]*

- **Opening:** Kalaman's army has marched thirty-five miles and is tired. The enemy is rested, and there are more of them than the outriders counted.
- Most of Kansaldi's force is draconians, who need no tents or fires. The scouts counted what an army of men looks like, so the numbers were wrong.
- Ignia passes low over the field. Kansaldi is on her back.
- Kalaman's units are retreating in disorder, and the order has come too late to help.
- **If a Kalaman unit is destroyed:** another banner goes down, and the units next to it are now flanked.
- **If an enemy unit is destroyed:** more come out of the old diggings round the springs.
- **If Darrett's unit is hurt:** Darrett has Lord Bakaris on his own saddle and is leaving the field.
- **The turn:** Ignia breathes a column of fire straight up. The Dragon Army's horns answer it, and its line falls back.
- **Ending:** The ford over the Inkwater is behind the army, and the wounded are already in the water. *(Go to the Inkwater skirmish.)*

### Scenario 5: Valley of Barbed Tails

*[[3.12 Steel Springs#Scenario 5: Valley of Barbed Tails|3.12]]*

- **Opening:** The valley narrows. Three hundred men turn around to hold it so the rest can get through.
- A rearguard's job is to delay, not to win. Every hour held is a few miles of distance for the wounded.
- The pursuers are tired too. They have been marching since Steel Springs.
- **Ending:** In Kalaman, Vendri is counting how many come home. *(On a loss, the city sees the losses.)*

### Scenario 6: Dark Moon Rises

*[[4.05 Dark Moon Rises#Scenario 6: Dark Moon Rises|4.05]]*

- **Opening:** Read the boxed text in 4.05. Visibility is a few feet, and ships can't see each other.
- Boarding is a land battle on a small deck. The attackers need grappling lines, and the defenders can cut them.
- Fire is the real threat. A burning ship takes everything on it down.
- Lunitari hangs low and red, and Solinari is almost gone. [A moon-touched PC] can feel the third moon overhead.
- **If a boarding party is destroyed:** a minotaur goes over the rail and swims back to its ship with the axe in its teeth.
- **If a fire spreads:** the supply ship's hold is next.
- **If a Kalaman ship is hit:** the ones nearby lose their formation in the fog, and the fleet scatters.
- **Ending:** The black ships turn away. They came to sink one ship and they did.

### Scenario 7: Break the Walls

*[[4.15 Break the Walls#Scenario 7: Break the Walls|4.15]]*

The party is slipping through Wind's End, so these are the battle as they hear and glimpse it.

- **Opening:** Darrett's horns, far off, then the crash of the first assault on the canyon wall.
- An assault on a wall uses ladders and ropes. The first wave carries the ladders, and it takes the worst of the fire.
- Dragonnels fly over, low, heading for the fighting. They haven't seen you.
- *(If the camp is starving.)* Darrett's soldiers are slow on the ladders. They haven't eaten properly since the wreck.
- **The turn:** A second horn, further up the canyon. More of the Dragon Army is coming.
- **Ending:** Whatever is happening at the wall, it's drawing every defender to it. Use it.

### Scenario 8: Fighting Retreat

*[[6.01 Fighting Retreat#Scenario 8: Fighting Retreat|6.01]]*

- **Opening:** About three hundred remain. A retreating army moves at the speed of its slowest wagon.
- A fighting retreat means one unit holds while the next falls back past it, then they swap. Done right, it costs few men. Done wrong, it's a rout.
- *(If Cudgel lived.)* Cudgel's mercenaries are the rearguard, as they were at Vogler.
- Something large and dead flies over, out of the citadel's shadow. Every unit in the column checks its formation.
- **Ending:** The coast, and the ships.

### Scenario 9: March of the Black Rose

*[[6.03 March of the Black Rose#Scenario 9: March of the Black Rose|6.03]]*

- **Opening:** The dead ride in silence. No drums, no horns, and no rest. They don't tire or need water.
- That's the advantage: our units run down over the day, and theirs don't.
- Behind the riders, the trade camps outside Kalaman's walls. The people in them are watching.
- At the head of the column rides a knight with Ispin's face.
- **If a skeletal knight falls:** the others close up the gap without a glance.
- **If a Kalaman unit breaks:** the dead don't pursue. They hold the road and keep marching.
- **If a knight PC strikes at the one with Ispin's face:** the whole column stops for a moment.
- Ispin's shield on [the knight PC]'s arm is warm.
- **Ending:** The black rose is down, or it isn't. Either way, the city has seen what's coming.

### Scenario 10: Day of a Thousand Fires

*[[6.04 Day of a Thousand Fires#Scenario 10: Day of a Thousand Fires|6.04]]*

- **Opening:** The enemy isn't trying to storm the walls tonight. They're setting the city on fire, and fire does more damage than the assault would.
- The defence is water. Bucket lines run up from the harbour, and the harbour is a long way from the fire.
- Ballistae on the towers fire at dragonnels. A hit panics the whole flight.
- A guard in Kalaman colours walks past you on the wall. You don't know his face. Nobody does.
- **If a wall unit is destroyed:** fire pots land in the gap, and the bell on the tower beside it rings faster.
- **If a bucket line is lost:** the fire reaches the next roof.
- **If a district burns:** the people run for the walls, and the soldiers have to decide whether to let them pass.
- **Ending:** Dawn is grey with smoke. Outside the walls, the army is forming up.

### Scenario 11: Siege of Kalaman

*[[6.05 Siege of Kalaman#Scenario 11: Siege of Kalaman|6.05]]*

- **Opening:** Six thousand of them, and the ladders go up all along the wall.
- Defenders on a wall have the advantage of height, but only where there are enough of them. A thin wall falls quickly.
- Every ally the party brought to this wall is on it: [the Siege Ledger allies]. This is what all of that was for.
- The ram hits the Trade Gate, and the whole gatehouse shudders.
- **If a wall unit is destroyed:** a ladder lands on the empty stretch, and the first man up is already shouting.
- **If the ram hits the gate:** every unit on the wall hears it. The portcullis groans.
- **If a Siege Ledger ally arrives:** name them, and say who they are.
- **Ending:** Clystran's dragonnels come in low over the harbour, and there's an empty saddle for each of you.

### Scenario 12: The Field of Kalaman

*[[6.07 Kalaman Victorious#Scenario 12: The Field of Kalaman|6.07]]*

- **Opening:** The gates open. Kalaman's army walks out for the first time since Steel Springs.
- The Dragon Army has lost its citadel and its commanders. The units left don't know who's giving orders.
- The militia goes out with the soldiers. Nobody tells them not to.
- **If an enemy unit breaks:** it runs for the hills, and its officers are the first ones running.
- **If a Kalaman unit is hit:** it should be easy by now, and it makes the survivors angrier than anything has been.
- **Ending:** Darrett comes back across the field carrying something out of the rubble. *(See 6.07.)*
