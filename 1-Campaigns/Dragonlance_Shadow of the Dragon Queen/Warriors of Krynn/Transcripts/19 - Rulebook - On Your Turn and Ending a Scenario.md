# Rulebook - On Your Turn and Ending a Scenario

## Rulebook p. 8, Stop, Stress, Wilderness, On Your Turn (event and action phases)

*Photo: [[09 - Rulebook p8 - Stop Stress Wilderness and Turn.jpg]]. The page number is cut off at the bottom-left of the photo. This page continues the Key Terms from page 7.*

#### Stop

Some adventure and battle tiles contain an obstacle represented by either a stop symbol printed on the tile itself or a stop token placed on the tile. If you move to a tile that has a stop symbol or token on it, you must stop moving until your next turn, unless an effect allows you to cover the symbol or remove the token.

A tile can't be both a stop tile and a wilderness tile. If an effect would add a stop token to a tile with a wilderness symbol or token on it, the stop token should cover the wilderness symbol or replace the wilderness token.

#### Stress

When a hero expends effort or something goes wrong, they take stress. For example, a hero might take stress due to a Woe result or leaving a tile with a vile champion on it. When a hero's stress changes, move their stress marker higher or lower by the specified amount. Once a hero has reached their maximum stress, they must remove 1 card from their hand per additional stress they would take.

**If a hero would take stress beyond their maximum and they have no cards in their hand, the scenario immediately ends in a loss.**

#### Wilderness

A tile with a wilderness symbol printed on it or a wilderness token on it is a wilderness tile. A tile can't be both a stop tile and a wilderness tile. If an effect would add a wilderness token to a tile with a stop symbol or token on it, the wilderness token should cover the stop symbol or replace the stop token on that tile.

### ON YOUR TURN

A player's turn progresses through 4 phases in order.

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Event phase** | Reveal and resolve event cards. |
| **Action phase** | Use your actions. |
| **Respite phase** | Reduce your stress or regain ability cards, if possible. |
| **Battle phase** | Resolve this turn's battle. |

### 1: EVENT PHASE

Reveal the top card of the event deck, and follow the instructions in the box labeled "Right Now" before doing anything else. Some event cards tell you to reveal another event card. When that happens, reveal the next event card and resolve its "Right Now" text. Continue until you reveal an event card with a box labeled "Battle Phase" at the bottom; this box will let you know the kind of battle that will occur in the battle phase. When you have resolved that card's "Right Now" text, the event phase ends.

#### Key Moments

Each scenario's event deck contains the Key Moment event card. When this card is revealed, advance the key moment tracker by 1, check the table in the scenario's "Key Moments" section to find the corresponding scenario entry, then look it up in the "Scenario Entries" section of this rulebook and follow its instructions.

### 2: ACTION PHASE

During each of your turns, you get 4 actions you can use during the action phase. You may spend actions on any combination of actions from among those below. You can take the same action more than once. Additionally, you can play cards with free action timing. Once you have taken all the actions you wish to take and played all the free action cards you wish to play, the action phase ends.

#### Activate a Class Power

You may spend 1 action and discard the required ability card to activate one of your hero's class powers if that power has action timing (see "Class Powers" on page 6).

#### Initiate a Conflict

Conflicts are challenges that involve risk, such as trying to defeat a vile champion or complete a dangerous scenario-specific task. You can initiate a conflict if your hero is on the same tile as a vile champion or on a tile specified by the scenario.

Each conflict specifies a numerical difficulty and an ability. A vile champion's difficulty and an associated ability are shown on its token, while the difficulty and any associated abilities of a scenario-specific conflict are noted in the scenario. A conflict's difficulty is reduced by 1 if the specified ability is one of your hero's mastered abilities. Initiating a conflict requires spending at least 1 action.

To succeed in a conflict, you must roll hits on the battle dice equal to or greater than the conflict's difficulty. You start with 1 battle die to roll during the conflict. You may spend any number of your remaining actions and discard any number of ability cards of the specified type to add that many dice to your dice pool, the total number of dice you roll during the conflict.

## Rulebook p. 9, Conflicts (end), Encounters, Moving Your Hero

*Photo: [[10 - Rulebook p9 - Encounters and Movement.jpg]]. *

*(Continued from "Initiate a Conflict", page 8.)* Once your dice pool is set, roll the dice and count the hits. If the hits equal or exceed the difficulty, you succeed. If you defeat a vile champion this way, remove it. If you roll more hits than the difficulty against a vile champion, you also draw 1 equipment card. If you roll fewer hits, you fail, but you may spend 1 or more actions to try again.

> **Example: Initiating a Conflict.** You are playing a Fighter, and you initiate a conflict against a Hill Giant, a vile champion whose difficulty is 3 and whose associated ability is Constitution.
> 1. You initiate the conflict by spending 1 action. You have 1 battle die.
> 2. Because your hero has Constitution as a mastered ability, the vile champion's difficulty is automatically reduced from 3 to 2.
> 3. You spend 1 additional action and discard a Constitution ability card to gain 2 more dice for your pool.
> 4. You roll a Single Hit, a Double Hit, and a Woe for a total of 3 hits (see "Battle Dice" on page 5).
> 5. You win the conflict, and the vile champion is removed. You draw 1 equipment card because you exceeded the difficulty. Because of the Woe result, your hero takes 1 stress.

### INITIATE AN ENCOUNTER

Encounters are the heroes' interactions with a scenario's locations, objectives, and characters. You can initiate an encounter if your hero is on the same tile as the thing you seek to encounter.

You may not initiate an encounter on a tile that has a vile champion on it. You may initiate more than 1 encounter during your turn, but you may not initiate the same encounter more than once during the same turn. There are three kinds of encounters (Activate a Tile's Power, Refresh a Shaken Commander, and Examine an Objective), each of which is described below.

#### Activate a Tile's Power

Each power specifies an action cost and an ability. This cost is automatically reduced by 1 if the specified ability is one of your hero's mastered abilities. You may discard any number of ability cards of the specified type to reduce the action cost by that amount. The action cost can't be reduced to less than 1 action.

> **Example: Activating a Tile's Power.** You are playing a Fighter, and you initiate an encounter to activate the Mine Entrance tile's power, whose action cost is 3 and whose associated ability is Constitution.
> 1. Because your hero has Constitution as a mastered ability, the action cost is automatically reduced from 3 to 2.
> 2. You discard a Constitution ability card to reduce the action cost from 2 to the minimum cost of 1 action.
> 3. You spend 1 action to activate the Mine Entrance tile's power.

#### Refresh a Shaken Commander

If you're on a tile with a shaken Alliance commander, you can Refresh that commander. The commander card's shaken side specifies an action cost to refresh the commander and an associated ability. This cost is automatically reduced by 1 if the specified ability is one of your hero's mastered abilities. You may discard any number of ability cards of the specified type to reduce the cost by that amount. The cost can't be reduced to less than 1 action.

#### Examine an Objective

You may spend 1 action to initiate an encounter with a scenario objective (represented by an objective token as specified in the scenario book). When you do, refer to the scenario to find out what happens next.

### MOVE YOUR HERO

Moving your hero to an adjacent tile costs 1 action.

#### Tiles Connected by Roads

Some tiles have roads, which can connect to roads on other tiles. Yellow pointers at the edges of tiles show where connections are available. When you spend an action to move your hero onto a tile with a road, you may move along the road to any tile along the road without spending additional actions. In the example below, you could move along the road from the far left adventure tile to the battle tile without spending more actions. The road ends there, so you'd have to spend another action to move to the adventure tile on the right.

*(Pictured: a row of tiles, Rocky Path, Trail, the Prairie battle tile and Woods. A green tick marks the road continuing to the battle tile, and a red cross marks the end of the road before the Woods tile.)*

#### Stop Tiles

If you move to a tile that has a stop symbol or token on it, you must stop moving, even if you're moving along a road. Unless an effect allows you to remove the stop token or cover the stop symbol, you can't move again until your next turn. However, you may still play a card or activate a power that places your hero on a different tile (see "Place a Hero" on page 7).

#### Vile Champions

If you move off a tile with 1 or more vile champions on it, even if traveling by road, you take 1 stress for each vile champion on that tile.

#### Enemy Territory

You can't move from the Dragon Army's side of a battle tile to an adjacent adventure tile; you must use another route.

## Rulebook p. 10, Free Actions to Fights between Lines (steps 1 and 2)

*Photo: [[11 - Rulebook p10 - Respite and Battle Phase.jpg]]. The page number is not visible in the photo.*

#### Play Free Action Cards

Ability, equipment, and honor cards with free action timing can be played as free actions. These cards must be played during the action phase of your turn, but they don't count toward your total of 4 actions.

#### Put Out a Fire

You may spend 1 action to remove a fire token from the tile your hero is on.

#### Transfer Equipment

On your turn, you may spend 1 action to either give an equipment card to another player or receive an equipment card from another player. In either case, the other player's hero must be on the same tile as your hero, and only the player whose turn it is spends an action.

### 3: RESPITE PHASE

During the respite phase, if your hero is on a tile with no Dragon Army units or vile champions on it, you may take respite: either reduce your hero's stress by 2 or return 2 ability cards from your discard pile to your hand.

If you still have actions left, you can take respite again for each remaining action. For example, if you have 1 action left, you can take respite a second time, allowing you to do one of the following during this phase: return 4 ability cards to your hand, reduce your stress by 4, or regain 2 ability cards and reduce your stress by 2.

### 4: BATTLE PHASE

Each turn ends with a battle on the active flank, either a skirmish (1 battle line from each side fights) or an all-out battle (all 3 pairs of lines fight, one after the other). The kind of battle is set by the last event card revealed during the event phase.

| Step | |
|---|---|
| **Battle starts** | Activate class powers or play cards. |
| | Activate commander powers. |
| | Resolve fights between lines. |
| | Update controlled battle tiles. |
| | End the turn. |

If the battle is a skirmish, roll the skirmish die before the start of battle. If you roll the Foot, Missile, or Mounted symbol, only that line fights. If you roll the Foot, Missile, or Mounted symbol plus the commander symbol on the same face, commander powers may also come into play (see "Activate Commander Powers" on page 10).

#### Battle Starts: Activate Class Powers or Play Cards

First, heroes on the active flank may activate class powers or play cards if those powers or cards have one of the following response timings: "At the start of battle," "At the start of a skirmish" (if the battle is a skirmish), or "At the start of an all-out battle" (if the battle is an all-out battle). Heroes can use as many eligible cards or powers as they want in any order.

#### Activate Commander Powers

Next, activate the commander powers of ready commanders on the active flank, depending on the type of battle:

**All-Out Battle.** Activate if instructed to by the last event card's "Battle Phase" text.

**Skirmish.** Activate if the skirmish die's result included the commander symbol.

If commanders on both sides are ready, activate the Dragon Army commander's powers first.

#### Resolve Fights between Lines

Perform the following steps for each line that's fighting. If the battle is an all-out battle, all 3 lines fight in the following order: Foot, Missile, then Mounted (from the inside of the battle tile to the outside).

##### 1: Determine Combat Strength

Determine the combat strength of the 2 lines that will fight. To do this, add the number of units (ready or shaken) on that line to the number of tactic tokens on that line (see page 4 for how tactic tokens work). The line with the greater total is the attacking line. If the lines' combat strength is equal, the Dragon Army's line is the attacking line. Once the combat strength of a line is determined, remove any tactic tokens on that line.

##### 2: Reveal a Unit Card

Reveal a unit card from the deck matching the attacking line's unit type. If that line's unit deck is empty, shuffle its discard pile to form a new unit deck first.

When the unit card is revealed, any hero on the active flank may play cards and activate powers with the response timing "When a unit card is revealed." Once any responses have been resolved, resolve the unit card and put it in the corresponding discard pile.

## Rulebook p. 11, Applying Hits, Controlled Tiles, Ending a Scenario

*Photo: [[12 - Rulebook p11 - Hits Control and Ending.jpg]]. A few words along the left edge are washed out by glare and reconstructed from context.*

##### 3: Roll Battle Dice and Apply Hits

Apply hits according to the unit card. A unit card states whether to apply a certain number of hits or to roll battle dice. The unit card also states which of the defending side's lines takes any hits that are rolled (for example, an Alliance Missile line might apply its hits to a Dragon Army Mounted line). Once hits have been applied to the defending line, any hits from Woe results are applied to the attacking line. If a unit card says hits may be applied to any line, the hero whose turn it is decides which defending line to apply them to.

Use these guidelines when hits are applied:

- Hits to a line are applied to its barricades, if any, first. After a barricade has absorbed 1 hit, it is removed. Any remaining hits are applied to that line's units.
- A ready unit that takes 1 hit is shaken.
- A shaken unit that takes 1 hit is removed.
- Heroes on the active flank may choose to absorb hits instead of units or barricades. For each hit they absorb, a hero takes 1 stress.
- Apply hits to a line until the hits have all been applied or no units remain on that line.
- If a unit card is targeting a line with no units, it doesn't apply hits to that line, but any other effects still happen.

#### Update Controlled Battle Tiles

**A tile becomes controlled by one side either when all the opposing side's units are removed from that tile or when all the opposing side's units on that tile are shaken.**

When a tile becomes controlled, remove all commanders from that tile. Then remove all shaken units from the defeated side. Finally, move that tile's flank token and 1 unit of each type from the victorious side to a neighboring battle tile (for example, the Center Flank token would move to either the right or left flank along with a Foot unit, a Missile unit, and a Mounted unit from the victorious side). Once control is established, war engines can't add units or tokens to the controlled tile, and any power that would affect that tile is ignored.

When the Dragon Army takes control of a battle tile, add a stop token to that tile. Then check the scenario book to find the scenario entry listed under "Dragon Army Control," locate that entry in the "Scenario Entries" section that starts on page 13, and read it aloud. The events of a scenario entry can cause the scenario to end in a loss.

As tiles become controlled, uncontrolled battle tiles may gain multiple flank tokens, so they're more likely to be the active flank on future turns. It's possible for the entire battle to focus on a single tile that holds all the flank tokens.

**If all the battle tiles become controlled and the scenario hasn't ended in a loss, the scenario immediately ends.** The outcome depends on which side controls more tiles.

| Controls More Tiles | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Alliance | Win |
| Dragon Army | Loss |
| Tie | Hold |

#### End the Turn

The turn ends once the battle has been resolved and any controlled battle tiles have been updated. If you revealed the Key Moment event card during the event phase, shuffle the event discard pile (including the Key Moment card) and add the pile to the bottom of the event deck. Play then passes to the player on the left.

### ENDING A SCENARIO

A scenario always ends when one of the following occurs:

- The final key moment is read.
- The Dragon Army takes control of too many battle tiles (set by the scenario's "Dragon Army Control" section).
- A player has no ability cards left when their hero's stress would increase beyond the maximum (automatic loss).
- All the battle tiles become controlled (outcome varies).

Each scenario also has one or more scenario-specific end conditions, such as completing the objective.

#### Aftermath

If the scenario didn't end in an automatic win, hold, or loss, check the scenario's "Aftermath" section to see how the heroes and the Alliance fared; each scenario has its own criteria. The heroes might earn honor cards depending on the outcome. Record the outcome and any honor cards earned on the Campaign Chronicle in the scenario book. Keep honor cards the heroes have earned separate from the other cards in the storage tray between scenarios.

**Win.** The heroes gain a reputation card set by the scenario and 1 medal card chosen from those available.

**Hold.** The heroes gain a reputation card set by the scenario.

**Loss.** At the beginning of the next scenario, the heroes will draw 2 equipment cards and place 2 tactic tokens.

#### Campaign Chronicle

After each scenario, update the Campaign Chronicle on the scenario book's back cover. At the start of each scenario, check this record to see what honor cards are in play at the start of the game, and whether the players should start with extra equipment and tactic tokens due to a previous loss.
