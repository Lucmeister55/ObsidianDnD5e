## Rulebook p. 3, Setting Up a Scenario and Battlefield Components (Tiles)

*Photo: [[04 - Rulebook p3 - Setting Up and Tiles.jpg]]. *

![[04 - Rulebook p3 - Setting Up and Tiles.jpg]]

### SETTING UP A SCENARIO

Take the following steps when setting up a scenario (these steps refer to components described in the next few pages):

1. Turn to the scenario's entry in the scenario book, then set up the battlefield tiles according to its diagram.
2. Shuffle the Recruit vile champion tokens face down to make a pool. For each vile champion location shown in the scenario's diagram, place a random vile champion token on that tile. Turn the vile champion tokens face up as you place them.
3. Set up the Alliance and Dragon Army commanders and units as shown in the scenario. Commanders and units may be either ready or shaken at the beginning of a scenario, and units sometimes start with barricade tokens underneath them.
4. Create the event deck using the event cards listed in the scenario, shuffle it, and place it near the battlefield face down. (Players may look at the event cards they are putting into the deck before shuffling it.)
5. Shuffle the equipment deck, and place it near the battlefield face down.
6. Sort the unit cards into 6 decks of each type (Foot, Missile, and Mounted for both the Alliance and the Dragon Army). Shuffle them individually and place them near the battlefield.
7. Place the key moment tracker near the battlefield with the key moment marker on 0.
8. Each player chooses a hero board and hero figure and takes a stress marker, then places their hero board in front of them and their hero figure on the Command Tent or Command Post tile. Each player begins the scenario with their stress marker on 0 on the stress tracker at the bottom of their hero board.
9. Each player chooses class cards (as appropriate for the scenario's tier), places the cards in front of them, and takes the ability cards listed on the back of the class cards. (Ability cards form each player's hand of cards, which should be placed face up to be visible to all players.)
10. Place any honor cards earned during the campaign face up near the battlefield.
11. If the heroes lost the previous scenario, you start play with the loss bonus: Draw 2 equipment cards and give them to any player or players. Then, as a group, decide where to place 2 tactic tokens on any Alliance lines.
12. Read the text in the scenario's "Starting the Scenario" section aloud, and learn the scenario's special rules. Choose a starting player, and the scenario begins.

### BATTLEFIELD COMPONENTS

Each scenario includes a list of the game components in play and a diagram of its battlefield. Other components may be put into play during the scenario.

If you run out of a type of token or marker, no more are available until some in play are removed. If the effect of a card or power can't be resolved in full, resolve it as much as possible. For example, if an effect says to apply 2 hits to a line that has only 1 unit, you would still apply 1 hit to that unit.

#### Tiles

##### Adventure Tiles

Adventure tiles represent areas where heroes might encounter boons, obstacles, and conflicts as they make their way around the battlefield. Only heroes, vile champions, and certain tokens may be placed on adventure tiles. Some adventure tiles have powers that heroes can activate (see "Initiate an Encounter" on page 9). When an effect causes an adventure tile to be flipped, transfer any tokens and figures on that tile to the new face-up side.

*(Pictured: the High Road adventure tile, and the Haunted Woods tile, whose text reads: "Reduce the stress of 1 hero on any tile to 0. They return all ability cards from their discard pile to their hand. Flip this tile." with a 3 CHA cost.)*

##### Battle Tiles

Battle tiles are regions that can become flanks of the battles between Alliance and Dragon Army units.

Each battle tile has 6 battle lines, 3 for each side, on which units are deployed. Each battle line has space for 6 units. The image below and the shapes on the battle tile note where to set up each type of unit on a battle tile.

*(Pictured: a battle tile, the Scrublands, with labelled rows. From the top: Dragon Army Mounted, Dragon Army Missile, Dragon Army Foot, then Alliance Foot, Alliance Missile, Alliance Mounted.)*

### MARKERS AND TRACKER

#### Unit Markers

The Alliance and Dragon Army each have 3 unit types: Foot, Missile, and Mounted. Unit markers are limited to their respective battle lines on battle tiles; they can't move from line to line. Unit markers stay on their tile unless moved by certain effects.

#### Key Moment Tracker and Marker

The key moment tracker and marker track important narrative moments in a scenario. Each time a key moment occurs, move the marker farther along the track and read the next scenario entry listed in the scenario book.

### TOKENS

#### Barricade Tokens

Barricade tokens provide defense to units, both by absorbing hits that would apply to a unit on a barricade and by preventing any unit on a barricade from being shaken by the effects of cards and powers. Each unit can have only 1 barricade, and barricades can't be placed on spaces without units. Once a barricade has absorbed a hit, it is removed. Any card or power that would directly Shake a unit on a barricade has no effect on the barricade or the unit (see "Ready/Shaken," page 7, for the effect of Shaking a unit). If a unit on a barricade is removed directly by a power, the barricade is also removed.

#### Fire Tokens

A fire token occupies 3 unit spaces on half of a battle line. When a fire token is placed on spaces with units, those units are removed. Units can't be added to any space with a fire token. If a fire token would be placed on an Alliance line, choose where to place it. If a fire token would be placed on a Dragon Army line, the token must be placed so that it removes the fewest Dragon Army units possible.

#### Flank Tokens

A flank token states the relative position of that battle flank in a given scenario: far left, left, center, right, and far right. When the "Active" side is up, the battle tile it's on is the active flank.

#### Objective Tokens

Objective tokens are used differently depending on the scenario. Each scenario tells you what they represent and how they will be used.

#### Stop Tokens

Stop tokens indicate a tile has become a stop tile during a scenario (see "Stop" on page 8).

#### Tactic Tokens

Tactic tokens are added next to battle lines to represent a tactical advantage. Each tactic token on a battle line increases the combat strength of that line by 1 for its next fight. Once the line has fought, its tactic tokens are removed. Tactic tokens can't be added to a battle line with no units. If all the units on a line are removed, any tactic tokens on that line are also removed.

#### Vile Champion Tokens

Vile champion tokens represent servants of the Dragon Army who oppose the Alliance heroes. Vile champion tokens are found only on adventure tiles.

*(Pictured: three tokens, a black "Vile Champion, Recruit" back, a green "Wyvern, 4 DEX" and an orange "Mercenaries, 2 CHA".)*

#### War Engine Tokens

War engine tokens represent fearsome Dragon Army weapons. Each appears on an adventure tile and is accompanied by a war engine card that explains its powers.

#### Wilderness Tokens

Wilderness tokens indicate that a tile has become a wilderness tile during a scenario (see "Wilderness" on page 8).

![[06 - Rulebook p5 - Cards and Dice.jpg]]

### CARDS

#### Commander Cards

In most scenarios, each side has a commander card assigned to each battle tile. A commander may be ready or shaken. Ready commanders have powers that activate before some battles. A commander's power affects only that commander's battle tile unless the power specifically says otherwise. The shaken side of an Alliance commander card shows the actions needed to Refresh that commander (see "Refresh a Shaken Commander" on page 9).

#### Equipment Cards

Equipment cards are single-use items. The timing for playing each piece of equipment is printed on the card. When a player uses an equipment card, it is removed. If all equipment cards are removed over the course of a scenario, the equipment deck remains out of play for the rest of that scenario. Heroes may transfer equipment to one another (see "2: Action Phase" on page 8).

#### Event Cards

Event cards are drawn at the start of each player's turn. They have an effect and then tell you about the battle that will occur that turn. For example, event cards can cause skirmishes, affect the number of units in a battle line, or move the battle to a different flank. Each scenario uses a particular set of event cards listed in the scenario book.

#### Honor Cards

Honor cards are earned by completing missions in scenarios. There are two types: reputations and medals. At the start of each scenario, all honor cards earned during previous scenarios are in play as a shared pool. Any player can use those honor cards as though they were ability cards (page 6) in that player's hand. Honor cards can be played only with correct timing. Once an honor card is used, it is removed until the beginning of the next scenario.

#### Unit Cards

The Alliance and the Dragon Army each have 3 unit card decks, 1 for each type of unit: Foot, Missile, and Mounted. Unit cards are revealed when a battle line becomes the attacking line during battle (see "Battle Phase" on page 10). They can affect the defending side's units and commanders, determining the number of battle dice rolled, the line that hits are applied to, and more.

#### War Engine Cards

War engine cards describe the powers of the Dragon Army's weapons, which are represented on the battlefield by war engine tokens. Keep a war engine card near its token for reference.

### DICE

#### Battle Dice

Battle dice are used to resolve battles and conflicts. Each battle die has one or more of each of these faces:

- **Single Hit.** 1 hit.
- **Double Hit.** 2 hits.
- **Woe.** 1 hit to a unit on the attacking line or 1 stress to a hero.
- **Blank.** No effect.

**During Battle.** For an attacking Alliance battle line, hits are applied to the Dragon Army, while each Woe result is a hit applied to the Alliance battle line.

For an attacking Dragon Army battle line, hits are applied to the Alliance, while each Woe result is a hit applied to the Dragon Army battle line.

**During a Conflict.** Hits a player rolls count toward meeting or exceeding that conflict's difficulty (see "Initiating a Conflict" on page 8), while each Woe result causes the hero to take 1 stress.

#### Skirmish Die

The skirmish die is used to determine which battle lines will be involved in a skirmish (and whether commander powers will activate before the fight begins). The skirmish die's faces use these symbols:

- **Foot.** The Foot lines fight.
- **Missile.** The Missile lines fight.
- **Mounted.** The Mounted lines fight.
- **Commander.** The commander symbol appears above a battle line symbol to indicate that ready commanders' powers activate before the skirmish.
