# DM Guide

> [!tip] At the table
> Campaign state lives on the props at the table: the calendar and the faction Standing sliders. If anyone asks how many troops are left, look it up in [[The War in Numbers]]. Session notes go in **Sessions**, made from the `Session Note` template. Chapters live in one folder each under **Chapters**, numbered in play order (`N.00` is the chapter overview, `N.99` holds reference rules), and every file links to the previous and next one.

## Reading These Notes

Every section in the chapter notes starts with a coloured bar showing where it comes from (the colours come from the `source-callouts` CSS snippet):

> [!module] **Module**: *Shadow of the Dragon Queen*, with chapter and section linked.

> [!companion] **Companion**: the DMs Guild companion PDF for that act, in `Documents/DMs Guild/companions`.

> [!wok] **WoK**: a *Warriors of Krynn* board game scenario, often with a custom outcome.

> [!homebrew] **Homebrew**: our own, with links to the notes it lives in.

**Priority.** When versions disagree, homebrew usually wins, then the companion, then the module. Sections that mix sources say which part is which.

**Padding.** The chapters carry enough of the module and the companions to run a session from the notes alone. Go to the sources for full read-alouds, maps and room-by-room detail.

## Chapters

**The prologue** is chapters 0 to 2: the pre-made origin characters in Vogler, told mostly from inside their heads. **The players start it blind**: no session zero, no handout, nothing about their characters or the world. They go straight into the dream, and get each character sheet at the moment that character enters the story. Everything they need to know, they learn in play. **It is planned in full**, down to every encounter, read-aloud and objective, so it can be run as written.

**Everything after it is a high-level plan.** Chapters 3 to 6 are mapped out so the threads have somewhere to go, but they are expected to change once the prologue has been played: which characters the players keep, swap or replace ([[Session 1.5]]), who lived and died in Vogler, which hooks the players bit on. Treat the later chapters as a strong draft, and revise them after the prologue, not before.

**Session 1.5** comes after the prologue: the players get [[Session 1.5]] (the campaign overview, the house rules and the world so far), and decide whether to keep, customise, swap or replace their characters. Then the campaign proper starts in Kalaman.

## House Rules

- **Variant encumbrance**, all campaign. It's tracked on the players' D&D Beyond sheets, where it's already switched on. It matters most in the Wastes ([[4.99 Survival Rules#Encumbrance]]). In Kalaman it barely matters: the military and the factions feed, clothe, arm and pay the party, and anything spare goes in the Bastion. Don't track food or carried weight in Storm Clouds.
- **No easy outs.** The party has no cleric, paladin, druid or artificer on purpose, so nothing trivialises food, water or carrying. Watch for:
  - ***Goodberry*** only heals in this campaign. It gives no nourishment.
  - **Bags of holding** and similar items don't exist before the Wastes. The first chance to craft one is back in Kalaman, after the trek ([[Harvesting and Crafting]]).
  - ***Create food and water*** comes only from Mages scrolls, and the ***decanter of endless water*** only from Mages at Honoured. They're meant to make the Wastes easier, as the reward for a lot of investment.
  - ***Rope trick*** (Davos might take it) lasts an hour, so it can't shelter a long rest.
- **Factions**: [[Faction Rules]].
- **Combat budget**: aim for **one full, played-out fight every 2–4 sessions**. Every encounter in the chapter notes is tagged as a **one-turn** candidate, **avoidable**, or a **full fight** with an objective. Offer one-turns to the table rather than imposing them ([[One-Turn Resolution]]).
- **Experiments**: [[Playtest Ideas]] (weapon triangle, combos, skirmishes, one-turns, last stands).
- **The player-facing version** of all of this is [[Session 1.5]].

## Why They Stay Together

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**, built on the **Module**'s council meeting, where Lord Bakaris claims the credit for Vogler ([[05-chapter-4-shadow-of-war#Encounter with Bakaris|ch. 4]]), and its esquire commission.

**In the prologue,** Vogler holds them together: the funeral, High Hill and the evacuation are one long emergency, and nobody walks out in the middle of one.

**In Kalaman, they're trapped together.** It isn't their fault.

**1. The charges.** Lord Bakaris was on the first boat out of Vogler, and he means to tell the council he led the evacuation. The party are the only people who could contradict him, so he gets to the castle first. By the time they reach the Trade Gate, soldiers are waiting to **arrest** them on three formal charges:
- **Desertion.** Becklin and the Ironclad Regiment held the pass, and most of them died there, while the party "ran with the boats and left the knights to it". (If Becklin survived, she's the best witness they have, once she's back on her feet.) It's a lie, but it's plausible, and he has witnesses who saw them go. In wartime, deserting a rearguard is a **hanging** offence.
- **Theft, and worse.** A dragonborn PC is under contract to the Bakaris family, and the party brought him to Kalaman. On paper that's theft, and on paper it's true.
- **Conspiracy against Vogler.** The worst one, and the one that ties the others together. A band of strangers turns up in Vogler the same week as the Dragon Army, one of them a creature that "looks just like the animals that burned the village". The shield is stolen, the militia is betrayed at High Hill, the army arrives, and the strangers walk away with the boats. Bakaris says they were the enemy's agents all along, and the dragonborn PC is the proof.

**Why the charges stick.** Nobody checks them. Raven is dead, so Bakaris, the only noble to come out of Vogler, is its de facto leader, and he speaks for it until the refugees choose a voice of their own. The guild leaders take a lord's word over a band of strangers' without a second thought. And the hall has seen the dragonborn PC. To a room full of frightened merchants, he looks exactly like the enemy, and it's easier to hang his friends than to think about it. The refugees know the truth, but nobody on the council asks fishermen. So the party goes into the cells under the castle, to wait for the hangman.

**2. The deal.** A few hours later, Vendri comes down to the cells: "Swear to me, as one company, and you're under military law. The council can't touch you, and Bakaris's charges come to my desk. I'll sit on them, for as long as you're useful." Once they're sworn in, they walk into the council meeting as her esquires, and Bakaris has to watch. She has two reasons of her own, and she tells them neither:
- **The dragonborn.** She's no believer. But soldiers' stories from High Hill have reached her, of a "draconian" who died and stood up again to fight *for* the villagers. The old stories of the gods begin like that. She has a feeling, no more, that what Bakaris is describing is the first sign of something coming back into the world, and she wants it close, where she can watch it.
- **The war.** She needs fighters she can trust, and a war is coming that her council won't believe in. She can only trust these because the law binds them to her. For now, that will have to do.

**The feel: a suicide squad.** Convicts who were never convicted: accused, sworn, armed and pointed at the enemy, with the noose held in reserve. They're the Marshal's to spend.

**Why they get so much authority, and the worst missions.** On paper, the company is perfect for Vendri. It answers only to her, so the council can't recall it. Its people are strange (a knight, a mage, a kender, an elf, a "draconian"), so they can go where no Kalaman soldier could. And it's **expendable**: if they die out there, the file says they were deserters anyway, and nobody on the council will ask questions. So they get a lieutenant's rank, real authority, and the jobs she can't send anyone else on. Let the players work that out for themselves, ideally around the time they're sent to Wheelwatch.

**3. The file.** The charges sit in **one file, against the company**. It's a real thing, a folder of statements in Vendri's desk.
- **Anyone who leaves** the company leaves military law, and the file reopens on them. The rest become their accomplices.
- **A dragonborn PC** is only safe as "in the company's custody", and Vendri's protection covers a member of her company. Alone, he's stolen property, and a draconian.

So each of them is held by all the others, whether they like it or not. Standing is shared for the same reason: to Kalaman, they're "the esquires".

**4. From coerced to chosen.**
- **At the Trial,** Bakaris drags the file out as proof that "they ran before" ([[4.01 The Trial#The Charges]]).
- **At Trusted with the Military**, or once the Bakaris contract is dealt with ([[3.08 Downtime 4]]), Vendri burns the file in front of them: "You were never deserters. I needed you." From then on they're free to go. The point is that they stay.
- **At the end,** the honourable discharge is a handout in the RPGPrintables pack.

**Every character needs the company for something of their own,** on top of the file.

| Character | What the company gives them |
|---|---|
| **A knight PC** | Rank, and a way up it ([[Ser Edric of Brackenridge (DM)#Arc: Rising Through the Ranks\|the ranks]]). |
| **A sorcerer PC** | Pay for the family in Relgoth, and a reason the Mages can't simply claim him. |
| **A kender PC** | Access: the castle, the archives, the sealed doors. |
| **A Silvanesti PC** | A voice for the refugees, and soldiers who will listen to a stranger in a war. |
| **A dragonborn PC** | His freedom, literally. |

**New characters** are sworn into the company: assigned by Vendri, or recruited on a mission (a freed prisoner, a refugee, a Maelgoth scout, a deserter from the other side). If the file still exists, Vendri adds them to it, because now they're accomplices too ([[#When a Character Dies]]).

## When a Character Dies

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**.

**The newcomer is sworn into the company**, so they have a reason to be there from the first scene ([[#Why They Stay Together]]).

Origin characters can die, and the chapters are written so that losing one doesn't mean rewriting anything. The player chooses: a **new origin character** made by the DM, or a **custom character** made together ([[Session 1.5]]). Either way, offer a [[Last Stands|Last Stand]] first.

**How the chapters handle it.** Anything that belongs to one character is marked with their name ("**Tie: Thorn.**", a level moment, a pairing, a hero's own unit). If that character is gone, skip the line, or give it to whoever picks the thread up. Nothing in the main plot needs a particular origin character. The module's plot always still works without them.

**The few threads that carry plot,** and where they go if their character dies:

| Thread | If the character is gone |
|---|---|
| **Ispin's shield** (Edric) | It passes to whoever Edric gives it to, as his Last Stand Bequest, or to Darrett. It still holds back the Cataclysm fire in the citadel, for whoever carries it. Soth still wants it destroyed. |
| **The leak to Takhisis** (Wurm) | It ends with him. If you still want the Wastes ambush, the spy is [[Jeyev Veldrews|Jeyev]] or Caradoc instead. |
| **The ring and the drone** (Milo) | The drone stays with the party and still leads them home to Onyari. The ring goes back to the forge in M8, or into Demelin's hands. |
| **The Test of High Sorcery** (Davos) | Optional anyway. Offer it to any other caster. |
| **Silvanesti and the Orb shard** (Thorn) | Zhelsuel and Dalamar run as the module has them. |
| **Kansaldi's "brother"** (Wurm, in the finale) | Her demand still stands: one of them must kneel and burn. She picks whoever she hates most. |

**A new origin character** should come with one tie to a thread the dead character leaves behind: a Greenshield cousin for the shield, another of Takhisis's experiments, a kender who knew Milo, an elf from Zhelsuel's camp. That way, the story keeps its shape.
