# DM Guide

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. Campaign-wide notes for running *Shadow of the Dragon Queen* our way.

## Reading These Notes

Every section in the chapter notes starts with a coloured bar showing where it comes from (the colours come from the `source-callouts` CSS snippet):

> [!module] **Module**: *Shadow of the Dragon Queen*, with chapter and section linked.

> [!companion] **Companion**: the DMs Guild companion PDF for that act, in `Documents/DMs Guild/companions`.

> [!wok] **WoK**: a *Warriors of Krynn* board game scenario, often with a custom outcome.

> [!homebrew] **Homebrew**: our own, with links to the notes it lives in.

**Priority.** When versions disagree, homebrew usually wins, then the companion, then the module. Sections that mix sources say which part is which.

**Padding.** The chapters carry enough of the module and the companions to run a session from the notes alone. Go to the sources for full read-alouds, maps and room-by-room detail.

## Chapters

**The prologue** is chapters 0 to 2: the pre-made origin characters in Vogler, told mostly from inside their heads. **The players start it blind**: no session zero, no handout, nothing about their characters or the world. They go straight into the dream, and get each character sheet at the moment that character enters the story. Everything they need to know, they learn in play. **It is planned in full**, down to every encounter, read-aloud and objective, so it can be run as written.

**Everything after it is a high-level plan.** Chapters 3 to 6 are mapped out so the threads have somewhere to go, but they are expected to change once the prologue has been played: which characters the players keep, swap or replace ([[Session 1.5]]), who lived and died in Vogler, which hooks the players bit on. Treat the later chapters as a strong draft, and revise them after the prologue, not before.

**Session 1.5** comes after the prologue: the players get [[Session 1.5]] (the campaign overview, the house rules and the world so far), and decide whether to keep, customise, swap or replace their characters. Then the campaign proper starts in Kalaman.

## House Rules

- **Variant encumbrance**, all campaign. It's tracked on the players' D&D Beyond sheets, where it's already switched on. It matters most in the Wastes ([[4. Duty - Survival#Encumbrance]]). In Kalaman it barely matters: the military and the factions feed, clothe, arm and pay the party, and anything spare goes in the Bastion. Don't track food or carried weight in Storm Clouds.
- **No easy outs.** The party has no cleric, paladin, druid or artificer on purpose, so nothing trivialises food, water or carrying. Watch for:
  - ***Goodberry*** only heals in this campaign. It gives no nourishment.
  - **Bags of holding** and similar items don't exist before the Wastes. The first chance to craft one is back in Kalaman, after the trek ([[Harvesting and Crafting]]).
  - ***Create food and water*** comes only from Mages scrolls, and the ***decanter of endless water*** only from Mages at Honoured. They're meant to make the Wastes easier, as the reward for a lot of investment.
  - ***Rope trick*** (Davos might take it) lasts an hour, so it can't shelter a long rest.
- **Factions**: [[Faction Rules]].
- **Combat budget**: aim for **one full, played-out fight every 2–4 sessions**. Every encounter in the chapter notes is tagged as a **one-turn** candidate, **avoidable**, or a **full fight** with an objective. Offer one-turns to the table rather than imposing them ([[One-Turn Resolution]]).
- **Experiments**: [[Playtest Ideas]] (weapon triangle, combos, skirmishes, one-turns, last stands).
- **The player-facing version** of all of this is [[Session 1.5]].

## Roles, Not Names

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**.

After the prologue, the chapters don't name the origin characters. They say **"a knight PC"**, **"a Silvanesti PC"** or **"a tinkerer PC"**: the trait that makes a thread fit. The name is arbitrary. Any PC with that trait can pick the thread up, and one character can carry several labels.

Nobody in Krynn knows the word "dragonborn". In the world, everyone calls such a character a draconian. The label is only for you.

If a character dies or is replaced, update the **Currently** column. Give each thread to whichever PC fits its label best, or drop it (see below).

## When a Character Dies

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**.

Origin characters can die, and the chapters are written so that losing one doesn't mean rewriting anything. The player chooses: a **new origin character** made by the DM, or a **custom character** made together ([[Session 1.5]]). Either way, offer a [[Last Stands|Last Stand]] first.

**How the chapters handle it.** Anything that belongs to one character is marked with their name ("**Tie: Thorn.**", a level moment, a pairing, a hero's own unit). If that character is gone, skip the line, or give it to whoever picks the thread up. Nothing in the main plot needs a particular origin character. The module's plot always still works without them.

**The few threads that carry plot,** and where they go if their character dies:

| Thread | If the character is gone |
|---|---|
| **Ispin's shield** (Edric) | It passes to whoever Edric gives it to, as his Last Stand Bequest, or to Darrett. It still holds back the Cataclysm fire in the citadel, for whoever carries it. Soth still wants it destroyed. |
| **The leak to Takhisis** (Wurm) | It ends with him. If you still want the Wastes ambush, the spy is [[Jeyev Veldrews|Jeyev]] or Caradoc instead. |
| **The ring and the drone** (Milo) | The drone stays with the party and still leads them home to Onyari. The ring goes back to the forge in M8, or into Demelin's hands. |
| **The Test of High Sorcery** (Davos) | Optional anyway. Offer it to any other caster. |
| **Silvanesti and the Orb shard** (Thorn) | Zhelsuel and Dalamar run as the module has them. |
| **Kansaldi's "brother"** (Wurm, in the finale) | Her demand still stands: one of them must kneel and burn. She picks whoever she hates most. |

**A new origin character** should come with one tie to a thread the dead character leaves behind: a Greenshield cousin for the shield, another of Takhisis's experiments, a kender who knew Milo, an elf from Zhelsuel's camp. That way, the story keeps its shape.
