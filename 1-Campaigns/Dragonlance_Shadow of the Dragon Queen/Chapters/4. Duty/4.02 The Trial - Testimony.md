## Testimony

Defaults: rewrite the lines around what happened in your Storm Clouds. Every witness either **lies for a reason** (cracks when the party finds the motive and asks the right question) or **makes an honest mistake** (often admits it once evidence clears it up).

**Charge 1: The Order sent the killers.**

*Witness: Sergeant Holt of the castle gate.*

*Why:* **an honest mistake, and his neck.** He let the "knights" in, so he needs them to have been real. Shown the remains, he goes grey and says he wondered why they never lifted their visors.

1. "Six knights came up the hill at noon, in the Order's armour, with the Crown on their shields. And a tall one at the back, hooded, who never said a word."
2. "They came straight from the chapterhouse. I saw them leave it." → **Point out with** Tatina**: she was at the gate, and they came up from the **harbour**, not the chapterhouse.
3. "I saw their faces. Solamnic men, every one." → ★ **Break it with** the wights' remains**: there were no faces under those visors.
4. "They said the Order's oath at the gate, so I let them in." → Press: they said it wrong. Holt didn't know the words himself.

*Witness: Lord Bakaris, speaking for himself.*

*Why:* **lying, with a motive.** While the city is angry at the Order, nobody asks about Steel Springs or his "missing" son. Press him on Steel Springs, and he loses his temper.

1. "The Order has wanted Kalaman back since before the Cataclysm."
2. "This 'Dread Knight' is a nursery tale the knights tell to frighten nobles." → ★ **Break it with** the catacomb records**, or with **Svilnt's boasting**: the Dread Knight is real, he was in the catacombs, and the Dragon Army calls him an ally.
3. "No knight of Solamnia has worn that armour in Kalaman for a century, except to make trouble." → **Point out with** Durstan Rial**: the armour came out of the crypts, on the dead.

**Charge 2: The esquires let it happen.**

*Witness: Mistress Oddny, a councillor's clerk who hid behind the tapestries.*

*Why:* **an honest mistake, and a small kindness.** She heard boots behind the knights and assumed. DC 15 Insight: someone has been kind to her. Pressed, she admits Bakaris paid for her husband's funeral.

1. "The esquires walked into the castle right behind the knights."
2. "They went straight up to the council chamber with them." → ★ **Break it with** the wights' remains**: those wights died at the **castle doors**. The party was fighting them outside.
3. "When it was over, they were the only ones standing."
4. "They didn't even look surprised." → Press: she was behind a tapestry. She didn't see their faces.

*Witness: Lord Bakaris.*

*Why:* **lying,** as before.

1. "These so-called heroes knew the Dragon Army's plans before anyone else."
2. "If they'd cared about Kalaman, they'd have warned us." → ★ **Break it with** the war council minutes** (they argued against Steel Springs) or **the Wheelwatch letters** (they did warn the city).
3. "Instead, they let my son ride to his death." → Press: Bakaris led that attack himself.

**Charge 3: The draconian is one of them** (only if the rumours spread).

Everyone calls him a draconian, his friends included. The charge is whether he's **the enemy's**.

*Witness: Teague, a dockworker.*

*Why:* **an honest mistake, and grief.** His brother died to draconians at Steel Springs, and he really did see the eyes burn. If the High Hill villager speaks, he listens, and may end up apologising.

1. "I've seen the lizard-men up close. Scales, claws, the lot. That one's the same."
2. "And at night, his eyes burn. Just like theirs." → **Point out with** the High Hill villager**: his eyes burned when he fought **for** Vogler. Draconians' eyes don't burn at all (passive Perception or any character who's fought them).
3. "He came out of nowhere, and nobody knows what he is."

*Witness: Lord Bakaris.*

*Why:* **lying, with a motive.** A draconian is property or a bounty. Anything else makes the contract a slave sale Kalaman might undo. He doesn't know what the dragonborn PC is either, but he knows how long he's owned him, and his seal is on the contract.

1. "The creature has been my family's property for years. I know what it is."
2. "It's one of the Dragon Army's draconians, the same as the rest, and it always has been." → ★ **Break it with** a petrified baaz**: the Dragon Army's draconians turn to stone, acid or fire when they die. A dragonborn PC **died** at High Hill, and got back up. Whatever he is, he isn't one of theirs. Or ★ **break it with the contract**: Bakaris has owned him for decades, since he was a hatchling. Draconians appeared out of nowhere a few years ago, with the Dragon Armies.
3. "It should be chained, or killed." → Press: because Bakaris would lose his property otherwise.

## The Prosecutor: Caradoc in Disguise

Caradoc can't be caught or killed here: in danger, he simply leaves the body. He wears the prosecution's **guild advocate**, and got the party accused in the first place. If Durstan Rial was never freed in Storm Clouds, Caradoc dropped him just before the Trial: Durstan is found alive in a cellar, with no memory of the last weeks, which is the first clue ([[3.05 Mission 2 - Ambushing the Enemy]]).

*Who he is:* **Advocate Orlen Vass**, a lawyer the guilds have used for years, middle-aged and well dressed. The real Vass has been Caradoc's host since the massacre. Never meets an eye for long, coldly persuasive.

*Why:* **lying, for his master.** Turning Kalaman against the Order and its champions weakens the city before the siege, and it amuses him.

*How he works.* He doesn't testify. He questions every accuser, tells the story the way they want it, and lets slip what only someone in the catacombs could know.

**The slips** (tally on a card), each one for the party to put to Thren:
1. He states a detail as fact that nobody in the hall has been told (the east door of the council chamber was open, the first councillor died with his sword in him).
2. He knows how the killers' armour sounded, or smelled, or that it was wet.
3. His water cup has frost on it, and the lamp nearest him gutters. A character who is a spellcaster feels the cold of the Shadowfell coming off him (**DC 18 Arcana**, no casting needed).
4. He objects to anything that would test whether he is who he says: a name, a childhood story, a question from someone who has known him. **Darrett**, who met Durstan in Vogler, can ask something only the real man would know.

**At three slips,** Thren orders him to give testimony under oath, as a witness. He can't, and the ★ lie falls: he is no advocate. At that point, or at any moment the party breaks the ★ lie in Charge 1, Caradoc leaves the body in front of the whole hall.

**When he's exposed,** he laughs and leaves the body, a cold shape drifting up through the rafters. Vass collapses with no memory of the last weeks, every charge he argued falls, and the hall understands what really walked into the castle.

**If he isn't exposed,** his case gets the party arrested ([[4.03 The Trial - Verdicts#If They Are Arrested]]).

## Defence Witnesses

Each faction at **Trusted or better** sends a trusted voice for a charge it believes in, backing an argument or filling a missing piece of evidence. At **Honoured**, their word alone can carry a charge. At **Hostile**, it sends a prosecution witness instead.

- **The Knights' charge:** the Knights, the Military (Vendri knows better) and the Mages (Wyhan can read Soth's fire). The Guilds testify against it unless they're Trusted or better.
- **The party's charge:** any faction at Trusted or better.
- **A dragonborn PC's charge:** the Military (Vendri's protection) and the Knights, if a knight PC speaks for him. The Smallfolk testify against it unless they're Trusted or better, and then Vogler's people stand up to say what they saw at High Hill.
