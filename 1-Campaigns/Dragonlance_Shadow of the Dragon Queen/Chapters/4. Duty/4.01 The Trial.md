## The Trial

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. It's an expansion of the **Module**'s debriefing ([[06-chapter-5-the-northern-wastes#Group Debriefing|ch. 5, "Group Debriefing"]]), where Lord Bakaris is already blaming the party and the city wants someone to blame.

The council is dead. Knights in Solamnic armour walked into Castle Kalaman, and an hour later the governor and five guild leaders were dead on the floor. The city wants someone to blame, and Lord Bakaris is happy to give it names. The party was found alone among the dead and is held under guard in the castle ballroom, under Vendri's custody ([[3.13 The Raided Catacombs#Held, Not Hanged]]). Two days after the catacombs, an **inquiry** is held in the great hall: what's left of the council, the guilds' new leaders, Marshal Vendri, and as many of the smallfolk as can push in at the back.

It's the last thing that happens before the Wastes, and its verdicts follow the party all the way to the siege.

### The Charges

Run up to three. Bakaris brings each one himself, loudly. After Steel Springs he's finished in Kalaman unless someone else takes the blame for something, and a massacre in the council chamber is the best chance he'll ever get. The guilds let him, because it isn't them in the dock.

1. **The Order sent the killers.** The "knights" at the door wore Solamnic armour. The Knights of Solamnia have always wanted Kalaman back. *(Against the Knights.)*
2. **The esquires let it happen.** They were in the castle, they're the Order's friends, they came back alive, and the council didn't. *(Against the party.)* Bakaris brings the **Vogler file** as proof that "they ran before". If Vendri has already burned it, she says so, in front of the whole hall.
3. **The draconian is one of them.** Use this one only if the rumours about a dragonborn PC have spread ([[3.00 Storm Clouds]], "Running Threads"). *(Against the dragonborn PC.)* He's one of the esquires, so if this charge stands, the whole company is accused of harbouring him.

### How the Trial Plays

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**: a courtroom framework of testimony, cross-examination and evidence.

The inquiry is chaired by **Fuline Thren**, the oldest surviving councillor (the module's future governor). She's fair, frightened, and running out of patience. The **accusers** are Lord Bakaris and the guilds' new leaders, but they don't argue the case themselves. The prosecution is led by a polished **advocate** nobody remembers hiring, who is really **Caradoc** wearing a guild lawyer's body ([[4.02 The Trial - Testimony#The Prosecutor: Caradoc in Disguise]]). The party **represents itself**: they are their own defence counsel, and any of them can take a charge, rotating as they like.

Each charge runs the same way:
1. **Testimony.** A witness gives their statement, a few lines. Read it out one line at a time, or hand the players the lines on cards.
2. **Cross-examination.** The players work through it:
   - **Ask** about a line: a good question gets more detail, and often a hint at the lie. An Insight check is fine if they're stuck.
   - **Point out** what doesn't fit: present a piece of evidence, call a witness, or say something a character knows for certain.
3. **Closing.** If the charge is still undecided, one character closes against the advocate (see "The Closing" below).

**No magic settles the truth.** The gods are silent, so there are no clerics and no *zone of truth*, *detect thoughts* or *detect evil and good* in the hall, and nobody in Kalaman trusts a spell. The only caster in the hall is **Davos** (Wyhan sits as a witness, and reads, but never casts). If Davos casts anything noticeable in the hall (DC 15 Perception for Thren's guards and the gallery, DC 18 to hide a subtle spell), it is **tampering**, and **tampering that is noticed is an immediate failure**:
- **The charge on the table stands at once.** No closing, no ★ break.
- The Mages take **-1**, and the advocate seizes on it: "Sorcery in the hall of justice."
- If it happens on the esquires' charge, or happens twice, the prosecution paints Davos as **one of the culprits** and that charge stands as well.

Davos can still help without magic: with Insight, with what he learned at the card tables, with his seat on the council.

**What decides it.** There's no score. A charge falls when the party has **broken the lie at its heart**, marked ★ in each testimony, or has shown the hall enough smaller cracks that nobody believes it any more. Faction witnesses (below) make it easier: they're voices the hall already trusts. Use your judgement, and let a clever argument win even if it isn't the one written here.

**Three clocks.** Getting it wrong has a cost, and the table can see it. Keep them as props on the table (paper clocks, a dial), not in the vault.
- **Thren's patience** (6 segments). Fill one for every accusation that falls flat, every insult and every shout. (Tampering with magic that's noticed doesn't fill it. It loses the charge outright.) When it's full, Thren stops the hearing: the charge stands, and if it's the party's, they're **arrested** (below).
- **The gallery** (a dial: *Hostile, Wary, Torn, Warm, Behind you*). It starts at **Torn**. A good question or a well-timed witness moves it one step towards the party, a clumsy accusation or a slip left alone moves it away. At **Warm or better**, Thren's patience doesn't advance on the first miss in each charge. At **Hostile**, every charge starts with one patience segment already filled. Hidden marks move the starting position.
- **The slips** (3 marks, below). Each time the advocate lets something slip and the party catches it, mark one.

**The court record.** The evidence table below is a **court record**. Print each item as a card and lay them in front of the players. To **point something out**, a player **presents a card**: slides it across to Thren and says what it shows. A card can be presented once per charge (unless it breaks the ★ lie, which it can always do), and the advocate can challenge it. A card the party never got isn't on the table, and a hidden mark can take one away.

> [!tip] Run it as a scene, not a procedure
> The hall is packed and loud. Let the crowd groan, cheer and heckle, and let Bakaris play to the gallery. Run the charges in order, the Knights first, so the party warms up. Break between charges for a recess in the corridor, where Vendri, Darrett or a nervous witness can catch them. Save the advocate for last. When Caradoc is exposed, that's the climax: stop, describe the hall going silent, and let it land. Keep the clocks and the cards on the table, and nothing else.

**Hidden marks.** Go through your card of [[Faction Rules#Hidden Consequences|hidden consequences]] from Storm Clouds. A refugee Spokesperson who was snubbed won't speak, a story Bakaris was allowed to keep makes the hall hostile from the start, and a favour someone owes may bring an unexpected witness. Don't announce them. Let the players feel the room turn.

### The Closing

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**, borrowing from the idea of Burning Wheel's *Duel of Wits*: both sides commit to a move in secret, then reveal together.

Use this when a charge is still undecided after the testimony. The advocate and the character taking the charge close against each other in up to **three exchanges**, each with a secret move on a card:
- **Evidence** (point to the record). Beats Procedure.
- **Plea** (to the gallery's heart). Beats Evidence.
- **Procedure** (to Thren's authority and the rules of the hall). Beats Plea.

Reveal together. The winner of an exchange moves the gallery **one step** their way, and **two** if they won with a card on the record (Evidence) or a faction witness (Plea). A tie changes nothing. **Win two exchanges** to carry the closing. A broken ★ lie wins the first exchange automatically. The advocate plays to the gallery; Caradoc knows what the hall wants to hear and plays Plea more often than he should.

**What they can bring.** Before the hearing, just remind the players what they came out of Storm Clouds with. They can only bring what they actually have, so the choices they made there matter.
