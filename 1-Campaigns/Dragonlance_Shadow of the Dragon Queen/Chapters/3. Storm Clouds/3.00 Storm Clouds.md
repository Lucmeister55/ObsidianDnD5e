## Summary

> [!homebrew] **The campaign proper starts here.** Chapters 0 to 2 were the prologue, planned in full. From here on it is a **high-level plan**, to be revised once the prologue has been played: characters may have been customised, swapped or replaced, and Vogler may have gone differently ([[DM Guide#Chapters]]). That is why the chapters say "a knight PC" or "a tinkerer PC" instead of a name: each label is the kind of character a thread suits best ([[DM Guide#Roles, Not Names]]).

This chapter handles everything between the players' flight from Vogler to Kalaman, culminating in their confrontation with Lord Soth in the catacombs (until then known only as the Dread Knight, a whispered legend of a shadow cursing Dargaard; his name is rediscovered in the catacombs, see [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge (DM)]]), and learning that they must travel to the Northern Wastes to foil Takhisis' plans. For the most part, the [[3-Shadow_of_War.pdf]] companion guide may be followed, with deviations being mentioned here in this note.

This act will chronologically be the longest, giving the players enough time to gain plenty of reputation with a number of competing factions in the city, and to learn various hints and clues about the world and the looming threat of the Dragon Queen. Between every major story beat and mission, copious amounts of downtime will be provided, allowing for taking bastion turns, crafting, research, sucking up to factions, and so forth. These periods will vary from weeks to months.

> [!info] No bookkeeping in Kalaman
> Food, water and carried weight don't need tracking in Storm Clouds. The party is fed, clothed, armed and paid by the military and the factions, and stashes everything spare in the Bastion. Supply only starts to matter when they pack for the Wastes.

## Running Order



> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. The major events come from the **Module** ([[05-chapter-4-shadow-of-war|ch. 4]]) and **WoK**, as tagged in [[3.00 Storm Clouds]].

The running order for [[3.00 Storm Clouds]]: every major event, every downtime, and the events that move faction Standing in between. Factions run on [[Faction Rules]]. Most of the combat stays in the major events. The downtime events are almost all social choices, and that's where Standing really moves.

**How to run a downtime.** Each downtime, every character takes their personal downtime activity, a bastion turn, crafting or whatever else they like, once per week. Alongside that, run **two or three of the events listed for that downtime**, whichever fit the table best. Each event is a short scene with a choice, and each choice moves Standing. Events you don't use can roll over into the next downtime.

**Briefings.** Before every downtime and every major event, the party is called to a war room or council meeting: news from the front, the consequences of their choices, and a strategic dilemma. Each one sits at the top of the file where it happens, numbered in the order they come. How they work is in [[#How a Briefing Works]].

**Starting Standing:** 0 for every faction, except the Vogler Refugees, who carry over whatever the party earned in Vogler.

## Running Threads

**Keep the downtimes varied.** At most **one** event per downtime about Bakaris or a dragonborn PC (the war council in Downtime 5 is the exception). The rest should be the city itself, and should vary in kind: a dispute to settle, a dilemma inside the company, a beast or the dead, a favour for a faction. Not everything is about food either.

Three threads run through the whole act, on top of the downtime events. Bring each one back once or twice.

**Bakaris the Younger.** The guilds are the only reason the Bakarises have a voice in Kalaman. They admire old noble blood, and the Elder's money and Solamnic name buy him a seat beside them on the council. So anything that humiliates the Bakarises costs a little with the Guilds. The Younger is a thorn in the party's side all act, and how badly depends on the party's **Guilds** Standing:
- **Guilds Trusted or better:** he can't touch them openly. He sulks, spreads rumours and waits.
- **Guilds Neutral:** he works the merchants against them. Once per downtime, one purchase costs double, or one guild favour is "unavailable".
- **Guilds Wary or worse:** he gets them **boycotted**. The guilds' Hostile effects kick in a band early, he spreads stories that the party are Dragon Army sympathisers, and he tries to buy a dragonborn PC's contract out from under any deal they're making.

**A dragonborn PC and the city.** Rumours that the black-scaled creature is a draconian spy grow all act (see [[Wurm (DM)]]). At first the dragonborn PC is protected. [[Nestra Vendri|Marshal Vendri]] makes it known that he serves under her esquires, and that anyone who lays a hand on him answers to her. That protection holds **as long as Military Standing is Neutral or better**. If the party slips to Wary with the Military, Vendri quietly withdraws her protection, and soldiers stop looking the other way. If the party slips to Wary with the **Smallfolk**, the docks turn on him regardless. Either one turns the rumours violent (see "the dragonborn PC in the alley" in Downtime 3).

**The Dragon Army's knives.** The Dragon Army knows exactly who has been wrecking its plans, partly because Takhisis hears everything a dragonborn PC hears. By the middle of the act it sends **sivak draconians** after the party. Sivaks take the shape of whoever they kill, which makes them perfect assassins (see "The friendly face" in Downtime 4). Their eyes inside the castle are [[Jeyev Veldrews|Jeyev]], Becklin's murderer, now Darrett's trusted right hand. Drop one wrong note about him per downtime (see his note), and have him make his own attempt on Darrett just before the Lord's Arrival.

## How a Briefing Works

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. The news items are tagged where they come from the **Module** or **Companion**.

Before every downtime and before every major event in [[3.00 Storm Clouds]], the party is called to a meeting. It's how the players see the war move: what the Dragon Army has taken, what's happening abroad, what their own choices did to the world, and what they could do next. And most of the time, someone asks them what Kalaman should do.

There are two kinds:
- **The war room.** Vendri, Darrett, maybe Cudgel or Tatina, around the big map table in Castle Kalaman. Military news, plain talk, and a decision about the next move. Short.
- **The council.** Governor Miat, the five guild leaders, Lord Bakaris, Vendri, and sometimes the refugees' Spokesperson, in the great council chamber. Politics, money, blame, and a vote. Longer, and louder.

Every briefing starts with **the summons**, how the party is called in: a page at midnight, a bell, a herald, or Darrett at the door. Vary it, and let it set the mood before anyone speaks. Then come the same four beats:
1. **The map.** Move the pieces. Put a big hand-drawn map of the region on the table ([[map-4.01-kalaman-player.png|Map 4.1]] and the region map from the module), and let the NPCs move tokens on it as they talk: a red marker on Vogler, another on Wheelwatch. The players see the war get closer.
2. **The news.** Three or four items, read out by whoever would know. Keep each one to a sentence.
3. **Your doing.** One item that's the direct result of something the party did, good or bad. Always include one. It's how they learn that the world remembers.
4. **The question.** A dilemma with two or three options, put to the players. Let them argue, then decide. Each option moves Standing ([[Faction Rules]]). The NPCs push for what they'd want, loudly.

**The Mages' seat.** Once a spellcaster PC is the Order's observer ([[3.01 Arrival in Kalaman#Message for a Mage]]), he has a chair at every council, apart from the company. Once per council, he can put the Mages' position. Make it one of the options on the question, and let how he argues it move **Mages** Standing (+1 if he serves the Order's interest, -1 if he ignores Wyhan's instructions). Wyhan always sends instructions. He doesn't always follow them.

**Keep them quick.** Fifteen minutes at the table, at most. The players should leave with a choice made and the next move clear.

**"Your doing" ideas** to drop in wherever they fit:
- The refugees they housed inside the walls are working the docks. Or the ones left outside are sick.
- The Shipwrights' new invention (if Tatina went to them) is already on the guild ships, not the walls.
- Bakaris the Younger has been telling the council the party are Dragon Army spies, and someone believes it.
- The farm they saved has sent a cart of food to the castle, with a letter.
- A village they didn't reach in time is gone.
