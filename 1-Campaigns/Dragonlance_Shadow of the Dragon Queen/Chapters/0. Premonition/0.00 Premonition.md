## Overview

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. It replaces the **Module**'s [[03-chapter-2-prelude-to-war|Chapter 2: Prelude to War]]. The characters' ties to Ispin Greenshield and the sorcery trials happen off-screen, in their backstories.

This is the first thing the players hear: a nightmare that hasn't happened yet. The dreamer is [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge|Edric]], and the dream is the **Invasion of Vogler**, a few days in the future ([[2.00 Broken Silence]]), as it might go. Nobody at the table should know any of that yet. Don't name him, the village or the enemy.

It has one job: show the players the end of the road before they've taken the first step. Vogler burns, the knight's companions die (the other player characters, though nobody knows that yet), and whatever he chooses, the boy in the rubble dies too. Then something comes out of the sky that was supposed to be gone from the world. It's a promise of what's coming, and that it can't be stopped by one man alone.

> [!tip] Running it
> - Read the first box in the dark, or with eyes closed. Keep it slow.
> - After the first box, let the players decide who claims this character. That player makes the choice in the second box.
> - **Every choice ends the same way.** Don't soften it. The players should come away knowing this campaign doesn't care how brave you are.
> - **Write down which choice Edric's player makes.** He lives this exact moment again in Vogler, for real ("Déjà Vu" in [[2.00 Broken Silence]]), and there, choosing differently can change it.
> - Then go straight into [[1.00 An Ordinary Day]], where the same knight rides west on a bright spring morning, and let the players work out whose dream it was.

## The Dream

**Music:** [Battle In City](ttrpg-sound:Soundbar/Ambience/Battle In City.mp3), looped for the whole dream, with three volume cues:
- **Start it low** before you read, like fighting heard through stone.
- **Fade it to almost nothing** as he gives up ("The best anyone could do was let go"). Let the last lines sit in near silence.
- **Bring it back at full volume, instantly,** on "Then he gasped awake." The screaming should hit the table like it hits him.

> Darkness. No sound, no room to move, nothing but a still, close dark that felt very like mercy. He thought he could sleep here. Mother would come and wake him soon. She always did.
>
> But Mother was dead. He remembered the smell of the smoke, and her arms around him, and how still she had gone. And the old man was dead too, the one who had pulled him out of those ashes and trusted him, and gone away down the road without him. What was one man against all that? He had always known it. The best anyone could do was let go.
>
> He would stay here, a little while longer.
>
> Then he gasped awake.
>
> Ash filled his mouth, and he coughed it out in black strings. His ears rang. A beam lay across his chest, and he got his hands under it and heaved, and the stones shifted, and he was out. He stood, and doubled over, and was sick in the rubble. Smoke ran up the street like an undulating river. Above it, on the cliffs against the night, stood the black shell of a roofless keep with fire in its windows, and along the top of its wall a long timber jutted out, snapped off at the elbow.
>
> The ringing faded. Somewhere a whistle blew, one thin note, and stopped. Then the screaming came back into focus.
>
> His companions had been with him when the searing blast of shrapnel hit. He found them one by one. A severed finger lay beside a fallen beam, a ring still on it. A long grey sleeve, burnt through. A pale braid lying in the ash. A shape too broad for any man, dark, half under the stones. He turned over what he could lift. Their faces were whole and peaceful, their limbs were wrong, and none of them moved. He did not look for the rest.
>
> He sat down among them in the ash. Each of them had come to him running from something, and not one had done anything to deserve this. They were troubled souls, all of them, and as innocent as children, and he had not been able to keep them safe. It would be easy to lie down beside them, the way he had lain under the stones, a little while longer, and let the rest happen without him.
>
> "Not this time," he said, to nobody, and his voice cracked on it.
>
> He picked up his sword.

*Prompt the players to decide who will claim this origin character.*

> You stumble down the burning street. Soot has blackened the wall beside you, all but one patch where the paint held: two red claws, locked together. Something is coming up the street through the smoke. You hear it before you see it: claws on the cobbles, a great many feet, and under them a dry clicking, like hail on a roof. Then the first shapes, running low, with the firelight on plates of red and black. They make no cry. One lifts its head, and the rest turn with it, and every one of them is looking at you.
>
> To your left, under a fallen roof beam, a young man is pinned in the wreck of a doorway. You know him. He is barely more than a boy, with a militia armband gone black with soot and a fisherman's knife in his belt, a blue feather bound round the hilt. He reaches out a hand toward you.
>
> "Please..."
>
> The creatures are coming. What do you do?
>
> 1. Heave the beam off him before they reach you.
> 2. Drag him out from under it, whatever it does to him.
> 3. Stand between him and them, and buy him time to crawl free.

*Read only the ending they chose.*

> 1. The beam lifts. It's enough. He slides free and falls into your arms, alive and gasping, and for one heartbeat you hold him. Then something drops from the broken roof above, all scales and claws, and drives into him before you can raise your sword. His eyes go wide. He doesn't make a sound.
> 2. You drag him out by the arms while he screams, and haul him up, and run. He's heavy and you're failing, and every step is slower than the last. They catch you at the end of the street. You hear the blades go into him, again and again, and his voice breaks, and stops.
> 3. Steel meets claw, and for a moment you hold them all. Behind you, you can hear him dragging himself out of the doorway. Then the wall above him cracks. You don't even have time to turn. When the dust settles, there is nothing moving behind you.

> You kill them. Every one of them that comes. Then the strength runs out of you all at once, and you go down on your knees in the ash, in front of the place where he was.
>
> You look up at the sky, at the gods who stopped answering three hundred and fifty years ago.

**Music:** Omen (1-02 - Omen / Main Theme). Start it on "Then something passes over them", and fade the battle ambience out underneath it.

> The stars are still there, cold and far away. Then something passes over them, and they go out. Something vast is coming down out of the dark, slow and certain, on wings as wide as the street is long. Somewhere close by, a man is crying the same word over and over, a word out of the old stories, a word for a thing that was supposed to be gone from the world.
>
> "*Dragon.*"
>
> And the sky catches fire.
