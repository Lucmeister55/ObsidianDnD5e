## Overview

This act loosely follows the plot of the first half of [[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns|Chapter 3: When Home Burns]]. In this act, all the characters will slowly enter the story and be allocated to a player. The relatively quiet and positive narrative will culminate with the battle at High Hill, where the final character will be revealed. This storyline will mostly adopt the adaptions made in the companion guide: [[2-When_Home_Burns.pdf]].

> 
> It is the year 351 AC, three hundred and fifty-one years since the Cataclysm, and the 13th day of Brookgreen. It was a hard winter. You spent it in the yards at Kalaman, drilling in sleet with the wind coming in off the bay, until your hands split and the frost got into your mail. That is behind you now. Spring has come to Hinterlund all at once. The brooks run loud and full, the birches are putting out their first green, and there are lambs in the fields by the river. The sun is warm on your back for the first time in months. You wear a knight's spurs, a few weeks old and still bright. On a morning like this it is easy to believe the world is mending.
> 
> You ride west with two nobles in your charge. Becklin Uth Viharin rides on their right: a Knight of the Crown like you, older, longer in the saddle, and the one who sets the pace. You crossed the Vingaard by ferry early this morning. Since then the road has left the river flats and climbed into the forest, narrowing until it is little more than a cart track between the trees.
> 
> Her hair has gone silver, and she wears it in a plain braid down her back, though a few strands always work loose by noon. Her face is lined and weathered, quick to smile and slow to frown. She rides in full plate, old but well kept, worked all over with Solamnic knotwork, and her horned helm hangs from her saddle beside a round shield that has been dented and beaten straight more times than you could count. For ten years she has been castellan of Thornwall Keep above Vogler. Ispin used to say she had retired without telling anyone, least of all herself.
> 
> Vogler lies at the end of this road, on the western edge of Hinterlund, where the track gives out and the river takes over. You have never been there. But Ispin Greenshield talked about it often, and so did Becklin, the few times your paths crossed. Both spoke of it the way people speak of home. North of here, the woods thin into brook and scrub, and beyond them lie the Northern Wastes. South, across the river, lies Nightlund, a land of hard storms and a bad name, though few can tell you any more how it earned it. Its capital is Kalaman, the Beacon of the East, down where the Vingaard meets the sea. Its walls run out into the harbour, and at either end stands a tower with a beacon that the old Istarians lit before the Cataclysm. Sailors say its light will find you through any fog.
> 
> The nobles in your charge are the Bakarises, father and son, Vogler men going home for the Kingfisher Festival. Between them they have made the road twice as long as it is.
> 
> The elder Bakaris is a heavy man gone soft in the saddle, red in the face and wrapped in furs too warm for the season, with a ring on every finger that will take one. He has complained of everything since the ferry: the mud, the cold, the pace, the ferryman's manners, the birches. You had his measure inside the first mile.
> 
> His son is another matter. Bakaris the Younger is lean and dark and handsome, and knows it. His riding coat is better cut than anything you own, and a fine ebony crossbow rides across his back, oiled and unscuffed. He talks less than his father and smiles more, and of the two, you have come to like the smiling least.
> 
> Behind the son's horse walks his servant, a great stooped creature, scaled like a lizard, carrying a load no horse was asked to bear. Even this near Kalaman, where the law is meant to count for something, there are serfs. The elder Bakaris calls it Wurm, and calls it a good beast, the only one of the company that can keep up. The creature has not said a word all morning. You have not once seen it lift its eyes from the road. Despite the noble's demeaning comments, you can't help but feel sorry for it, stumbling through the mud on its bare feet as it was.
> 
> Not long after the ferry, Becklin suggested a slower pace. The north is close, she said, and it is no place to draw attention. That was all the Bakarises needed. They have been at her ever since: about restraint, about discipline, about what all the Knights' fine oaths did to stop the Cataclysm. She answered them as patiently as she answers everything. The Knights were never charged with stopping it, she said, only with holding what could still be held. Crown for loyalty, Sword for courage, Rose for wisdom. She named herself a Knight of the Crown, and you beside her.
> 
> At last, as you knew they would, they came round to Dargaard, the black castle in the mountains of Nightlund, and the shadow that is said to hang over it still, the curse of some ancient evil no one can name. The old whispers call it the Dread Knight. Every noble keeps that tale ready for a knight's company, because the story goes that the evil was once one of the Order's own. For the first time that day, Becklin's jaw set hard. "You know the story," she told them. "Not the truth."

## Arrival in Vogler

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Arriving in Vogler|ch. 3, "Arriving in Vogler"]]), with **Homebrew** character introductions (see the origin characters' notes).

As soon as the entourage unmounts in the village square, have Becklin steer Edric towards the Fishbowl tavern to meet up with Darrett. Cudgel can also be found here, getting drunk. Eventually Becklin carries her back to her camp, making her disappear until she's needed later. 

Seated at Darrett's table is Davos, who was trying to butter up Darrett after overhearing that he was a knight. He arrived at the tavern that morning, and was waiting for his ferry to Kalaman (since all the ferries had been delayed due to the festival, unbeknowst to him). Since he has no choice to wait, he figured he might as well already try getting into the good graces of the knights before his arrival in Kalaman. For the last hour, he has been playing cards with Darrett, whilst using his magic to cheat (but not too much). For this reason, he tags along with the party during the later festival, keeping up a facade of cheerfulness and engagement.

### Running Order

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns|ch. 3]]) and **Companion** ([[2-When_Home_Burns.pdf]], Parts 3–5), in the order this campaign uses them.

1. **Day 1, afternoon.** Arrival, the Fishbowl, and the companion's **missing child** search with Darrett (Ross, who "saw a dragon in the woods"). This is where the wounded dragonnel crashes in (see "The Missing Child and the Stolen Shield" below).
2. **Day 1, dusk.** **Ispin's funeral** at the Brass Crab's dock, then the reception: tales of Ispin, the **heckler** (Bakaris the Younger), **Cudgel**, the **mysterious mourner** (Leedara), and Milo caught with the shield.
3. **Day 2, morning.** Becklin's invitation to **Thornwall Keep**: breakfast, Darrett, and Ispin's parting gift (module, "Parting Gift").
4. **Day 2, midday.** The **Kingfisher Festival**, at most 4 activities.
5. **Day 2, afternoon.** The walk to **High Hill**, the reenactment, and the betrayal.

### Vogler at a Glance

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Vogler Gazetteer|ch. 3, "Vogler Gazetteer"]], map 3.1) and **Companion** (Appendix B, "Location Reference Sheet"; Part 3, "Vogler Details").

Vogler is a fishing village of about **four hundred** people at the foot of a pre-Cataclysm bridge that stops halfway across the Vingaard. It's under the distant protection of Maelgoth. Fish carvings are everywhere, the festival honours Habbakuk (the "Fisher King", mostly forgotten), and the villagers are proud of their hospitality but share Solamnia's distrust of the Knights: mention the Order and the room goes quiet.

### The Missing Child and the Stolen Shield

> [!companion] Source: **Companion** ([[2-When_Home_Burns.pdf]], p. 6 onwards), with the **Homebrew** changes below (see [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge (DM)]] for the Red Ruin and the shield).

From here, you can mostly follow [[2-When_Home_Burns.pdf]] verbatim from page 6 onwards. A few minor things to change include:

- Once the party finds the missing child, it blubbers out that there is a dragon chasing him overhead, with a terrified look in its eyes. Upon saying that, a dragonnel crashes through the boughs of the trees. It gets up, looking dazed, wounded and feral. Make the dragonnel immediately flee if the characters take hostile action. If the party searches the area around the dragonnel, they find a clasp of what could have been a belt of some kind. If they tell anyone from the village, they say it's probably just a dragonnel from the Northern Wastes (false).
- Ispin's shield was on his body when the villagers found him, but was later taken from his casket in secret by Gragonis. Furthermore, he clearly died by some sort of bestial strike, though Becklin believes they were too long and targeted to have been some random wild beast from the wastes. In fact, the true culprit was the Red Ruin's dragonnel mount.

### The Funeral and Reception

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Final Farewell|ch. 3, "Final Farewell"]]), with **Companion** and **Homebrew** additions where marked.

- **Sending off a friend.** At dusk, on the stage and dock behind the Brass Crab, Ispin's body goes out on a funeral boat, with locals in small boats on the water around it. (*Homebrew:* Soth's servants take the boat downstream, see [[Soth's Host]].)
- **Tales remembered.** Yalme keeps bread, cheese and ale coming. Everyone in the village turns up, and people share stories of Ispin. Edric's player should start with one ([[Ispin Greenshield#Stories to Tell About Him]]). This is a **+1 villagers** moment.
- **The heckler.** After a story or two, Bakaris the Younger yawns loudly and calls out that they've "heard this a thousand times". Confronting him works with roleplay or DC 12 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion). It shouldn't come to blows. It establishes him as a cad.
- **Cudgel** introduces herself as Ispin's old friend and the leader of the Ironclad Regiment. She's already deep in a bottle and abrasive, and apologises for it later (companion).
- **The mysterious mourner.** Leedara, a blue-skinned Silvanesti elf, keeps to the edges and calls herself a seer. Anyone with passive Perception 14+ notices her watching them. She talks long enough to leave an impression (a cryptic warning, a word about fate), then is gone behind a passing villager. She's a ghost bound to Soth's story (module ch. 4).
- **After the funeral,** Becklin invites the party to Thornwall Keep in the morning: Ispin left something for them.

### Parting Gift

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Parting Gift|ch. 3, "Parting Gift"]]), with **Homebrew** changes.

On the way to the keep, the party meets Darrett, earnest, clever and a little starstruck. At Thornwall Keep, Becklin gives them breakfast and tells them what Ispin left them: **his shield**. But the shield is missing. It lay on his casket at the funeral, where Milo tried to "borrow" it, and sometime in the night it vanished. Gragonis took it, and the party gets it back from him at High Hill (homebrew). Over breakfast, Becklin shares what she suspects about Ispin's death.

**Becklin's suspicions** (companion, "Investigating Ispin's Murder"): Ispin was found far off his usual patrol route, his sword was still sheathed, and the wound was too long and too precise for a beast. If the party goes to the spot, they find large clawed prints that start and stop out of nowhere (DC 14 Survival), and a ragged gap in the branches overhead (DC 12 Perception): something came down from the sky. It was Red Ruin's dragonnel (homebrew).

## The Battle of High Hill

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Betrayal at High Hill|ch. 3, "Betrayal at High Hill"]]). Wurm's death and rebirth and Thorn's entrance are **Homebrew** (see [[Wurm (DM)]] and [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn) (DM)]]).

### Setting Up the Betrayal

> [!companion] Source: **Companion** (Part 5, "Setting Up the Betrayal" and "The Battle of High Hill") and **Module** ("Behind the Battle", "Betrayal").

- **The walk out.** Raven leads twenty Vogler reenactors and twice as many spectators to the hill, with Becklin and Darrett. She points out the wagon of padded spears. DC 12 Insight: she's taking it seriously. She's heard rumours of war in the east and hopes this doubles as a drill.
- **No real weapons.** Cudgel and Raven make everyone leave real weapons at the bottom of the hill. The mercenaries comply, but Gragonis hid real spears under the brush there the night before. **The characters fight with padded spears, improvised weapons or whatever they take from a mercenary.** That makes Edric's shield-less, sword-less first fight a lot more desperate.
- **The signal.** Raven, Becklin and Cudgel leave for a better vantage point. Raven blows the whistle to start.
- **The turn.** Anyone with passive Perception 15 sees the mercenaries' spear tips glint, unpadded, and it's already too late: a villager the party has come to like (Nesau, Froswin) is run through. Gragonis pulls the cloth off the shield on his arm. It's **Ispin's shield**. He orders the slaughter.
- **Taking command.** DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation) gets the panicking villagers to at least defend themselves.
- **Darrett** is cut off by two mercenaries early. He breaks through later, if the party is close to going down, and takes the Dodge action to cover them.
- **Cudgel betrayed.** Meanwhile, a mercenary named **Svilnt** tries to kill Cudgel, and Becklin stops him. He's taken alive (see [[2. Broken Silence]]).

### The Battle

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**, on the **Module**'s map 3.2.

Make it so that it is described that there are about 50 villagers and mercenaries each, with two main hills on which the villagers start. Darrett is described as starting on the other hill and will thus stay away from the party's fray, unless needed to swing the battle in their favor if the party start struggling. Right before Raven blows the whistle, Edric notices a ragged figure stumble out of the brush some distance behind the mercenaries. The two forces start charging and clash, but upon impact, Wurm is immediately felled, and effectively dies.

**Milo loses their head.** Early in the fight (round 1 or 2), a mercenary's axe takes **Milo's head clean off**. Describe it plainly and let the table go quiet. On Milo's next turn, the head **grows back**: skin knitting over bone, the grin coming back first, while Milo blinks and asks what they missed. They're back at full hit points. It's the ring ([[Milo Geartickle (DM)]]). A death costs a death: as the head grows back, a **kingfisher** drops dead out of the tree above them, the festival's own bird. Nobody connects the two. Log it in the death tracker. This is for shock, so don't explain it, and let the other players stare.

Also roll initiative for Thorn, and have him enter from the fray on the edge of the map. On his turn, give his character sheet to his respective player.

At the end of the first round of combat, Wurm has a vision of a vast grey dragon, like silver seen through smoke (a hint that it isn't truly Paladine), and is brought back to life with full hit points. Upon opening his eyes, they burn with a divine light, and the last thing he hears is "kill them all". Upon hearing this, he tears apart the mercenary bearing down upon his prone body. Give them their full character sheet at this moment, as well as make them roll initiative.

## Aftermath

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Aftermath|ch. 3, "Aftermath" and "Response"]]) and **Companion** (Part 5, "The Spy").

- **The rest scatter.** When Gragonis falls, the remaining mercenaries run. Raven, Becklin and Cudgel arrive. Ask the players to describe the killing blow on Gragonis, and whether they leave him alive. Edric gets **Ispin's shield** back here ([[Ser Edric of Brackenridge (DM)]]).
- **Raven** thanks the party and promises a reward, but she's busy with the wounded.
- **Cudgel** is livid: one of her own tried to kill her. She wants answers, and she won't get them on the hill.
- **The wounded.** Most of Vogler's militia died. A DC 12 Wisdom (Medicine) check brings a villager back from the brink. Each one saved is a face the party will see again in Kalaman.
- **Back to Vogler.** Raven and Becklin ask the party to escort the survivors home. The festival is over.

No level-up here: the origin characters start at 3rd level. This is the moment the last of them, Wurm and Thorn, have joined.
