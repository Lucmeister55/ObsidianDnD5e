## Overview

The party follows the Dragon Army into the Northern Wastes, the Cataclysm's open wound, to learn what Lord Soth, Lohezet and Belephaion want with the City of Lost Names. The fleet is ambushed in the fog on the crossing, the supply ship goes down, and the army limps into Wrecker's Edge with a few days' food. Darrett stays to mend the ships. The party goes on alone, with only what they carried. From there it's a race: the army starves while they hunt for the city, and every location below is a danger, a clue and a chance to eat and drink for another week.

## Introduction

### The Trial

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. It's an expansion of the **Module**'s debriefing ([[06-chapter-5-the-northern-wastes#Group Debriefing|ch. 5, "Group Debriefing"]]), where Lord Bakaris is already blaming the party and the city wants someone to blame.

The council is dead. Knights in Solamnic armour walked into Castle Kalaman, and an hour later the governor and five guild leaders were dead on the floor. The city wants someone to blame, and Lord Bakaris is happy to give it names. Two days after the catacombs, an **inquiry** is held in the great hall: what's left of the council, the guilds' new leaders, Marshal Vendri, and as many of the smallfolk as can push in at the back.

It's the last thing that happens before the Wastes, and its verdicts follow the party all the way to the siege.

#### The Charges

Run up to three. Bakaris brings each one himself, loudly.

1. **The Order sent the killers.** The "knights" at the door wore Solamnic armour. The Knights of Solamnia have always wanted Kalaman back. *(Against the Knights.)*
2. **The esquires let it happen.** They were in the castle, they're the Order's friends, they came back alive, and the council didn't. *(Against the party.)*
3. **The draconian is one of them.** Use this one only if the rumours about a dragonborn PC have spread ([[3. Storm Clouds - Timeline]], "Running Threads"). *(Against the dragonborn PC.)*

#### How the Trial Plays

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**: a courtroom framework of testimony, cross-examination and evidence.

The inquiry is chaired by **Fuline Thren**, the oldest surviving councillor (the module's future governor). She's fair, frightened, and running out of patience. Bakaris is the prosecutor. The party defends itself.

Each charge runs the same way:
1. **Testimony.** A witness gives their statement, a few lines. Read it out one line at a time, or hand the players the lines on cards.
2. **Cross-examination.** The players work through it:
   - **Ask** about a line: a good question gets more detail, and often a hint at the lie. An Insight check is fine if they're stuck.
   - **Point out** what doesn't fit: present a piece of evidence, call a witness, or say something a character knows for certain.
3. **Closing.** One character sums up for the hall.

**What decides it.** There's no score. A charge falls when the party has **broken the lie at its heart**, marked ★ in each testimony, or has shown the hall enough smaller cracks that nobody believes it any more. Faction witnesses (below) make it easier: they're voices the hall already trusts. Use your judgement, and let a clever argument win even if it isn't the one written here.

**Thren's patience.** Getting it wrong has a cost. Every accusation that falls flat, every insult and every shout makes the hall less willing to listen. Too many, and Thren stops the hearing: the charge stands, and if it's the party's, they're **arrested** (below).

> [!tip] Run it as a scene, not a procedure
> The hall is packed and loud. Let the crowd groan, cheer and heckle, and let Bakaris play to the gallery. Run the charges in order, the Knights first, so the party warms up. Break between charges for a recess in the corridor, where Vendri, Darrett or a nervous witness can catch them. Save Caradoc for last. When he's exposed, that's the climax: stop, describe the hall going silent, and let it land. Don't track anything you don't have to.

**What they can bring.** Before the hearing, just remind the players what they came out of Storm Clouds with. They can only bring what they actually have, so the choices they made there matter.

| Evidence | What it shows | How they got it |
|---|---|---|
| **The wights' remains** | Under the visors, dead flesh, still smouldering violet. A spellcaster PC or Wyhan can name the fire: the Cataclysm's, the same as the farmstead dead. | Kept from the fight at the castle doors. |
| **Durstan Rial** (as a witness) | A real knight, possessed by Caradoc. He remembers the armour being handed out in a crypt, not a chapterhouse. | Freed from Caradoc in Storm Clouds. |
| **The catacomb records** | Soth's name and history, in the Order's own hand. | The catacombs. |
| **The Wheelwatch letters** | The Dragon Army's plans, including the trap at Steel Springs. | Wheelwatch. |

| **The war council minutes** | Who argued against the Steel Springs attack. | Briefing 10, if the party spoke against it. |
| **A petrified baaz** (or a report of one) | What happens to a draconian when it dies. | Any fight with baaz. |

#### Testimony

These are the defaults. Rewrite the lines around whatever actually happened in your Storm Clouds.

**Nobody is just wrong.** Every witness either **lies for a reason** or **makes an honest mistake**, and working out which is half the game. A liar's testimony cracks when the party finds the motive and asks the right question. An honest witness can be won over: once evidence or another witness clears up the mistake, they often admit it themselves.

**Charge 1: The Order sent the killers.**

*Witness: Sergeant Holt of the castle gate.*

*Why:* **an honest mistake, and his neck.** Holt let the "knights" in. If they were monsters, it's his fault, so he needs them to have been real knights. He believes every word, and he's terrified. Shown the remains, he goes grey, and says quietly that he wondered why they never lifted their visors.

1. "Six knights came up the hill at noon, in the Order's armour, with the Crown on their shields."
2. "They came straight from the chapterhouse. I saw them leave it." → **Point out with** Tatina**: she was at the gate, and they came up from the **harbour**, not the chapterhouse.
3. "I saw their faces. Solamnic men, every one." → ★ **Break it with** the wights' remains**: there were no faces under those visors.
4. "They said the Order's oath at the gate, so I let them in." → Press: they said it wrong. Holt didn't know the words himself.

*Witness: Lord Bakaris, speaking for himself.*

*Why:* **lying, with a motive.** Bakaris led the disaster at Steel Springs, and his son is "missing". As long as the city is angry at the Order, nobody asks about that. And he's hated the Knights for every snub since Vogler. Press him on Steel Springs, and he loses his temper.

1. "The Order has wanted Kalaman back since before the Cataclysm."
2. "This 'Dread Knight' is a nursery tale the knights tell to frighten nobles." → ★ **Break it with** the catacomb records**, or with **Svilnt's boasting**: the Dread Knight is real, he was in the catacombs, and the Dragon Army calls him an ally.
3. "No knight of Solamnia has worn that armour in Kalaman for a century, except to make trouble." → **Point out with** Durstan Rial**: the armour came out of the crypts, on the dead.

**Charge 2: The esquires let it happen.**

*Witness: Mistress Oddny, a councillor's clerk who hid behind the tapestries.*

*Why:* **an honest mistake, and a small kindness.** She heard boots right behind the knights, and assumed. From behind the tapestry, she couldn't see a thing. DC 15 Insight: someone has been kind to her since the massacre. Press her, and she admits Lord Bakaris paid for her husband's funeral.

1. "The esquires walked into the castle right behind the knights."
2. "They went straight up to the council chamber with them." → ★ **Break it with** the wights' remains**: those wights died at the **castle doors**. The party was fighting them outside.
3. "When it was over, they were the only ones standing."
4. "They didn't even look surprised." → Press: she was behind a tapestry. She didn't see their faces.

*Witness: Lord Bakaris.*

*Why:* **lying, with a motive.** Same as before: blame the party for Kalaman's dead, and nobody blames him for Steel Springs.

1. "These so-called heroes knew the Dragon Army's plans before anyone else."
2. "If they'd cared about Kalaman, they'd have warned us." → ★ **Break it with** the war council minutes** (they argued against Steel Springs) or **the Wheelwatch letters** (they did warn the city).
3. "Instead, they let my son ride to his death." → Press: Bakaris led that attack himself.

**Charge 3: The draconian is one of them** (only if the rumours spread).

Nobody in Kalaman has a word for what a dragonborn PC is, so everyone calls him a draconian, his friends included. The charge isn't really what he is. It's whether he's **the enemy's**.

*Witness: Teague, a dockworker.*

*Why:* **an honest mistake, and grief.** His brother died at Steel Springs, cut down by draconians. He really did see a dragonborn PC's eyes burn, and he isn't lying. He just doesn't know what it means. If the High Hill villager speaks, Teague listens, and by the end he might be the one who apologises.

1. "I've seen the lizard-men up close. Scales, claws, the lot. That one's the same."
2. "And at night, his eyes burn. Just like theirs." → **Point out with** the High Hill villager**: his eyes burned when he fought **for** Vogler. Draconians' eyes don't burn at all (passive Perception or any character who's fought them).
3. "He came out of nowhere, and nobody knows what he is."

*Witness: Lord Bakaris.*

*Why:* **lying, with a motive.** If a dragonborn PC is a draconian, he's a monster, and Bakaris keeps his property or gets paid for its head. If he's anything else, the contract was a slave sale, and Kalaman might set him free. Nobody in the hall knows *what* the dragonborn PC is, Bakaris included. But Bakaris knows how long he's owned him, and his own seal is on the contract.

1. "The creature has been my family's property for years. I know what it is."
2. "It's one of the Dragon Army's draconians, the same as the rest, and it always has been." → ★ **Break it with** a petrified baaz**: the Dragon Army's draconians turn to stone, acid or fire when they die. A dragonborn PC **died** at High Hill, and got back up. Whatever he is, he isn't one of theirs. Or ★ **break it with the contract**: Bakaris has owned him for decades, since he was a hatchling. Draconians appeared out of nowhere a few years ago, with the Dragon Armies.
3. "It should be chained, or killed." → Press: because Bakaris would lose his property otherwise.

#### The Hidden Witness: Caradoc

Caradoc, Soth's seneschal, can't be caught or killed here: he's a ghost bound to Soth, and when he's in danger he simply leaves the body he's wearing. But he can **testify**, wearing someone else, and he's the one who got the party accused in the first place.

- **If Durstan Rial was never freed** in Storm Clouds, Caradoc is still wearing him. "Ser Durstan" is the prosecution's star witness: a real knight of the Order who survived the massacre and swears he saw the esquires **open the castle doors** to the killers. He's the reason the party is in the dock at all.
- **If Durstan was freed,** Caradoc takes someone else for the day: a guard, a clerk, a juror. Pick whoever would hurt most to lose.

*Why:* **lying, for his master.** Turning Kalaman against the Order and its champions weakens the city before the siege, and it amuses him.

*Witness: "Ser Durstan Rial".*

1. "I was in the hall when the gates were opened. I saw the esquires at the doors."
2. "They let the killers in. I watched them do it." → Press: ask him something only the real Durstan would know. **Darrett** met him once, in Vogler, years ago, and Caradoc has been faking that memory all along (Storm Clouds).
3. "I would know a true knight anywhere. Those were not the Order's." → **Point out**, with the **wights' remains**,: he's right that they weren't knights, which he could only know if he knew what they were.
4. "Search my memory, if you like. I have nothing to hide." → ★ **Break it** with anything that exposes the possession: Darrett's question, a *detect evil and good* scroll from Wyhan, or a spellcaster PC feeling the cold of the Shadowfell coming off him (DC 18 Arcana).

**When he's exposed,** Caradoc doesn't fight. He laughs, and leaves the body in front of the whole hall: a cold shape that drifts up through the rafters and is gone. Durstan, or whoever he wore, collapses, with no memory of the last weeks. Every charge he testified to falls with him, and it's the moment the hall understands what really walked into the castle.

**If he isn't exposed,** his testimony is what gets the party arrested ([[#If They Are Arrested]]).

#### Defence Witnesses

Each faction at **Trusted or better** sends someone to speak for a charge it believes in: a voice the hall already trusts, which can back up the party's argument or fill in a piece of evidence they're missing. At **Honoured**, their word alone can carry a charge. Each faction at **Hostile** sends a witness for the prosecution instead, with one more testimony to break.

- **The Knights' charge:** the Knights, the Military (Vendri knows better) and the Mages (Wyhan can read Soth's fire). The Guilds testify against it unless they're Trusted or better.
- **The party's charge:** any faction at Trusted or better.
- **A dragonborn PC's charge:** the Military (Vendri's protection), the Vogler Refugees (they saw him at High Hill) and the Knights, if a knight PC speaks for him. The Smallfolk testify against it unless they're Trusted or better.

#### The Verdicts

| Charge | If it fails (they're cleared) | If it stands |
|---|---|---|
| **The Knights** | The false-knights warning ends. **+1 Knights.** The chapterhouse may guard the castle in the siege. | The warning stays, and the chapterhouse is watched. A knight PC's next promotion is frozen. In the siege, the Knights are kept off the walls until the Trade Gate is about to fall. |
| **The party** | They leave for the Wastes as the city's champions: **+1 Smallfolk**, and Bakaris loses his seat on the new council. | They leave for the Wastes half in exile, which is the module's reason anyway: "get out of the city for a while". **-1 Smallfolk**, and Bakaris keeps his seat. |
| **A dragonborn PC** | Vendri's protection becomes official: he's under Kalaman's law, not the Bakarises'. | He's ordered out of the city. He goes to the Wastes anyway, but he isn't welcome back without proof, and the Smallfolk turn on him in the siege. |

#### If They Are Arrested

If the party's charge goes **badly** (Thren's patience runs out, nothing they say lands, or someone draws steel or lays a hand on Bakaris in the hall), the council doesn't just doubt them. It **arrests them on suspicion**, and they spend the night in the cells under Castle Kalaman while Bakaris talks about a hanging.

They don't stay there. The faction they stand best with gets them out before dawn and smuggles them aboard the fleet bound for the Wastes. Pick the highest Standing, and on a tie, whoever makes the better scene:

| Who frees them | How |
|---|---|
| **Kalaman Military** | Vendri can't overrule the council in public, but a guard shift changes, a door is left unlocked, and Darrett is waiting at the docks with their packs. |
| **Knights of Solamnia** | Two knights walk in, "transfer the prisoners to the Order's custody", and walk out with them. Nobody dares to stop them. |
| **Mages of High Sorcery** | Wyhan visits the cells with a basket of bread. The guards see the party asleep in their cells until noon. |
| **Kalaman Guilds** | A purse changes hands. The party leaves in barrels, on a cart, through the Trade Gate. |
| **Kalaman Smallfolk** | The lower city's tunnels (the Cistern House, if that's their Bastion) come up under the cells. |
| **Vogler Refugees** | A fishing boat under the castle's sea wall at midnight, and a rope. |

**The fallback.** If no faction will help, the **befuddled old man** does ([[Wurm (DM)]]). He's the one who always knows they have to go north. He's in the next cell, nobody knows how, complaining about the food. In the morning the cell door is open, the guards are asleep, and he's gone, leaving a hat that isn't his and directions to the right ship. Don't name him.

**What it costs them:**
- They leave with only what they're wearing and what the rescuers bring. The **Wastes dividends don't come**, except from the faction that freed them, which pays in full.
- They're **fugitives** in Kalaman. When they come back for the siege, the grim report starts with guards at the door. The verdict can be overturned there with new evidence from the Wastes, or by the simple fact that they're the only ones who know what's coming.
- Whoever freed them takes a risk for it: **+1** with that faction, and **-1** with its rival when the escape is discovered.

#### Every Road Leads to the Wastes

Whatever the verdicts, the inquiry ends the same way it does in the module: Vendri and Darrett make the case, and the party goes north with Darrett's army. What the Trial decides is **how** they go: as champions, as an awkward necessity, or as exiles. That decides what the city puts in their packs.

| How it went | How they leave | What it changes for **Leaving Kalaman** |
|---|---|---|
| **The party cleared** | Champions. The council votes the expedition its full support, and the docks are crowded to see them off. | Every faction at Neutral or better pays its Wastes dividend in full. The Smallfolk add their gift even at Neutral. |
| **Arrested** | Smuggled aboard at night ([[#If They Are Arrested]]). | Only the faction that freed them pays. |
| **The party's charge stood** | Half exiled. Vendri sends them "to get them out of the city for a while", and the council won't spend a copper. | The Guilds' final dividend is **halved** (Bakaris has the purse). The Smallfolk give nothing. The Military and Mages still pay what their Standing earns. |
| **The Knights cleared** | The chapterhouse stands with them in public. | A knight or two sails with Darrett, and the Knights' ([[Knights of Solamnia]]) mission perk applies to the whole Wastes trek. |
| **The Knights' charge stood** | The Order keeps its head down. | No knights on the ships, and a knight PC sails as a suspect's friend. |
| **A dragonborn PC ordered out** | He boards in chains, by order, until Darrett has them struck off once they're out of sight of the city. | Nothing for the packs, but say it at the table. |

The dividends themselves are in [[Faction Rules#Dividends]] and each faction's note.

**While they're in the Wastes,** Tatina's fargab carries news of the verdicts' fallout: riots at the chapterhouse, or knights cheered in the streets; Bakaris on the new council, or Bakaris drunk and alone. Use it in the evenings at Darrett's camp.

**When they return** ([[6. Conquest#Return to Kalaman]]), the verdicts are where the city's trust starts. If a charge stood, new evidence from the Wastes (Lohezet's papers, the flying citadel itself) can still overturn it at the grim report.

### The Deadline

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. The module's Wastes have no timer, and this one exists to keep the party from waiting out the ship repairs.

Soth let the date slip in the catacombs. Between the Trial and the ships, a spellcaster PC, Wyhan or the Mages can pin it down.

The Dragon Army needs more than the Cataclysm's fire to raise the dead dragons under Onyari. It needs the right night: the **Night of the Eye**, when all three moons stand full together, once every 504 days. The next one is **14 Reapember 351 AC** ([[Calendar]]). That's the night the ritual can be done, and the dead will rise and fly.

**How the party learns it.** In the catacombs, as Soth takes the fire, he tells them, out of contempt:

> "Run home, little knights. Count your moons. When the Eye opens over the Wastes, the dead of the old world will rise and fly, and then you may come and kneel to them."

Afterwards, the date can be pinned down:
- **[[Davos Bloodbane|a moon-touched PC]]** knows at once. He's a lunar sorcerer, and his own trial on the Barb fell on the last Night of the Eye, 504 days before. "The Eye" can only mean one night. He can tell them the date without a roll.
- **Wyhan** or **the Mages** can confirm it as a favour, or for free if Mages Standing is Trusted or better.
- **Lohezet's notes**, a captured officer, or Dalamar's research in the Wastes all point to the same night.

**How long.** The party lands at Wrecker's Edge on **23 Fierswelth** (see the Timeline), so the Night of the Eye is **47 days** after the landing. A party that does three or four locations and doesn't dawdle should get there with a week or two to spare. Tell the players the date. The point isn't to catch them out, it's to make the Wastes the only road.

**Why they can't wait it out.** The ships need weeks of work, and the army's food runs out long before that. Sailing home to resupply and coming back would take longer than they have. Whatever happens, someone has to cross the Wastes now.

**If they're late.** It isn't game over. The ritual has begun when they arrive: the plateau shakes, bone dragons stir in the canyon walls, and dead dragons claw out of the ground during Scenario 7 (add a dracolich-touched **young red dragon zombie**, or reflavour an extra dragonnel as a bone dragon, each round from round 3). Chapter 6 opens with the city already trembling. They can still stop it, but it'll cost more.

> [!tip] The countdown
> Keep a running count of **days left** next to the Supply and Water. Mention it every morning in one line: "Thirty-one days to the Eye." Track the moons day by day from the [[Calendar]], and let a moon-touched PC read them out.

### Leaving Kalaman

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[06-chapter-5-the-northern-wastes#Desperation in Kalaman|ch. 5, "Desperation in Kalaman"]]). The faction bullets are **Homebrew** ([[Faction Rules]]).

After the Trial, run the rest of the module's "Desperation in Kalaman" (the castle library, Darrett's negotiations and Wyhan's message), then settle the factions before the ships leave:

- **The Trial's verdicts** decide how the city sends them off, and adjust the dividends below ([[4. Duty#Every Road Leads to the Wastes]]).
- **Dividends.** The Guilds pay their **one final dividend** under their new leaders, and the Mages pay theirs for the Wastes (see [[Faction Rules#Dividends]]). Add everything up as **Supply** (food), **Water** and gear.
- **Last favours.** Any faction at Trusted or better can be asked for one last favour. Point the players at the "Ways the Party Can Fight Back" table in [[4. Duty - Survival]]. This is their last chance to buy a still, a map or a mule.
- **Travel light.** The quartermaster at the military docks tells them: the ships are small, the holds are for the troops' food and water, and each passenger brings one pack. Everything else goes into the Bastion, and a little spare gear can ride in Darrett's baggage. Don't hint at the ambush. Let a knight PC decide about his plate ([[4. Duty - Survival#Travel Light]]).
- **Who's on board.** Cudgel and Tatina volunteer as in the module. Tatina gives the party one pair of fargabs and keeps the other with Darrett.
- **A dragonborn PC hears the plan.** Darrett briefs the party on the route and the landing at Wrecker's Edge. Make sure the dragonborn PC is in the room. Takhisis is listening (see [[Wurm (DM)]]).

## Scenario 6: Dark Moon Rises

> [!wok] Source: **WoK** scenario 6 (the module doesn't reference it). The ambush on the crossing and the sinking of the supply ship are **Homebrew** ([[4. Duty - Survival#Cut Off]]). The module's crossing is uneventful.

The night crossing, run as described in "Cut Off" in [[4. Duty - Survival]]. It's the night of **22 Fierswelth**: Nuitari is full (invisible to all but its servants, though a moon-touched PC feels it), Lunitari is waning and Solinari is close to new, and the fleet sails into a fog bank in the middle of the bay. If you're using *Warriors of Krynn*, this is where its mass-combat scenario belongs. Otherwise, run it as a set piece aboard the party's ship.

> The lanterns go out one by one as the fog takes them, until all that is left of the fleet is the creak of other hulls somewhere in the grey, and a sailor singing softly to keep his nerve. Then the singing stops. Out of the fog, close enough to touch, comes a black sail, and then another, and the air fills with the hiss of fire arrows.

**The target.** The black ships go for the **supply ship**. The party's ship is alongside it, and they can see it being boarded and set alight. Let them try to save it: cross on grappling lines, fight on its burning deck, and fail. It goes down whatever they do. What they can save is a few crates: each round they spend fighting on its deck after the second wave, they haul back **1d6 Supply** or **1d6 Water**, but they lose the 1d4 × 5 roll from [[4. Duty - Survival]] twice over if anyone is still aboard when it sinks.

**Round by round.** Run three waves of boarders. Between waves, roll a d6: 1–2 the fire spreads (put it out within 2 rounds, or the supply ship's hold goes up), 3–4 a dragonnel strafes the deck (DC 14 Dex save, 2d10 fire), 5–6 a troopship nearby takes a fire arrow in the rigging, close enough to hear the screaming.

## The Starving Camp

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. It replaces the module's "Kalaman Troops" section until the army marches.

On the beach, Darrett does the sums out loud, and they're bad: a few days' rations for a few hundred soldiers, ships that need weeks of work, and black sails patrolling the bay. He stays at Wrecker's Edge with the soldiers and the engineers to mend the fleet, with Cudgel rationing the water with an axe. He can't spare the party a single ration, and he tells them so. What he needs is for them to find what the Dragon Army is doing out here, and to do it fast.

> [!warning] The clock
> The army runs out of food **10 days** after landing. After that, it loses soldiers every day, and the camp gets uglier: desertions, fights over the water, a soldier hanged for theft. Mark the days. Tatina's fargabs let Darrett report how it's going, and he's honest.

**Sending food back.** Anything the party finds and doesn't need can go back to the coast: carried there, sent with Heart's Hollow scouts, or brought by Clystran. The camp has a brackish spring, so it's short of food, not water.
- For every **20 Supply** sent, the army holds out **5 more days**, and **+1 Kalaman Military** (at most +2 in total).
- The richest sources are Camp Carrionclay's storehouse and Heart's Hollow's goodwill. A shipment from Carrionclay is the moment the camp cheers the party's name.
- If the party sends **nothing** and the clock runs out, the army fights Scenario 7 half-starved: every Kalaman soldier stat block has 1 level of exhaustion, and Darrett asks the party to their faces where the food went.

**The march.** When the party finds the City of Lost Names, they bring Darrett word (or call him on the fargabs), and the army marches to meet them, leaving the engineers to finish the ships. Heart's Hollow scouts guiding the army (module) let it move at normal pace. From then on, use the module's army camp rules, but every camp is a hungry one.

> [!example] Skirmish option: raid on the beached fleet
> *Source: **Homebrew** ([[Skirmish Battles]]).*
>
> Once in the chapter, ideally when the party comes back to the coast with food, a Dragon Army force finds the beached ships (thanks to the Leak, below).
> - **Objective:** keep the ships from burning for **5 rounds**. Each enemy unit that ends its turn touching a ship sets it alight, and every burning ship adds a week to the repairs.
> - **Allies:** 2 **Infantry** (hungry, so -2 Morale if the camp has run out of food), 1 **Siege Crew** (the engineers have turned a ship's ballista round), and Cudgel's **Mercenaries**.
> - **Enemies:** 2 **Baaz**, 1 **Kapaks** gliding down the cliffs, and 1 **Dragonnel Riders** with fire pots.
> - **The beach:** units in the surf are on **bad footing**.

## The Leak

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew** ([[Wurm (DM)]]).

Takhisis hears through a dragonborn PC until the dragonborn PC's story says otherwise. Whenever the party makes a plan in front of him, she can use it **once per location**: the Dragon Army patrol is waiting where they planned to camp, the draconians at Bluemaw have been warned, or Akhviri arrives at Carrionclay early. Don't overdo it. Two or three times in the chapter is enough for the players to start looking at each other.

## Locations

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[06-chapter-5-the-northern-wastes#Exploring the Wastes|ch. 5, "Exploring the Wastes"]]) for every site and encounter. The water and food, ties and extra payoffs in each list are **Homebrew** ([[4. Duty - Survival]] and the origin characters' DM notes).

Every location has three things worth marking: **water** (or food), **a reason to go there**, and **a tie** to one of the characters. Locations are lettered as on map 5.1.

### A: Wrecker's Edge

Run as in the module: the party scouts the coast for a landing and finds the smugglers' cove, the Silvanesti ship and its two air elementals, and Dalamar. The cove becomes **Darrett's camp** for the rest of the chapter.

- **Water.** The smugglers' huts hide a stone cistern of stale rainwater: **20 Water**, once. Barrels from the supply ship wash ashore over the first day: **1d4 × 5 Supply**, salt-soaked but edible, and Darrett's quartermaster claims all of it for the army unless someone argues.
- **The elven ship** is the only seaworthy, unburned vessel on this coast, and the players will want it, to fetch food from Kalaman. Dalamar won't give it up, the air elementals guard it, and stealing it strands the elves. Even if they take it, the black sails still patrol the bay. Let them be tempted.
- **Dalamar** is fed by his own magic, and he trades: a casting of *create food and water* (15 Supply and 30 Water) for news of Kalaman, or for an hour alone with a sorcerer PC.
- **Tie: [[Davos Bloodbane|a sorcerer PC]].** Dalamar sees what the sorcerer PC is at once, a sorcerer with raw, unschooled power, and he's fascinated. He offers to teach the moon-touched PC "the things Antimodes won't". It's real knowledge, and it's a black robe's offer. If the moon-touched PC accepts, give him one free moon charm and 1 point of *Heat*.

- **Map:** the same sail ship (companion map), as the elven ship in the hidden cove
- **Terrain:** a small deck, the cove's rocky beach and the rotten smugglers' huts at one edge.

```encounter
name: Elven Ship Guardians
creatures:
  - "2": Air Elemental (XMM)
```

> [!tip] **Avoidable.** This fight only happens if the party boards the elves' ship. If it does, one-turn it, and make sure Dalamar hears about it.

### B: Elven Encampment

Zhelsuel's exiles from House Mystic, cataloguing every rock in the Wastes. Run as in the module. Dalamar's arrangement (sending the party to C, D and E) is the chapter's spine.

- **Water and food.** The elves make what they need by magic and share nothing with outsiders. If Zhelsuel is won over (Elvish, DC 16 Persuasion, or a Silvanesti PC), they fill every waterskin the party has and give them **quith-pa**, elven travel bread: **10 Supply** that weighs only half a pound a ration.
- **Tie: [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)|a Silvanesti PC]].** Zhelsuel was his teacher, as near to an uncle as a prince could have. Zhelsuel doesn't recognise the scarred, white-haired thing in front of him, at least not at first. Give the Silvanesti PC the scene and let him choose (see [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn) (DM)]]):
  - **Reveal himself.** Zhelsuel breaks. The camp's doors open: free water and quith-pa every visit, the elves' maps (advantage on navigation within 2 hexes of the camp), and a dozen elven mages for Scenario 7, if Zhelsuel can be persuaded the war is Silvanesti's war too. And now the exiles know a Caladon prince is alive, and so will Alhana, eventually.
  - **Stay nameless.** Zhelsuel says, kindly, that a Silvanesti PC reminds him of a boy he once taught. That's all.
- **Tie: a sorcerer PC.** Zhelsuel is contemptuous of a human with untrained magic, and says so.

### C: Blue Phoenix Shrine

Habbakuk's flooded shrine and the Dimernesti sea elves. Run as in the module.

- **The sea elves** know which ravines carry fresh springs at low Wash. If the party helps Ishvern, he marks them on the map: **advantage on foraging checks** for the rest of the chapter.
- **Habbakuk's Blessing** (module) for everyone who honours the shrine.
- **Tie: a ranger PC.** Habbakuk is one of the ranger PC's gods. When he washes in the basin, a sea bird lands on the phoenix statue and watches him. Ishvern asks him, puzzled, why the shrine has never been so bright.

### E: Wakenreth

The House of Silence, the tower that fell off Onyari, half sunk in the Shadowfell. Run as in the module, then meet Dalamar here as he directs.

- **Tie: a spellcaster PC.** The second crypt in E2 holds a dead elf in the **white robes of a Mage of High Sorcery**, the second set the spellcaster PC has found by stealing. Whether he takes them is up to him.
- **Tie: [[Milo Geartickle|a tinkerer PC]].** Wakenreth came from Onyari. The drone goes mad with lights the moment it sees the tower, and calms only at the top, pointing north. This is the first sign that home is close.
- **Tie: a Silvanesti PC.** Silvanesti tradition demands a silver coin on the guardian's pedestal. The Silvanesti PC knows it without being told, and he's the only one who does.

### F: Bluemaw Cave

The old blue dragon's lair, the Migan clan, the gricks and the draconians digging out the hoard. Run as in the module.

- **Food and shelter.** Once the draconians are gone, the dwarves feed and shelter the party for as long as they like. Treat the cave like a settlement: rest, top up to **15 Supply**, refill every waterskin from the dwarves' seep in the rock, and no daily rolls while they're here.
- **Grasha's still.** Grasha is a gnomish-engineering enthusiast, and the draconians' gear is full of good brass. Give her two days and she builds a **gnomish still** (see [[4. Duty - Survival]]), if the party doesn't already have one. Her warning: "It works. Mostly. Don't stand behind it."
- **Bluemaw salt.** The blue crystals preserve meat for months. Butchered beasts give **double Supply** if packed in Bluemaw salt.
- **Tie: a tinkerer PC.** Grasha and the tinkerer PC get on terrifyingly well. A day working on the drone with her gives the tinkerer PC advantage on their next drone tinkering check.
- **Tie: a dragonborn PC.** The draconians here are his kind, digging out a dragon's bones. If any of them is spared, it's the first draconian the dragonborn PC has spoken to in his own tongue since he left the army. Use his level 7 moment.

- **Map:** [[map-5.05-bluemaw-cave-player.png|Map 5.5: Bluemaw Cave]] (module), area F1

### G: Heart's Hollow

The hidden crater village, Clystran and Ness. It's the one real town in the Wastes. Run as in the module, and start with Clystran and the giant spiders as the Wash rises.

- **Resupply.** Everything is scarce. End's Odds sells Supply and Water at **5 gp each** (a cutthroat price, and End knows it), and the town will gift up to **10 Supply** and all the Water they can carry to anyone who has earned a stone in the Mosaic. The crater's deep well is the only sweet water for fifty miles. The Fort can spare a mule for a favour.
- **Lavender's** tinctures: a draught that lets someone ignore one level of exhaustion for a day (25 gp, and it costs 1 extra Water that night).
- **Clystran** as a guide doubles travel distance and skips navigation (see [[4. Duty - Survival]]), once the party has fed his dragonnels at H.
- **Tie: a kender PC.** This is where the kender PC spent those lost months. Let the memories come back in a rush:
  - Halfway along the **Mosaic** there's a stone painted with a crooked little drone. The kender PC put it there.
  - **End Pike** has very large, patient hands. He taught the kender PC forgework, and he greets them by name: "Took you long enough, you little thief."
  - **Ness** found the kender PC half dead in the canyons years ago, and it was her who took the memories, gently, to keep the Hollow's secret. The kender PC saw her true shape once. She is the bronze dragon. Whether she admits it depends on the egg from Carrionclay.
  - This is also the moment to reveal who the kender PC promised never to return alone (see [[Milo Geartickle (DM)]]).

- **Map:** a canyon. [[1878331-edited_cliff.jpg|Cliff]] (companion map, probably) works.
- **Terrain:** the canyon walls, Clystran's rope hanging from the rim, and water-level markers (the Wash rises 10 feet on initiative 10 and 0).

```encounter
name: The Dragon Hunter
creatures:
  - "5": Giant Spider (XMM)
```

**Harvest** ([[Harvesting and Crafting]]): Each giant spider (Survival DC 11): **1 hide** (leather armour, 50 gp, 2 days) and **2d6 Supply**.

> [!check] Short full fight. **Objective:** save Clystran. He's on a rope above the rising Wash, and the spiders are climbing after him. If he falls, he's swept away and turns up a day later, half-drowned and indignant. Saving him on the spot makes him a friend for life.

### H: Dragonnel Spire

Twelve wasteland dragonnels on a rock shaped like a dragon. Run as in the module, with Clystran.

- **Water.** A rain pool in the dragon rock's "eye", far above the canyon floor, filled by the rare storms: **2d6 Water**, once, if someone climbs for it (DC 15 Athletics, and the dragonnels watch).
- **Payoff.** Clystran becomes the party's guide.
- **Tie: a dragonborn PC.** The dragonnels won't go near anyone but Clystran, except the dragonborn PC. They come right up to him and sniff, then back away, unsettled, as if they can't decide what he is.

The plateau of the Giant's Spine, the Dragon Army camp in the canyon, and Wind's End. Run "The Occupied City" as in the module.

## Scenario 7: Break the Walls

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[06-chapter-5-the-northern-wastes#K: City of Lost Names|ch. 5, "K: City of Lost Names"]]) and **WoK** scenario 7. The faction payoffs are **Homebrew**.

The assault on the canyon wall, while the party slips through Wind's End. This needs **Darrett's army** to march from Wrecker's Edge (see "The Starving Camp"). If the party never sends word, there's no distraction, and they'll have to find another way in.

Run the module's "The Allied Army" and "Assaulting the Dragon Army". With *Warriors of Krynn*, play its scenario 7. Without it, run "Attack on Wind's End" and its battlefield events table.

**Faction payoffs here:**
- **Kalaman Military** at Trusted or better: Darrett holds the line long enough. At Wary or worse, the attack starts to break after 3 rounds: roll on the battlefield events table twice each round.
- **A Silvanesti PC's exiles**, if Zhelsuel joined: elven mages strike the dragonnels overhead, so ignore battlefield event results of 1–4.
- **Fed or starving** (see "The Starving Camp").

- **Map:** [[map-5.08-attack-on-winds-end-player.png|Map 5.8: Attack on Wind's End]] (module)
- **Terrain:** the dragonnel pens and cage, crates of water and meat, the Fray at the edges, and the loose boards hiding the passage.

```encounter
name: Wind's End
creatures:
```
