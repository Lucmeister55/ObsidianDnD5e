## Overview

*Source: **Homebrew**. It replaces the **Module**'s [[03-chapter-2-prelude-to-war|Chapter 2: Prelude to War]]. The characters' ties to Ispin Greenshield and the sorcery trials happen off-screen, in their backstories.*

This is the first thing the players hear: a nightmare that hasn't happened yet. The dreamer is [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge|Edric]], and the dream is the **Invasion of Vogler**, a few days in the future ([[2. Broken Silence]]), as it might go. Nobody at the table should know any of that yet. Don't name him, the village or the enemy.

It has one job: show the players the end of the road before they've taken the first step. Vogler burns, the knight's companions die (the other player characters, though nobody knows that yet), and whatever he chooses, the boy in the rubble dies too. Then something comes out of the sky that was supposed to be gone from the world. It's a promise of what's coming, and that it can't be stopped by one man alone.

> [!tip] Running it
> - Read the first box in the dark, or with eyes closed. Keep it slow.
> - After the first box, let the players decide who claims this character. That player makes the choice in the second box.
> - **Every choice ends the same way.** Don't soften it. The players should come away knowing this campaign doesn't care how brave you are.
> - **Write down which choice Edric's player makes.** He lives this exact moment again in Vogler, for real ("Déjà Vu" in [[2. Broken Silence]]), and there, choosing differently can change it.
> - Then go straight into [[1. An Ordinary Day]], where the same knight rides west on a bright spring morning, and let the players work out whose dream it was.

## The Dream

**Music:** [Battle In City](ttrpg-sound:Soundbar/Ambience/Battle In City.mp3)

> Darkness. No sound, no room to move, nothing but a still, close dark that felt very like mercy. He thought he could sleep here. Mother would come and wake him soon. She always did.
>
> But Mother was dead. He remembered the smell of the smoke, and her arms around him, and how still she had gone. And the old man was dead too, the one who had pulled him out of those ashes and trusted him, and gone away down the road without him. What was one man against all that? He had always known it. The best anyone could do was let go.
>
> He would stay here, a little while longer.
>
> Then he gasped awake.
>
> Ash filled his mouth, and he coughed it out in black strings. His ears rang. A beam lay across his chest, and he got his hands under it and heaved, and the stones shifted, and he was out. He stood, and doubled over, and was sick in the rubble. Smoke ran up the street like water running the wrong way. Above it, on the cliffs against the night, stood the black shell of a roofless keep, with fire in its windows.
>
> The ringing faded. In its place came the screaming.
>
> His companions had been with him when the blast came. He could not have said their names, or how he knew them, only that they were his. He looked for them in the rubble, for a cloak, a voice, a hand, anyone. There was no one.
>
> He picked up his sword.

*Prompt the players to decide who will claim this origin character.*

> You stumble down the burning street. Out of the smoke come shapes that run like men but are not men: scaled things with clawed feet and stunted wings, their eyes catching the firelight, armour of red and black on their backs. There are more of them than you can count. They have seen you.
>
> To your left, under a fallen roof beam, a young man is pinned in the wreck of a doorway. He's barely more than a boy, with a fisherman's knife still in his belt and a militia armband gone black with soot. He reaches out a hand toward you.
>
> "Please..."
>
> The creatures are coming. What do you do?
>
> 1. Heave the beam off him before they reach you.
> 2. Drag him out from under it, whatever it does to him.
> 3. Stand between him and them, and buy him time to crawl free.

*Read only the ending they chose.*

> 1. The beam lifts. It's enough. He slides free and falls into your arms, alive and gasping, and for one heartbeat you hold him. Then something drops from the broken roof above, all scales and claws, and drives into him before you can raise your sword. His eyes go wide. He doesn't make a sound.
> 2. You drag him out by the arms while he screams, and haul him up, and run. He's heavy and you're failing, and every step is slower than the last. They catch you at the end of the street. You hear the blades go into him, again and again, and his voice breaks, and stops.
> 3. Steel meets claw, and for a moment you hold them all. Behind you, you can hear him dragging himself out of the doorway. Then the wall above him cracks. You don't even have time to turn. When the dust settles, there is nothing moving behind you.

> You kill them. Every one of them that comes. Then the strength runs out of you all at once, and you go down on your knees in the ash, in front of the place where he was.
>
> You look up at the sky, at the gods who stopped answering three hundred and fifty years ago.
>
> The stars are still there, cold and far away. Then something passes over them, and they go out. Something vast is coming down out of the dark, slow and certain, on wings as wide as the street is long. Somewhere close by, a man is crying the same word over and over, a word out of the old stories, a word for a thing that was supposed to be gone from the world.
>
> "*Dragon.*"
>
> And the sky catches fire.

*Let the silence sit. Then:*

> He woke in the barracks at Kalaman, in the cold before dawn, with the sheets soaked through and the wind coming in off the bay. Outside, somebody was whistling. A horse stamped in the yard. It was spring.

*Go to [[1. An Ordinary Day]].*
