## Introduction

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**, a rewrite of the module's opening from Edric's perspective (see [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge]]).

**Music:** [1-05 - Prelude](ttrpg-sound:Soundbar/FE-Awakening/1-05 - Prelude.mp3)

> 
> It is the year 351 AC, three hundred and fifty-one years since the Cataclysm, and the 13th day of Brookgreen. It was a hard winter. You spent it in the yards at Maelgoth, drilling in sleet with the wind coming down off the plains, until your hands split and the frost got into your mail, and then you knelt before the Council and swore the Oath. That is behind you now. Spring has come to Hinterlund all at once. The brooks run loud and full, the birches are putting out their first green, and there are lambs in the fields by the river. The sun is warm on your back for the first time in months. You wear a knight's spurs, a few weeks old and still bright. On a morning like this it is easy to believe the world is mending.
> 
> You ride west with two nobles in your charge. Becklin Uth Viharin rides on their right: a Knight of the Crown like you, older, longer in the saddle, and the one who sets the pace. You crossed the Vingaard by ferry early this morning. Since then the road has left the river flats and climbed into the forest, narrowing until it is little more than a cart track between the trees.
> 
> Her hair has gone silver, and she wears it in a plain braid down her back, though a few strands always work loose by noon. Her face is lined and weathered, quick to smile and slow to frown. She rides in full plate, old but well kept, worked all over with Solamnic knotwork, and her horned helm hangs from her saddle beside a round shield that has been dented and beaten straight more times than you could count. For ten years she has been castellan of Thornwall Keep above Vogler. Ispin used to say she had retired without telling anyone, least of all herself.
> 
> Vogler lies at the end of this road, on the western edge of Hinterlund, where the track gives out and the river takes over. You have never been there. But Ispin Greenshield talked about it often, and so did Becklin, the few times your paths crossed. Both spoke of it the way people speak of home. North of here, the woods thin into brook and scrub, and beyond them lie the Northern Wastes. South, across the river, lies Nightlund, a land of hard storms and a bad name, though few can tell you any more how it earned it. Its capital is Kalaman, the Beacon of the East, down where the Vingaard meets the sea. Its walls run out into the harbour, and at either end stands a tower with a beacon that the old Istarians lit before the Cataclysm. Sailors say its light will find you through any fog.
> 
> The nobles in your charge are the Bakarises, father and son, Vogler men going home for the Kingfisher Festival. Between them they have made the road twice as long as it is.
> 
> The elder Bakaris is a heavy man gone soft in the saddle, red in the face and wrapped in furs too warm for the season, with a ring on every finger that will take one. He has complained of everything since the ferry: the mud, the cold, the pace, the ferryman's manners, the birches. You had his measure inside the first mile.
> 
> His son is another matter. Bakaris the Younger is lean and dark and handsome, and knows it. His riding coat is better cut than anything you own, and a fine ebony crossbow rides across his back, oiled and unscuffed. He talks less than his father and smiles more, and of the two, you have come to like the smiling least.
> 
> Behind the son's horse walks his servant, a great stooped creature, scaled like a lizard, carrying a load no horse was asked to bear. Even this near Kalaman, where the law is meant to count for something, there are serfs. The elder Bakaris calls it Wurm, and calls it a good beast, the only one of the company that can keep up. The creature has not said a word all morning. You have not once seen it lift its eyes from the road. Despite the noble's demeaning comments, you can't help but feel sorry for it, stumbling through the mud on its bare feet as it was.
> 
> Not long after the ferry, Becklin suggested a slower pace. The north is close, she said, and it is no place to draw attention. That was all the Bakarises needed. They have been at her ever since: about restraint, about discipline, about what all the Knights' fine oaths did to stop the Cataclysm. She answered them as patiently as she answers everything. The Knights were never charged with stopping it, she said, only with holding what could still be held. Crown for loyalty, Sword for courage, Rose for wisdom. She named herself a Knight of the Crown, and you beside her.
> 
> At last, as you knew they would, they came round to Dargaard, the black castle in the mountains of Nightlund, and the shadow that is said to hang over it still, the curse of some ancient evil no one can name. The old whispers call it the Dread Knight. Every noble keeps that tale ready for a knight's company, because the story goes that the evil was once one of the Order's own. For the first time that day, Becklin's jaw set hard. "You know the story," she told them. "Not the truth."
> 
> No one has spoken since. The trees close in and the road climbs, and you find you cannot put his words out of your mind.
>

> 
> As you approach, it's wooden palisades reveals itself slowly. The village clings to a narrow spit of land reaching into the river, its wooden homes clustered tightly together as if for comfort against the wider world. Cliffs rise behind it, crowned by the ivy-choked remains of a crumbling keep, watching silently over the settlement below. The only road descends from that height, guiding all who arrive directly into the heart of the village.
> 
> Dominating the river is a structure that does not belong to this age: an immense, unfinished stone bridge, its craftsmanship far surpassing anything built in recent memory. It stretches partway across the Vingaard before ending abruptly, replaced by simple ferries—ropes and rafts that carry travelers the rest of the way. Around it, small fishing boats drift across the slow, murky waters, their presence lending the village a quiet, persistent life.
> 
> Becklin notes, as the village comes into clearer view, that you have arrived at an opportune time. The Kingfisher Festival is underway—a local celebration meant to bring music, color, and warmth to an otherwise modest place. Even from a distance, there are signs of it: movement along the docks, decorations catching the light, the faint suggestion of gathering crowds.
> 
> Under normal circumstances, you might have welcomed it. A festival meant rest, distraction, perhaps even a moment of ease among your duties.
> 
> But not this time.
> 
> You know what awaits in Vogler. You volunteered for this assignment because of it. And as the village draws nearer, the weight of that choice settles in your chest, heavier than steel, dulling whatever comfort the festival might have offered.
> 
> But you, Edric of Brackenridge, had to see it through. For an old friend.
