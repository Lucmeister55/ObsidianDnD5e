## Arrival in Vogler

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Arriving in Vogler|ch. 3, "Arriving in Vogler"]]), with **Homebrew** character introductions (see the origin characters' notes).

As soon as the entourage unmounts in the village square, have Becklin steer Edric towards the Fishbowl tavern to meet up with Darrett and Cudgel.

> The Fishbowl is one low room with a fire at the far end, and every table in it is taken. The air is thick with pipe smoke and frying fish. In a corner an old man with a banjo is losing a long argument with his own strings, and nobody minds. Behind the bar, a glass jar hangs from a beam with one fat goldfish turning slowly in it.
>
> By the fire, a short, broad dwarf with copper braids bound in iron rings is singing something unfit for company. An iron-shod club leans against her chair, and she holds a tankard the size of her head. You have ridden with her on Ispin's road, and the stories were not exaggerated. Across the table, a young man in a polished tabard has risen halfway to his feet, a hand of cards still fanned in his fist. It is Darrett. He is grinning, and he has knocked over his ale.

> The man sitting opposite him does not get up. He is young and lean and sun-browned from days on the river road, and he deals the next hand without looking at his fingers. His hair falls to his shoulders, silver as a new coin in the firelight. He wears a sky-blue shirt under a dark vest sewn with small stars, and his right sleeve is rolled to the elbow, over a line of moons inked down the forearm, from full to thin to gone. A neat pile of Darrett's coins sits at his elbow. He looks at the knight's tabard on your chest, and smiles as if you were exactly the person he'd been waiting for.

*Prompt the players to decide who will claim this origin character, then give them the character sheet.*

**Cudgel** is here too, drunk by mid-afternoon. Before she's claimed anyone's attention for long, she hauls Edric down onto the bench beside her, still gripping his arm, and the singing stops being cheerful. Play her as a loud woman who has gone quiet in the wrong places, slurring and grinning and not meaning the grin. Give her a few of these, one at a time, and let Edric answer or not:

> "Lad. LAD. You look just like him when he's disappointed in me. Don't. I've had a day."
>
> "Fourteen years he had you on the road. Thirty he had me. And you know what he sent me, when he went home to retire? A letter. Two lines. 'Come for the festival, Cud, and bring coin for the tavern.' That's it. Two lines."
>
> "They're gonna put him in the ground tomorrow, and I'm gonna stand there, and I'm gonna say something loud and clever, and I'll feel nothing. Because I'll be sober by then. That's the problem with sober."
>
> "A beast, they said. A BEAST. That man once stared down a fire giant over a card game. A beast. Pah." *(She drinks. She doesn't finish the thought.)*

She breaks off when **Becklin** strides in, the way she'd collar a stray dog, and says "That's enough of that, Cud" quietly enough that only the table hears. Becklin takes her under one arm, nods to Edric and Darrett, and walks her back to her camp. Cudgel goes without a fight, mumbling "I'm fine, I'm fine, I'm *fine*", and she disappears until she's needed later.

**Davos** arrived this morning, expecting a ferry to Kalaman, but the Kingfisher Festival has delayed every one of them and he didn't know. With nothing else to do, and having overheard that Darrett is a knight, he sat down at his table to get into the Knights' good graces before Kalaman. For the last hour, he has been playing cards with Darrett and cheating with a little magic, but not too much. Once he's claimed, he tags along with the party through the festival, keeping up a cheerful, engaged front.

### Optional Arrival Vignettes

> [!companion] Source: **Companion** ([[2-When_Home_Burns.pdf]], Part 3, "First Day in Vogler").

If the characters reach Vogler in separate groups, give each a small scene that introduces a villager the party will meet again:
- **By ferry.** The group crosses the Vingaard on the rope ferry beside **Froswin**, the kender stallholder, who takes an interest in their adventuring gear. If they are kind to her, she offers to help them find any vendor at tomorrow's festival.
- **At the Fishbowl.** The group is already waiting when **Elthar**, the owner, offers a free sample of a new pastry for an honest opinion. It's delicious, though a little heavy on the butter.

### Running Order

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns|ch. 3]]) and **Companion** ([[2-When_Home_Burns.pdf]], Parts 3–5), in the order this campaign uses them.

1. **Day 1, afternoon.** Arrival, the Fishbowl, and the companion's **missing child** search with Darrett (Ross, who "saw a dragon in the woods"). This is where the wounded dragonnel crashes in (see "The Missing Child and the Stolen Shield" below).
2. **Day 1, dusk.** **Ispin's funeral** at the Brass Crab's dock, then the reception: tales of Ispin, the **heckler** (Bakaris the Younger), **Cudgel**, the **mysterious mourner** (Leedara), and Milo, if they go looking for the shield, finding it isn't on the body.
3. **Day 2, morning.** Becklin's invitation to **Thornwall Keep**: breakfast, Darrett, and Ispin's parting gift (module, "Parting Gift").
4. **Day 2, midday.** The **Kingfisher Festival**, at most 4 activities.
5. **Day 2, afternoon.** The walk to **High Hill**, the reenactment, and the betrayal.

### Vogler at a Glance

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Vogler Gazetteer|ch. 3, "Vogler Gazetteer"]], map 3.1) and **Companion** (Appendix B, "Location Reference Sheet"; Part 3, "Vogler Details").

Vogler is a fishing village of about **four hundred** people at the foot of a pre-Cataclysm bridge that stops halfway across the Vingaard. It's under the distant protection of Maelgoth. Fish carvings are everywhere, the festival honours Habbakuk (the "Fisher King", mostly forgotten), and the villagers are proud of their hospitality but share Solamnia's distrust of the Knights: mention the Order and the room goes quiet.

### Vogler's Details

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Vogler Gazetteer|ch. 3, "Vogler Gazetteer"]]; map: [[map-3.01-vogler-player.png|Map 3.1: Vogler]]) and **Companion** (Part 3, "Vogler Details"; Appendix B).

- **Habbakuk.** The festival and the carvings nod to Habbakuk, the Fisher King, neutral good god of animal life and natural balance, far less worshipped than he was. A cleric of Habbakuk, or a character who succeeds on a **DC 12 Intelligence (Religion)** check, recognises the bird-with-a-fish carving on the post at the end of the southernmost pier as a **phoenix**, his symbol. Locals rub it for luck before the morning catch.
- **The festival's origin.** It commemorates Lord Decater Vogler's victory with the Knights of Solamnia over the Empire of Istar, centuries before the Cataclysm, which led to the village's founding. The kingfisher is a symbol of the Knights and a lucky bird to Vogler's fishers.
- **Protection.** Hinterlund's capital, Maelgoth, protects the village in name, with the odd representative checking in and Becklin's occasional report. The village is peaceful, bothered only by bandits and predators from the Northern Wastes.
- **The Brass Crab.** A single-storey inn on the wharf, round common room, four short halls of rooms to rent. The party's funeral invitations hold rooms there. Yalme calls the crabs on the faded mural Fancy and Gorgeous George.
- **The Fishbowl.** Cramped but well kept, famous for its baked fish pies more than its weak ale.
- **The Mercenary Camp.** The Ironclad Regiment, a mile past High Hill. Cudgel brought them a week ago for Ispin's funeral and volunteered them for the reenactment. By day she stays with her lieutenants, Gragonis and Jeyev, and several dozen mercenaries who drill. At night they keep to themselves under strict orders not to bother the locals. (Companion: if the party visits before the festival, Cudgel is abrasive and most of the way through a bottle of wine. Before they leave she apologises and promises them a meal or a drink the next day.)
- **The River Gate.** A 15-foot wooden arch carved with two leaping fish, prayers for good fishing etched on their scales. It's unguarded, and its doors are embedded in the dirt.
- **The Village Circle.** A grassy green with an ancient tree, ringed by a dirt road. A tall pole by the mayor's house carries an **iron bell**: ring it hard and the mayor arrives in about 5 minutes with **2d12 volunteer militia** (use the guard stat block), mostly retired farmers and fishers with more zeal than skill. (The companion's reference sheet says 1d12.)
- **Vogler's Crossing.** The unfinished bridge, a glorified pier. A ferry of ropes and winches runs along its supports to posts on the far shore. Rafts and skiffs carry travellers and small wagons across in about **10 minutes**, or hire a ferrier for **1 sp**.
- **Westide House.** A horseshoe-shaped house barely bigger than its neighbours, which Lord Bakaris speaks of as an opulent manor. He claims to live in exile after raiders burned his family home in Estwilde. In truth, he and his son were driven from the region and sought anonymity in Vogler, where they have spent over a year throwing their wealth around and treating the villagers like yokels.
  - **Why Vogler puts up with him (homebrew).** Money, and a debt. After the hard winter and the sahuagin that killed Raven's father, the village couldn't pay for the wharf's repairs or for the Ironclad Regiment's contract to train the militia. **Bakaris paid for both, as a loan against the wharf.** He doesn't call it in as long as he is treated like a lord. His title also keeps the Maelgoth tax collector away, and his name on a letter to Hinterlund's capital counts for something. Raven is a scout, and she has worked out that he didn't come here for the fish, but she needs the loan and keeps quiet about the exile. Bakaris knows she knows. Neither says so. Everyone else in Vogler grumbles, and drinks his free ale.
- **The Wharfinger's Office.** Wharfinger **Umpton Lanth** inspects boats at dawn and fines those in poor repair. His son **Nesau** forgives fines and waives fees whenever his father isn't there, which makes him very popular.
- **The festival stalls** sell fish-filled pastries, sweet drinks and kingfisher-shaped crafts for **3 cp** each.

### The Missing Child and the Stolen Shield

> [!companion] Source: **Companion** ([[2-When_Home_Burns.pdf]], p. 6 onwards), with the **Homebrew** changes below (see [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge (DM)]] for the Red Ruin and the shield).

**The search for Ross** (companion, "Finding Darrett" and "Finding the Lost Child").

- **The call for help.** A boy named **Ross** says he saw a dragon in the woods yesterday and ran away from home when no one believed him. He hasn't come back, so Raven has organised a search, and Darrett volunteers. Raven offers **25 gp** to anyone who helps find the boy. (The companion's version has Darrett not turn up at the Fishbowl. The party waits two hours, then asks around: a **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation)** check leads them to Raven, who says Darrett joined the search. Use that if you'd rather the party go looking for him.)
- **Finding the boy.** An hour of searching the woods and a **DC 15 Wisdom (Survival)** check finds Ross's or Darrett's footprints. On a failure the party wanders an hour and takes **1d4 - 1 piercing** damage from hazards (a thorn bush, a tree root), and the DC drops by 1 after each failure.
- **The dragonnel.** Once they find the child or Darrett, a character with **passive Perception 15** hears loud flapping wings. A moment later a dragonnel ([[dragon-army-dragonnel-dsotdq|Dragon Army dragonnel]]) drops out of the trees, with a **surprise round** if nobody passed. It was a Red Dragon Army scout's mount whose rider died in an accident a few days ago. It has since shed its rider and its barding breastplate, so its **AC is 12**. Darrett helps (if the party tracked Ross first, he follows the noise and joins the initiative after 1 round).
- **If you fight it** (five players, level 3): 2 dragonnels, which retreat at half hit points. The companion wrote it for a level 2 party, so for level 3 let them fight on, or add a third. After a kill, a **DC 15 Intelligence (History)** check identifies the species, and a **DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation)** check notices indentations on its scales where a saddle was once strapped.
- **After the search,** the companion lets the party take a **long rest** so they are fully rested for the festival.

**What changes in this campaign:**

- Once the party finds the missing child, it blubbers out that there is a dragon chasing him overhead, with a terrified look in its eyes. Upon saying that, a dragonnel crashes through the boughs of the trees. It gets up, looking dazed, wounded and feral. Make the dragonnel immediately flee if the characters take hostile action. If the party searches the area around the dragonnel, they find a clasp of what could have been a belt of some kind. If they tell anyone from the village, they say it's probably just a dragonnel from the Northern Wastes (false).
- Ispin's shield was already gone when the villagers found him. Gragonis took it off the corpse in the woods that night and carried it back to the Ironclad camp (companion), so it was never at the funeral, and nobody in Vogler knows it was stolen. Furthermore, he clearly died by some sort of bestial strike, though Becklin believes they were too long and targeted to have been some random wild beast from the wastes. In fact, the true culprit was the Red Ruin's dragonnel mount.

### The Funeral and Reception

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Final Farewell|ch. 3, "Final Farewell"]]), with **Companion** and **Homebrew** additions where marked.

- **Sending off a friend.** At dusk, on the stage and dock behind the Brass Crab, Ispin's body goes out on a funeral boat, with locals in small boats on the water around it. (*Homebrew:* Soth's servants take the boat downstream, see [[Soth's Host]].)
- **Tales remembered.** Yalme keeps bread, cheese and ale coming. Everyone in the village turns up, and people share stories of Ispin. Edric's player should start with one ([[Ispin Greenshield#Stories to Tell About Him]]). This is a **+1 villagers** moment.
- **The heckler.** After a story or two, Bakaris the Younger yawns loudly and calls out that they've "heard this a thousand times". Confronting him works with roleplay or DC 12 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion). It shouldn't come to blows. It establishes him as a cad.
- **Cudgel**, back from her camp where Becklin sobered her up, introduces herself to the rest of the party as Ispin's old friend and the leader of the Ironclad Regiment. (Edric already knows her from Ispin's road, and she has already talked at him in the Fishbowl.) She's still abrasive, and apologises for it later (companion).
- **The mysterious mourner.** Leedara, a blue-skinned Silvanesti elf, keeps to the edges and calls herself a seer. Anyone with passive Perception 14+ notices her watching them. She talks long enough to leave an impression (a cryptic warning, a word about fate), then is gone behind a passing villager. She's a ghost bound to Soth's story (module ch. 4).
- **Milo's attempt (homebrew).** If Milo's player goes for Ispin's shield, they assume it lies on the body like any knight's, so they will "borrow" it before the boat is launched or from the shrouded bier. It isn't there. Ispin lies in a plain shroud with his sword at his side and nothing on his arm. Milo can hush it up or tell Edric, and it plants the question of where the shield is. Becklin has not told Edric the shield is missing (see [[#Parting Gift]]).
- **After the funeral,** Becklin invites the party to Thornwall Keep in the morning: Ispin left something for Edric.

#### Meeting Milo (homebrew)

*As the mourners gather on the dock, before Becklin speaks, introduce the kender origin character.*

> The dock behind the Brass Crab is crowded with lanterns and low voices. Out at the far end a little boat rests on the water, with a shape in a grey shroud laid along it, and a string of paper kingfishers hung over it.
>
> A small figure is kneeling at the edge of the planks, leaning out over that boat as far as a person can lean without going in. They are round-faced and freckled, with a topknot stuck full of pencils and twigs, and a mustard-yellow scarf wound up to their ears. A brass thing the size of a fist hums at their shoulder, turning to watch each mourner in turn.
>
> The figure sees you and waves, with a hand that is holding somebody else's candle. "Hello! Sorry. Is it rude to ask where they've put his shield? I only want to look."

*Prompt the players to decide who will claim this origin character, then give them the character sheet ([[Milo Geartickle]]).* If the answer to the shield question matters later, see "Milo's attempt" above.

#### Becklin and the Eulogy (module)

Becklin wears full plate and a horned helm. She sent the funeral invitations . She already knows Edric, who rode in with her; to the other characters she introduces herself as Ispin's friend, since Ispin never spoke of them. She offers her condolences, and explains that the service is a **celebration**: people are encouraged to share over-the-top stories, like the inflated tales he was known for. At dusk, with the mourners' small boats in the water, a musician plays softly and she takes the stage by the little boat where Ispin lies shrouded:

> "You all knew Ispin as a friend, and there were none like him. Before he died, he told me this: 'There should be tales, not tears, for an old scoundrel like me. Make sure everyone knows my stories when I head off on the greatest adventure of them all.'"
>
> Villagers carry the boat to the dock's edge, where she and Cudgel lower it into the water. "So, let us do just that. On the eve of the Kingfisher Festival, we celebrate you, Ispin. Good travels, friend. Until we meet again."

The boat drifts downriver out of sight. After a moment of silence Becklin invites everyone into the Brass Crab.

#### The Heckler, Cudgel and the Mourner (module)

- **The heckler.** After one or two tales, **Bakaris the Younger** lets out an exaggerated yawn, and if left alone blurts out "We've heard this a thousand times!" or "You're telling it worse than he did!" He cares nothing for Ispin or the mourners; he's there for the free ale, which he says is the worst he's ever tasted. The mourners grumble, but nobody confronts him. Use the scene to establish him as a **cowardly cad**, without coming to violence. A character who confronts him can silence him by roleplaying or with a **DC 12 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion)** check. Either way he scoffs that nobody in Vogler can take a joke and leaves of his own accord.
- **Cudgel.** In the module she jovially introduces herself as Ispin's old friend and leader of the Ironclad Regiment, asks how they knew him and where they came from, talks freely about tomorrow's reenactment and leaves early to check on her camp. (The companion's version is the drunk, abrasive Cudgel above.)
- **Leedara.** She arrived the day before the characters, and nobody knows anything about her but her name and that she says she was a performer who knew Ispin (the companion has her call herself a seer). She hovers at the edge, polite, aloof and evasive, sympathises with the party and says she has lost many loved ones in her long life. A **DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion)** check gets her to sing a heartbreakingly beautiful Silvanesti song in Elvish. Soon after she retires to her room at the inn. Use the scene to introduce her, but reveal nothing of her agenda: she has a role in chapters 4 and 7. **Stat block:** a **ghost** (see [[Leedara]]) with one more action, **Change Shape**: she assumes the appearance she had in life, her creature type becomes Humanoid and her other statistics stay the same; this ends if she drops to 0 hit points or uses an action to end it.

### Parting Gift

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[04-chapter-3-when-home-burns#Parting Gift|ch. 3, "Parting Gift"]]), with **Homebrew** changes.

On the way to the keep, Darrett, whom the party met at the Fishbowl yesterday, catches up with them. At Thornwall Keep, Becklin gives them breakfast and tells Edric Ispin left him something, but she does **not** mention the shield, or that it was stolen, until Edric asks. Her reason: she was ashamed, and wanted to tell them gently once the funeral was over. If Milo has tried to take it at the funeral, or Edric simply asks "where is his shield?", she admits it: it was not on his body when the hunters found him, only a bare scabbard and a torn strap. It was taken in the woods, not from the funeral. **Gragonis** took it off the corpse (companion), and the party gets it back from him at High Hill (homebrew). Over breakfast, Becklin shares what she suspects about Ispin's death.

**Becklin's suspicions** (companion, "Investigating Ispin's Murder"): Ispin was found far off his usual patrol route, his sword was still sheathed, and the wound was too long and too precise for a beast. If the party goes to the spot, they find large clawed prints that start and stop out of nowhere (DC 14 Survival), and a ragged gap in the branches overhead (DC 12 Perception): something came down from the sky. It was Red Ruin's dragonnel (homebrew).

#### Meeting Darrett (module)

On the way to the keep the morning is busy: stalls going up and decorations being hung, with the keep on its low hill ghostly in the mist. Darrett steps out of a shop doorway with a sword at his hip and a cloth sack under his arm: "Good morning, everyone. Glad I caught you. I hope you haven't had breakfast already." Becklin sent him to buy baked hand pies at the Fishbowl for them all, and he invites the party along. He's eager but doesn't press if they'd rather go straight to the keep.

If they join him, he tells them he grew up in Vogler and peppers them with questions about themselves, their travels, their weapons and the world beyond. He shares:
- The festival celebrates Lord Decater Vogler's victory over the forces of the Empire of Istar, hundreds of years ago, which sparked the village's founding.
- Today's music, food and games all go back to the town's founding.
- Cudgel's mercenaries are training Vogler's small militia, and they take part in this afternoon's reenactment of the Battle of High Hill.

**Give Darrett one running joke** from this scene: he is jokingly frustrated that he's still a squire ("Becklin says next year. She's said next year for three years"). It matters later, because after Vogler he stops joking about it ([[Darrett Highwater#Why He's Still a Squire]]).

**Establish Darrett as reliable** from this scene: earnest, clever, a little starstruck, and always with the party's best interests in mind, with whatever quirks your players will find charming, so they know they can rely on him later.

#### Thornwall Keep (module)

Veins of bronze-coloured ivy cling to the keep's three-storey stone tower, the tallest building in Vogler, with the gnomeflinger on its crenellated roof. Crumbling walls and fallen stonework surround it, among shallow square pits and covered excavation sites. Becklin meets them at the door in a simple shirt and breeches and takes them to her first-floor library. The ground floor holds a small library and **over forty pieces of rusty tools and armour** from the excavations, Darrett's quarters are on the second floor, and Becklin's austere room is on the top floor, with a ladder up to the roof. Over breakfast (Darrett hands out the hand pies) she makes small talk about the keep and her excavations.

**The gnomeflinger** is the work of **Than**, Vogler's tinker gnome, who drops by to fine-tune it and makes early-morning noise doing it. It looks like a weapon but is rapid transport, a design from the tinker gnomes of Mount Nevermind. Becklin tolerates it, and the noise, because she thinks a catapult-shaped device deters attack.

#### The Green Shield (module)

*In the module, this is where Becklin hands over the shield. Here it is missing (see above), so use the rest as her request, once the shield comes up.* Ispin's signature shield is a **+1 shield** carved with a scarred broadleaf tree and painted mossy green. Everyone who knew him remembers his story that a unicorn gave it to him in Darken Wood, and nobody can say whether it's true. Becklin's condition, in the module: Ispin won't be at this year's festival, so he wants them to **take his place in the reenactment of the Battle of High Hill**. Every year he looked forward to taking part, and dying, in a more ludicrous way than the year before. In the module she gives the shield only if one of them agrees. Here there is no shield to hand over, so it is just her request: whoever agrees takes Ispin's place in the reenactment in his honour. If the party has been pleasant she chats a while longer before saying she has to prepare for the festival. Darrett eagerly suggests they all go together.

#### The Scene of the Murder (companion)

If the party asks to see where Ispin was found, Becklin escorts them. The ground is stained red with his blood and surrounded by large clawed footprints. Clues:
- **DC 14 Wisdom (Survival):** the unusually long claw marks mean it was not a direwolf, and the tracks seem to appear from nowhere, with no sign of anything arriving or leaving. If the party already fought the dragonnel, the marks are also too wide and large to be from it.
- **DC 16 Intelligence (Investigation):** three sets of humanoid footprints besides the hunters'. One has sharp angles, made by armoured greaves (Ispin). One is larger, in sturdy leather boots (Gragonis). One is slim and shallow, in shoes ill-made for woodland travel (Lohezet, who wore Cudgel's shape; her own boots are heavy and hobnailed, so the prints don't match hers).
- **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception):** an unusual number of broken branches on the ground, and a large gap in the branches overhead, where Red Ruin burst from the trees on her dragonnel.
- **The hunters' story (homebrew).** One of the hunters who found the body remembers seeing Ispin the evening before, heading into the woods with **a dwarf and an ogre**. He knew Ispin well, but he doesn't know the mercenaries, and he never got a look at their faces (dusk, hoods, a long way off). Vogler has almost no dwarves, but the Ironclad Regiment has several (Cudgel recruits from Kayolin) and one half-ogre, **Gragonis**, so the trail points at the **Regiment**, not at anyone in particular. Cudgel is the dwarf the party knows best, and the one with the most reason to be doubted, so a suspicious party may settle on her. It was really Lohezet wearing Cudgel's face (a *disguise self*). The hunter wouldn't swear to it, and nobody in Vogler suspects Cudgel just from walking past her. Cudgel says she was in camp all night, and her alibi is Jeyev, who is also a traitor, so it is worthless. If the party suspects her, play it straight: Gragonis betrayed her, Svilnt tried to kill her, and she's been sold out before. The proof is on the ground: the footprints at the scene (DC 16 Investigation, below) show the "dwarf's" tread was **slim and shallow, in shoes ill-made for the woods**, not the heavy hobnailed boots Cudgel wears. A *disguise self* changes the face, not the weight.
- Becklin knows: Ispin was found far from his usual patrol route; his wound was a single hole through the back of his armour into his heart (only a powerful, precise strike could do it); his sword was still in its sheath; and his shield was gone.
