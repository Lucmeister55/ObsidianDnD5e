# The Northern Wastes: Survival

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**, built on the **Module**'s travel rules, the Wash and the encounter tables ([[06-chapter-5-the-northern-wastes#Into the Wastes|ch. 5, "Into the Wastes"]]).

The survival rules for [[4. Duty]]. The Northern Wastes are a land the Cataclysm broke and never mended: endless canyons, blistering days and freezing nights, storms out of a clear sky, and the **Wash**, a salt flood driven by the three moons that roars through the canyons without warning and leaves them bone-dry a week later. Out here there is **no fresh water**, almost no food, and nobody coming to help.

These rules are meant to hurt. They're also meant to be easy to run, because there are only **three things to track**: each character's **Supply** (food), their **Water**, and their **exhaustion**.

## Cut Off

The party is cut off from the moment they land. The army can't feed them, and there's no going back for more.

**The crossing.** The fleet slips out of Kalaman by night, as the module describes. It was only ever meant to be a short hop across the bay, so every ship carries a few days' rations and the army's real stores ride on one heavy **supply ship**. In the middle of the bay the fleet runs into a fog bank, and out of the fog come Dragon Army ships, black-sailed and waiting **exactly where the fleet would be**. They ignore the troopships and go straight for the supply ship, as if they'd been told which one it was. They had: Takhisis heard every word of Darrett's plan through [[Wurm|a dragonborn PC]] (see [[Wurm (DM)]]). Let the players ask *how the hell did they know?*, and don't answer.

Run the ambush as a chaotic set piece: boarding actions in the fog, fire arrows, dragonnels shrieking overhead, and the supply ship burning to the waterline while the party fights to reach it. When the fog lifts, the supply ship is gone, and the rest of the fleet limps into Wrecker's Edge, holed and half-crewed.

**What survives:**
- The party and **whatever Supply and Water they carry themselves**: everything the factions gave them was in their own packs, minus whatever was lost in the fight (roll **1d4 × 5** Supply and the same of Water lost in the chaos, or more if they fought on the burning supply ship).
- **Darrett and the army**, with a few days' rations between a few hundred soldiers. Darrett stays at Wrecker's Edge with the soldiers and the engineers to **mend the ships**. They can't spare a single ration, and they'll be starving within the week.
- **No way back for more.** The black sails still patrol the bay, and the crippled ships would never survive the crossing unrepaired. Repairs will take weeks, the army will run out of food long before then, and the Dragon Army's ritual night is coming (see "The Deadline" in [[4. Duty]]). The only way through is forward: the party has to find the City of Lost Names before the Night of the Eye, and feed themselves while they do it.

From the moment they land, everything the party eats and drinks is something they carried in, found, or fought for.

## Supply and Water

- **Supply is food.** Each item of food is **one ration**: one day of food for one character, **2 pounds**. Dried fish, hardtack, quith-pa and butchered meat all count. The party's shared pool of rations is simply called **Supply**, but each character carries their own share on their sheet.
- **Water is separate.** **1 Water** is one day of fresh water for one character: a gallon, **8 pounds**. A waterskin holds 4 Water when full, and a cask holds 20.
- Both count toward encumbrance like any other gear. Water is the heavy one, and it's the one that runs out.
- **Pack animals** (a mule or pack sled from the Guilds) carry **300 pounds**, but a mule drinks **2 Water a day** itself.
- Where the party's Supply and Water came from is all in [[Faction Rules]]: the Guilds' stockpile, the Mages' scrolls, the Smallfolk's gifts, the Refugees' dried fish, and anything bought in Kalaman.

### Encumbrance

The campaign uses the **variant encumbrance** rule from the start. It hardly matters in Kalaman, where there's a cart or a Bastion for everything, but out here every ration and every gallon is on someone's back:
- Carrying more than **5 × Strength** in pounds: your speed drops by 10 feet.
- Carrying more than **10 × Strength**: your speed drops by 20 feet, and you have disadvantage on ability checks, attack rolls and saving throws that use Strength, Dexterity or Constitution.

Ten days of water weighs eighty pounds, so somebody is always going to be slow. That's the point.

### Travel Light

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**.

Before the ships leave, every character should **stash everything they don't need in the Bastion**: spare weapons, books, loot, the second set of armour. Out here, every pound of gear is a pound of water they can't carry.

**Make sure an NPC tells them, for a reason that doesn't give anything away.** The fleet's quartermaster, checking the loading at the military docks, says it plainly: the ships are small, every inch of hold is food and water for the troops, and each passenger gets one pack. "What you can carry, you can bring. The rest stays ashore." Cudgel adds her own advice: "Leave the silverware, lad."

A little spare gear can travel with **Darrett**'s baggage. It ends up at the camp at Wrecker's Edge, where the party can get at it whenever they come back to the coast.

**The numbers** (average Strength 13, so 65 lb before Encumbered and 130 lb before Heavily Encumbered, with about 30 lb of gear each):

| | Unencumbered | Encumbered (−10 ft) |
|---|---|---|
| Party carrying capacity for provisions | about 175 lb: **3 days** | about 500 lb: **10 days** |
| If a knight PC keeps his plate (about 80 lb of gear) | about 140 lb: **under 3 days** | about 450 lb: **9 days** |

The party uses **50 lb a day** (five characters, 1 Supply and 1 Water each). The longest stretch between water sources is about **4 days**, so the plan is: **everyone Encumbered, nobody Heavily Encumbered**. Going past 10 × Strength means disadvantage on the Constitution saves against heat and cold, and that's how people die out here.

A **mule** adds about 4 days (it drinks 2 Water a day), and a **pack sled** adds about 6. **Tents** count as shelter but weigh 20 lb per two people, about a day of provisions for the party.

## Exhaustion

Use the 2024 exhaustion rules: each level gives -2 to all d20 Tests and -5 feet of speed, and a character dies at level 6.

In the Wastes, exhaustion is **sticky**:
- A long rest removes **only 1 level of exhaustion**, and only if the character ate and drank that day and rested in shelter.
- Without all three, a long rest removes **no** exhaustion.

## The Daily Routine

Run every day in the Wastes in this order. It takes a minute once everyone knows it.

> [!info] Introduce it in stages
> Day 1: Supply, Water and exhaustion only (steps 5 to 7). Day 2: add navigation and the Wastes Day table. Day 3: add the Wash and foraging. By day 4 it's routine ([[Playtest Ideas#Introduction Schedule]]).

1. **The sky.** Count down one day to the Deadline (see [[4. Duty]]). Roll on the **Wastes Day** table below, and roll the module's **Nature of the Wash** table (d10: 1–3 low, 4–6 high, 7–10 unchanged).
2. **Pace and route.** The party picks a pace: fast (15 miles), normal (12) or slow (9, and they can use Stealth). The Wastes are difficult terrain, so these are already halved.
3. **Navigation.** One character makes a **DC 15 Wisdom (Survival)** check. On a failure, the party is lost. They cover only half the distance, but still eat and drink a full day's worth. Having Clystran as a guide doubles distance and skips this check.
4. **Encounter.** Roll the module's Northern Wastes Encounters table (d20) once a day, but treat **11–12 as no encounter** too: patrols on 13–14, the gate on 15, predators on 16–20. That's an encounter about 2 days in 5, or 10–12 over the whole trek. Most encounters are a **choice**, not a fight (see "Fight or Avoid" below).
5. **Drink.** Each character drinks **1 Water**. A character who can't **gains 1 level of exhaustion** automatically.
6. **Eat.** Each character eats **1 Supply**. A character who can't must succeed on a **DC 10 Constitution** saving throw, or gain 1 level of exhaustion. The DC rises by 2 for each day in a row without food.
7. **Make camp.** A long rest needs **shelter**: a tent, a cave, a ruin or a settlement. Resting in the open, a character must succeed on a **DC 12 Constitution** saving throw or gain 1 level of exhaustion. The DC is 15 during a sandstorm or a high Wash.

## Wastes Day (d8)

| d8 | The day | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Killing heat** | Each character must drink an **extra 1 Water** or make a **DC 13 Constitution** saving throw. On a failure, they gain 1 level of exhaustion. Heavy armour gives disadvantage. Travelling only by night avoids it, but halves the day's distance. |
| 2 | **Bitter cold night** | Each character without a tent or fire must succeed on a **DC 12 Constitution** saving throw or gain 1 level of exhaustion. |
| 3 | **Sandstorm** | Heavily obscured all day. Navigation DC rises to 20. Travel at half distance, or shelter in place and lose the day (still eating and drinking). |
| 4 | **Salt rain** | Stinging brine falls from a clear sky. It can't be drunk, and it fouls **1 Water** per character in any open or leaking container, unless someone thought to cover them. |
| 5–6 | **Merciless sun** | Nothing extra, just a long, hard day. |
| 7 | **Still and grey** | A rare kind day. Navigation and Survival checks are made with advantage. |
| 8 | **Strange light** | The moons are visible in daylight. The Wash will change tomorrow, so roll it twice and take the worse result. [[Davos Bloodbane|a moon-touched PC]] can feel it coming (see below). |

### The Wash

Use the module's rules. When the Wash rises, the party makes a **DC 12 group Wisdom (Survival)** check, or they're caught in a **flash flood** (module chapter 5). In this campaign, the Wash is also **salt water**. Anyone who drinks it must succeed on a **DC 13 Constitution** saving throw or be poisoned for the day, and it doesn't count as Water. Fresh water exists only where noted in the locations of [[4. Duty]].

## Fight or Avoid

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**, on the **Module**'s encounter tables.

Out here, a fight is a trade. It costs **hit points**, which cost **rest**, which costs **days** and **provisions**. But a dead predator is **food**. So when an encounter comes up, tell the players what they see, and let them choose:

| Encounter | Avoid it | Fight it |
|---|---|---|
| **Wastes predators** | DC 13 group Stealth or Survival to slip past. On a success, it costs half a day's travel to go round. On a failure, it's a fight anyway. | A [[One-Turn Resolution\|one-turn]] (or a full fight, if the table wants). The reward is meat: **2d6 Supply** from most predators, **4d6** from a giant shark, **10d6** from a purple worm, plus hides and teeth ([[Harvesting and Crafting]]). Butchering takes an hour or more. |
| **Dragon Army patrol** | DC 13 group Stealth. On a failure, they're spotted, and the patrol attacks or signals. | Their packs carry **1d4 Supply and 1d4 Water** per soldier. A survivor can be questioned. If the party loses, Camp Carrionclay (module). |
| **Malfunctioning gate** | Just walk on. | A clay golem fight, and a teleportation circle afterwards (module). Worth it if the party is far from water. |
| **Bones in the sand** | | Nothing to fight. Sometimes a dead traveller's pack: 1d4 Supply. |

**The economy in one line:** avoiding costs **time**, fighting costs **HP** and pays in **food**. With the deadline ticking and the water running low, both are real choices.

**Keep the combat budget.** Most of these should be one-turns. Save full fights for the ones the players pick, or the ones that matter (a patrol that knows the party's route, thanks to the Leak).

## Finding Food and Water

- **Foraging.** Instead of travelling for a day, the party can forage. Each character makes a **Wisdom (Survival)** check. On a 15 or higher they gain 1 Supply, and on a 20 or higher, 2. When the Wash is high, the DC drops to 13: the canyons fill with fish, crabs and stranded sea creatures, but the water still can't be drunk without a still or magic. Foraging finds food, not Water, and foraging days still cost a day's food and water.
- **Hunting predators.** A slain beast from the encounter table yields rations, which count as **Supply**: 2d6 from a giant scorpion, manticore, spider, octopus, plesiosaurus or dragonnel, 4d6 from a giant shark, and 10d6 from a purple worm. Each also yields hides, teeth and spikes worth crafting ([[Harvesting and Crafting]]). It takes a harvesting check (Survival, DC 10 + CR) and an hour or more. A ranger PC harvests with advantage, and his rite applies.
- ***Goodberry*** heals, but gives no nourishment in this campaign.
- **Fresh water** is the real prize. Every reliable source the party finds should feel like gold. They're marked in the location notes of [[4. Duty]].

## Ways the Party Can Fight Back

Everything here should feel earned in Kalaman or in the Wastes.

| Source | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Gnomish still** (Guilds) | Turns Wash water into **2 Water a day** while the Wash is high. |
| **Cartographers' map** (Guilds) | Advantage on navigation checks. |
| **Mule or pack sled** (Guilds) | Carries 300 pounds, and a mule drinks 2 Water a day. |
| ***Create food and water*** (Mages, or a caster) | Each casting is **15 Supply** and **30 Water**. |
| ***Decanter of endless water*** (Mages) | Water is solved. Only food matters now. |
| **Clystran** (Heart's Hollow) | Doubles travel distance and skips navigation checks. |
| **Tatina's fargabs** (module) | One pair stays with Darrett at Wrecker's Edge. The party can report in, warn the camp, or arrange a rendezvous from anywhere in the Wastes. |
| **A moon-touched PC and the moons** | The Wash follows the three moons. With a **DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana)** check each morning, the moon-touched PC can predict tomorrow's Wash, which gives advantage on the group check to avoid flash floods. |
| **A ranger PC's woodcraft** | The ranger PC gives advantage on foraging checks to anyone who forages with him. |
| **A knight PC's mare** | She smells fresh water within a mile, once per day. |
| **A tinkerer PC's drone** | Its lantern and scouting make one long rest per week count as sheltered, wherever the party is. |

## Tone

Describe the toll. Cracked lips, salt crusted on armour, the sound of the last waterskin sloshing, someone quietly handing their share to someone else. Let the players make the hard choices: who gets the last drink, whether to turn back, whether to drink the Wash water.
