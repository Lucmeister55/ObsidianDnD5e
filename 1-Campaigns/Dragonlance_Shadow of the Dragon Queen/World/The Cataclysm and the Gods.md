# The Cataclysm and the Gods

What people in 351 AC know, believe and get wrong about the Cataclysm, Istar, the gods and dragons. Secrets are in **DM only** boxes.

## What Happened

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[01-war-comes-to-krynn#Before the Cataclysm|"Before the Cataclysm"]], [[01-war-comes-to-krynn#The Cataclysm|"The Cataclysm"]], [[01-war-comes-to-krynn#After the Cataclysm|"After the Cataclysm"]], [[01-war-comes-to-krynn#Religion and the Gods|"Religion and the Gods"]], [[07-chapter-6-city-of-lost-names#Departure of Dragons|ch. 6, "Departure of Dragons"]]).

- **The Third Dragon War.** **Huma Dragonbane** used the first dragonlance to drive **Takhisis** and her evil dragons out of the world. Most metallic dragons then withdrew into hiding, to keep the balance.
- **The Age of Might.** **Istar** built a continental empire, allied with the **Knights of Solamnia**. Its **kingpriests** called themselves the messengers of the gods of good and made war on any people, act or thought they judged evil.
- **The last kingpriest** tried a ritual to become a god. The gods sent the **Thirteen Warnings** (trees weeping blood, unnatural fires, cyclones on the Temple) and warned a few chosen mortals. Istar called it the work of evil.
- **The Cataclysm (year 0).** The gods took their most devout away, and a mountain of fire fell on Istar. The **Blood Sea** swallowed the empire, and coastlines moved everywhere.
- **The gods withdrew.** **True clerics vanished**, and in time even the gods' names were nearly forgotten.
- **The Time of Darkness.** Famine, plague and migration. The Knights were persecuted as Istar's allies, the elves shut their borders, Thorbardin sealed its gates, and false religions spread, like the **Seekers**, who rule Haven for vague gods who condemn magic.
- **Not wholly gone.** Small miracles still happen, druids pray in the old ways, and "divine whispers reach those with the minds and hearts to listen".

> [!info] Source: **Canon**, from the novels and older books, partly from memory (the fan wikis block automated reading). Check before relying on it: [Beldinas Pilofiro](https://dragonlance.fandom.com/wiki/Beldinas_Pilofiro), [Lost Battles](https://dragonlance.fandom.com/wiki/Lost_Battles), [Knights of the Divine Hammer](https://dlsaga.com/knights-of-the-divine-hammer/).
> - The last kingpriest is **Beldinas Pilofiro**, who rejected the Doctrine of Balance.
> - In the **Lost Battles** (about 19 PC), the Mages of High Sorcery fought Istar, Solamnia and Ergoth, and nobody won. Istar's **Knights of the Divine Hammer** mostly died in it.
> - The moons stayed, and wizards kept their magic. Only clerics lost theirs.

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[02-chapter-1-character-creation#Towers of High Sorcery|ch. 1, "Towers of High Sorcery"]]).
> Of five Towers of High Sorcery, three were destroyed in the Cataclysm and Palanthas's lies cursed and empty. Only [[Tower of Wayreth]] still works.

## Who Knows What

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**, sorted from the canon above and the module's rumours ([[01-war-comes-to-krynn#Kalaman Region|"Kalaman Region"]]).

**Everyone.** The Cataclysm was 351 years ago (hence AC). Fire fell on Istar for its pride, and the gods turned their backs. There are no real priests, only frauds. The Knights were Istar's allies and could have stopped it, people say. Dragons belong in Huma's songs and children's stories.

**Faith follows hardship.** The poor pray anyway, because they need something to hold on to. The richer and better schooled, the less they believe, and the more they use belief when it suits them.
- **Still pious** (most smallfolk and refugees): prayers to half-remembered names, roadside shrines, charms, festivals. "The gods left" and "the gods are listening" sit in the same head.
- **They left us.** Said bitterly, often by the same people who pray.
- **They cursed everyone.** Bad storms and harvests are "the old gods' disfavour".
- **They're dead, or never existed.** Rare among the poor, common among the educated.
- **New gods** from preachers and cults, feeding on the desperate, like the market preacher of the "Queen of Many Colours" ([[3.06 Downtime 3]]).

**Vogler** keeps the festivals without the faith. Few know Habbakuk, the "Fisher King", was a god. They rub the wharf post for luck, live beside a bridge nobody alive could build, and distrust the Knights ([[Vogler]]).

**Kalaman.**
- **The council and guilds** are mostly agnostic or quietly atheist. The city came through the Cataclysm almost untouched, so the gods are other people's bad luck, and Istar is a rival they beat ([[Kalaman]]). They're pious in public when it pays.
- **The smallfolk** are devout and superstitious. The Dread Knight is a nursery tale they half believe, and much of why they distrust the Order.
- **The military** believes what it can count. Vendri believes in the Dragon Army because her scouts do.

**The council's line.** Panic is bad for business, so early on the council tells the city there's nothing to fear:
- "There is no Dragon Queen. The gods took her with them."
- "These 'draconians' are eastern raiders in masks and scale, or men twisted by some Estwilde sickness."
- "Kalaman's walls have never been breached. Istar built them."
- "Dragons are a sailors' tale. They saw dragonnels."

Most of the council believes it, because believing it is cheaper. It's why they doubt the threat at the first meeting, want the Maelgoth scroll kept quiet ([[3.08 Downtime 4]]) and let Steel Springs happen. It also helps Takhisis: a city told there's nothing to fear doesn't prepare. Let the line crack in the briefings: first the smallfolk stop believing it, then the soldiers, then the council.

**The Knights** swear by **Paladine**, **Kiri-Jolith** and **Habbakuk**, though none has worked a miracle since the Cataclysm ([[02-chapter-1-character-creation#Knights of Solamnia|ch. 1]]).
- Huma is history to them. Nobody alive has seen a dragonlance.
- They carry the blame for Istar. Becklin's answer: the Knights were only ever charged with holding what could still be held ([[1.01 Introduction]]).
- They're split over keeping the Measure to the letter or bending it.
- Their tombs hold records nobody reads (the catacombs, [[3.13 The Raided Catacombs]]).

**The Mages** know the most and say the least.
- Their magic and their gods (**Solinari**, **Lunitari**, **Nuitari**) never left, so they're the one group with reason to think the gods aren't dead.
- They've never forgiven Istar's war on magic, or the lost Towers.
- They know more about Istar's end and **Onyari** than they admit ([[Mages of High Sorcery]]).
- They don't know where the gods of good went, and argue about it.

**Elves.** Some Silvanesti lived through the Cataclysm, so Istar is a memory and the gods have names. They don't tell humans. Dwarves remember Thorbardin's sealed gates better than why.

> [!warning] DM only: the Dragon Army
> The only power with a god who answers. Takhisis returned first, unknown to the other gods, through a piece of the Temple of the Kingpriest planted at **Neraka** ([[01-war-comes-to-krynn#The Dragon Queen's Return|"The Dragon Queen's Return"]]). Her clerics ([[Kansaldi Fire-Eyes]], [[Belephaion]]) work real miracles, and to frightened people that's the best argument there is.

## The Dragon Queen's Story

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. **DM only.**

Her propaganda works because most of it is true: the gods of good did burn Istar, take their faithful and leave. She only adds the ending.

**What her preachers say:**
- The gods burned Istar for loving them too much, then left and never answered a prayer again. What kind of god does that?
- Maybe there never were gods of good. Maybe the kingpriests and the Knights made them up.
- One goddess never left. Her priests heal, and her soldiers don't starve.
- She's kind to those who kneel early.

**In Kalaman, she preaches liberation** to the hungry: the doubled camps, the guild prices, the bread fights at the Trade Gate ([[3.02 Downtime 1]], [[3.04 Downtime 2]], [[3.10 Downtime 5]]).
- The guilds grow fat and lock the granaries while you starve.
- The old gods were the gods of lords. They never fed a fisherman.
- The Queen comes to free Kalaman from the guild lords, by force if she must.
- Her priests heal in the camps for free. The guild physicians charge a week's wages.

The healing is the dangerous part, because **it's true**: since the Cataclysm, only her clerics have worked divine magic. One healing in the fever camps ([[3.08 Downtime 4]]) beats a hundred sermons.

**What she wants** isn't a revolt, it's a divided city: smallfolk against guilds, locals against refugees, everyone against the Knights. Every riot is a week the walls go unmended. The council's "nothing to fear" and her "she's coming to free you" feed each other.

**How it spreads**, always as someone else's question:
- **Preachers** in markets and camps ([[3.06 Downtime 3]]). Most believe it. A few are paid.
- **Refugees and deserters** who've seen her clerics heal. Some are sivaks.
- **Fanatics** like Svilnt ([[2.01 Back in Vogler]]), and [[Kansaldi Fire-Eyes|Kansaldi]]'s sermon at Vogler's bridge, meant to be carried downriver.
- **Bread** in occupied villages for anyone in her colours.
- **Silence**: every unanswered prayer argues for her.

**Who listens:** the hungry, the grieving, the refugees, and anyone who's given up on the Knights. Let it rise slowly in the briefings' news as the city suffers.

**The double game.** To crowds, the old gods were never real. To Wurm, she wears Paladine's face ([[Wurm (DM)]]). When the party works out who speaks to Wurm, they may work out who writes the sermons.

**Rumours** (d6 on the road, d8 in Kalaman once the camps go hungry):
1. "A Dragon Army priest healed a farmer's boy outside Vogler. Just touched him."
2. "The Knights knew the fire was coming. They let Istar burn so they could have Solamnia."
3. "My gran prayed to Mishakal every night for forty years. Look where it got her."
4. "The old gods were Istar's gods. Look what happened to Istar."
5. "They say she only wants the ones who kneel. Everyone else gets left alone."
6. "Whatever you think of her, she's the only one who's ever answered."
7. "The guilds have three years of grain under the Trade Hall. The Queen's people say they'll open it."
8. "A woman in red healed the Tamsin girl's fever last night. Didn't even ask for a copper."

## Istar: What's Remembered

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew** sorting of **Module** and **Canon** facts (above, plus [[07-chapter-6-city-of-lost-names#Built on Bones|ch. 6]] and [[05-chapter-4-shadow-of-war|ch. 4]] for Onyari and Sarlamir).

| What | Who knows it |
|---|---|
| Istar was the greatest empire ever, destroyed by fire for its pride. The Blood Sea lies where it stood. | **Everyone** |
| Istar built what nobody can now: Kalaman's walls and beacons, Vogler's bridge, the Wastes' teleport gates. | **Everyone**. **How** is **lost**. |
| A kingpriest's pride brought the fire. | **Everyone**, as a moral tale. His **name** and **decrees**: **scholars**. |
| He tried to make himself a god. | **Scholars and mages.** |
| The Thirteen Warnings. | **Legend**, as folk tales and omens. **Scholars** know the list. |
| The gods warned chosen mortals, who failed. | **Legend**. In Nightlund, the Dread Knight, whose **name is forgotten** until the catacombs ([[Ser Edric of Brackenridge (DM)]]). |
| The Knights were Istar's allies. | **Everyone**. "They could have stopped it" is a distortion. |
| Istar's war on magic and on "evil" peoples. | **Mages** and **elves**. Ordinary humans **don't know**. |
| Onyari, the flying City Without Sin, built on a dragons' graveyard. | **Lost.** The kingpriest erased its records. The few who remember call it the **City of Lost Names**. [[Demelin]] lived there. Old Silvanesti remember it as a song ([[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn) (DM)#What Lorac Told Him\|Thorn]]). |
| Sarlamir's quest, the dragonlance, Karavarix's death. | **Lost**, except in Sarlamir's tomb under Castle Kalaman. |
| Kalaman's old money came from Istarian wrecks and Onyari pieces washed out of the Wastes. | **Everyone** has heard the slander. The **oldest guild families** and [[Wyhan]] know it's true ([[Kalaman Guilds#Old Money]]). Nobody knows about Onyari. |
| The Temple fragment at Neraka. | **DM only.** |
| Where the gods went, and whether they'll return. | **Nobody.** |

> [!warning] DM only: this campaign
> The gods are closer than anyone thinks. Takhisis speaks through Wurm in Paladine's mask, Paladine answers with silver signs and a befuddled old man ([[Wurm (DM)]]), and the gods of good nudge the others deniably ([[DM Guide]]). Whoever finally says the gods are back should be someone the players trust.
