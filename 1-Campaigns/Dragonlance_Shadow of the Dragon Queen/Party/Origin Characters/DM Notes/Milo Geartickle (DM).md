## The Truth

What Milo doesn't know.

- **The ring was forged in Onyari.** Before the Cataclysm, the Kingpriest of Istar built Onyari, the flying City Without Sin, where his worthiest subjects would "cast off mortal weakness and live like the gods". It is now the module's City of Lost Names (chapter 6). A Black Robe in the Kingpriest's service forged a handful of rings for his chosen to cheat death. Fistandantilus, who lived in Istar in that era, is the obvious candidate, or you can leave the maker nameless. This one was smuggled out of Onyari before the Cataclysm and ended up sealed in the Estwilde strongroom where Milo found it. Affects [[Milo Geartickle#^flaw-death|treating death as an inconvenience]].
- **A life for a life.** The ring doesn't heal Milo. It takes life and pours it into them. Small wounds cost something small, like a patch of grass that browns or a beetle that stops moving. A death costs a death. Milo hasn't noticed, because kender rarely connect what they do with what follows. See "The Death Tracker" below.
- **Why it isn't random.** The Kingpriest's chosen were meant to live forever on the lives of those devoted to them: their servants, their followers, the people who loved them. So the ring always draws from **whoever is most bound to Milo**. For years that was no one. Milo was a wandering kender with no attachments, so it made do with whatever was nearest: rats, birds, a stray dog that had followed them for a day. Now Milo has a party, and friends, and people who care about them. Every bond Milo makes is more fuel. The more Milo comes to love the people around them, the more the ring can take, and the more it will. When it finally takes a person, it takes the one who matters most to Milo.
- **The drone is an Istarian drone** (stat block in the module's appendix B). It was built in Onyari as a warden to track the rings down and bring them home. It found Milo in the Wastes because of the ring, and the "checking they're all still there" by Milo's right hand is it checking on the ring. It isn't following Milo out of fondness. It's been trying to herd the ring back to Onyari all along, and its patterns of light, which no inventor can read, are directions. Affects [[Milo Geartickle#^bond-drone|the drone]].
- **The bronze dragon** Milo saw in the Wastes was real. The metallic dragons are returning in the module, and there's a bronze dragon egg at Camp Carrionclay. What it was doing there is left open. Optionally, the module's dragon-obsessed scout **Clystran** saw it too, and can turn up in the Wastes to back Milo's story at last.
- **The lost months in the Wastes.** Milo really did live among people in the Northern Wastes, and learned forgework from one of them (the "very large, patient hands"). Why they remember so little is left open: the cold, the ring, or something those people did to protect themselves. When the party reaches the Wastes in the module's chapter 5 and Milo meets those people again, the memories come back in a rush. Someone there knows Milo by name, and that's a lovely moment to play. This is also the natural place to reveal who they promised never to return alone.
- **Who they promised** never to enter the Wastes alone is left open (Elgo is the natural choice). Affects [[Milo Geartickle#^bond-wastes|the Wastes]].

## Hooks

- **Why Milo fights the Dragon Queen.** Milo doesn't care about kingdoms or gods, and their reasons can shift as the campaign goes on.
  1. **The relics (from the start).** The Dragonarmies are ransacking every pre-Cataclysm ruin they can find, smashing what they can't use and turning the rest into weapons. Module patrols in the Wastes are doing exactly that. To a self-appointed rescuer of relics, that's the worst crime imaginable. Give Milo one early, vivid example: a draconian melting down something beautiful for its metal.
  2. **The chronicle and the perks (early on).** Milo wants a front-row seat to write the heroes' story. Working for Kalaman's military also comes with official papers, a uniform and the authority of the Knights. Suddenly locked archives, guarded storerooms and nobles' private collections open their doors to Milo on request, which makes "borrowing" much easier. Expect them to abuse this cheerfully, and let it cause trouble with the factions.
  3. **The people (mid-campaign).** Milo doesn't make friends easily, being a wanderer. Once the party and the people of Kalaman are *theirs*, Milo will fight for them with everything they've got. That's exactly what makes the ring's price so cruel.
  4. **Onyari (late).** The Dragon Army wants the City of Lost Names, the one place where the ring can be unmade, and the drone's home. If they raise it, Milo loses any chance of fixing what the ring has done.
  5. **Optionally, home.** Kendermore is still under Black Dragon Army occupation, and Elgo is still fighting there. Word that he's been captured, or worse, would give the kender a rare, very personal anger.

- **Joining the party.** They're caught trying to study, sketch and "borrow" Ispin's shield at the funeral. Once confronted, they relent at once, more bewildered than ashamed, and explain quite sincerely that they only wanted to look, measure and record it. Why *this* party:
- **They didn't hand Milo over.** Whoever catches them, ideally Edric, Ispin's own squire, chooses to let it go, and Milo has never been let off by someone with that much right to be angry.
- **They're the best story Milo has ever stumbled into.** At the funeral it's a grieving knight and a sorcerer whose hair changes colour, which is already plenty. Then High Hill adds a dragonborn who came back from the dead and a mutilated elf who walked out of the woods killing draconians, and after that no kender alive could walk away.
- **The drone likes them.** It lights up in a new pattern the first time it sees them, and tinkering later can reveal why: it reacts to the strange magic around Davos and Wurm.

So Milo decides to tag along and chronicle their deeds, whether anyone invited them or not. Plays on [[Milo Geartickle#^bond-shield|the shield]] and [[Milo Geartickle#^ideal-story|Story]].
- **Scout and liability.** Their kender nature makes them a useful scout and a source of unexpected information, even when they're a liability. Plays on [[Milo Geartickle#^flaw-wandering|wandering off]] and [[Milo Geartickle#^flaw-touching|touching things]].
- **In-world chronicler.** Their habit of recording events gives the party a chronicler for the journey. Plays on [[Milo Geartickle#^bond-stories|stories]].
- **The Northern Wastes.** They want to go there, but promised someone they'd never go alone. The campaign's later trek into the Wastes gives them a safe chance to do it. Plays on [[Milo Geartickle#^bond-wastes|the Wastes]] and [[Milo Geartickle#^flaw-promises|promises]].
- **Relics and ruins.** Their curiosity will keep pulling them toward relics, ruins and secrets tied to Krynn's larger history. Plays on [[Milo Geartickle#^ideal-curiosity|Curiosity]].
- **Noticing the cost.** Let the party, not Milo, spot the pattern first: the dead bird under the tree where Milo got up, the horse found dead in a stable the morning after. Kender are bad at connecting cause and effect, so Milo may be the last to understand, or may refuse to. Plays on [[Milo Geartickle#^flaw-death|treating death as an inconvenience]] and [[Milo Geartickle#^flaw-borrowing|"borrowing"]].
- **Onyari.** When the party reaches the City of Lost Names, the drone suddenly knows exactly where it's going and leads Milo toward wherever the rings were made. The module's ghostly high wizard **Demelin**, a former master of the Tower at Istar, recognises the ring at a glance. She can tell them it can only be unmade in Onyari, where it was forged, and that unmaking it releases every life it ever took. That debt has to land somewhere: either all of Milo's borrowed deaths finally catch up with them, or someone else pays. Demelin can also give the Test of High Sorcery, which puts Milo and [[Davos Bloodbane]] at the same crossroads. Plays on [[Milo Geartickle#^bond-drone|the drone]] and [[Milo Geartickle#^ideal-freedom|Freedom]].

## Drone Tinkering

Each downtime workweek Milo spends on the drone, they make an Intelligence (tinker's tools) check:
- **DC 15:** 1 progress.
- **DC 20:** 2 progress.
- **Below 10:** something goes wrong. It sulks and won't light up for a day, or it shorts out and zaps Milo.

The Kalaman downtime in [[3.00 Storm Clouds]] runs from weeks to months, so that's when most of this happens. Each threshold unlocks one upgrade and one revelation. Give out the upgrades freely. The revelations are how Milo learns the truth, one piece at a time.

| Progress | Upgrade (tell the player) | Revelation (what Milo discovers) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **Lantern.** Sheds bright light in a 20-foot radius, or dim light, or none, on Milo's command. | Its light patterns aren't random. They repeat in a fixed cycle, like writing. |
| 4 | **Second hands.** It can take the Help action on Milo's ability checks using thieves' or tinker's tools. | A tiny sigil is etched deep inside its casing. Anyone who knows old Solamnic lore, which Milo does, recognises the crest of the Kingpriest of Istar. |
| 6 | **Scout.** As an action, Milo can see and hear through the drone until the end of their next turn. | Its patterns spell a single word in old Istarian, over and over: *home*. And whenever it's free to move, it drifts north. |
| 8 | **Recorder.** It can replay any sound it has heard in the last day. | It keeps a count. Every time the ring has revived Milo, the drone has logged it, along with a flicker of whatever died nearby. Milo can play the record back if they dare. *This is the moment they learn the cost, if the party hasn't already worked it out.* |
| 10 | **Key.** It reacts to Istarian magic, glowing near it, and can open sealed Istarian locks and doors. | A projected image, flickering and incomplete, of a city floating above the clouds. It's taking the ring back there. |

**The drone can't lead them to the City of Lost Names on its own.** Its memory of the way is too damaged, and finding the city is the whole of the module's chapter 5. All tinkering tells Milo is that home is north, in the Wastes. Once the party finds the passage into the city, the drone's guidance snaps into focus, and from then on it knows exactly where it's going. See the **Onyari** hook above.

## The Death Tracker

Every time the ring brings Milo back from 0 hit points, log it here. Each revival takes a life from whatever Milo is most bound to at the time. Escalate it as Milo's attachments grow:
1. Strays and small creatures, while Milo has no one.
2. Animals the party has grown fond of, like a mount, a pet or the drone's favourite perch-bird.
3. People Milo has befriended on the road, such as a villager, a soldier or a fellow kender.
4. Finally, someone who truly matters. Pick an NPC the whole table loves, or someone tied to Milo's past like Elgo or Tatina Rookledust. Never a player character unless that player agrees.

**The log so far:**
- **High Hill** (prologue): beheaded by a mercenary's axe, and grows a new head on their next turn. The price: a kingfisher, dead out of the tree above.

Don't hurry it. The person should die only after Milo, or the party, has been warned enough to see it coming. The whole log is the bill that comes due in Onyari.

## Level-Up Moments

The gods are almost entirely absent, so any divine touch has to be deniable: a coincidence, a sound, a spark, something mechanical behaving oddly. Never say a god did it. Milo's patron is **Reorx**, the forge god, whose lost Graygem kender folk say gave rise to their people. So Milo's moments come through metal, forges, the drone, and a faint grey gleam. The ring is a separate, cursed thing: keep its moments clearly creepier than Reorx's. *Divine* moments are the gods nudging; *training* and *earned* moments are just Milo getting better.

- **Level 4: the flotilla downriver** *(earned)*. Milo writes the first page of the party's chronicle by lantern light. Ask the player: what's the title?
- **Level 5: after the Rookledust rescue** *(training)*. Tatina Rookledust, Milo's old teacher, drags them back into her ruined workshop for a week of repairs, arguments and near-explosions. They come out knowing more than they went in.
- **Level 6: after the catacombs** *(the ring)*. Among the dead, the ring goes cold and tightens until it hurts, and Milo feels something they've never felt before: they want to leave. Ask: do they listen to it? Whatever they choose, the ring loosens afterwards, as if pleased.
- **Level 7: the Northern Wastes** *(divine)*. If the party rescues the bronze dragon egg from Camp Carrionclay, Milo carries it. It's warm, and it ticks faintly, in time with the drone. Milo is suddenly certain the dragon they saw years ago was real.
- **Level 8: the passage to the City of Lost Names** *(divine)*. An ancient anvil sits in the passage, cold for centuries. Milo hits it, because of course they do. It rings and keeps ringing far longer than it should, and the sparks come off grey.
- **Level 9: reforging the dragonlance** *(divine)*. Milo's hands are steady as they help with the work. When it's done, for the first time in their life, they hold something wonderful and don't want to keep it.
- **Level 10: escaping the Threshold** *(earned)*. The way out is a maze, and Milo walks it from memory, every turn, out loud. Afterwards they close the chronicle on its first full volume. If they've unmade the ring in Onyari by now, this is the first chapter they write as a mortal.
