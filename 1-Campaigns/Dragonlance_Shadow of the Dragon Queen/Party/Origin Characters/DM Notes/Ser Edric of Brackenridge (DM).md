## The Truth

What Edric doesn't know.

- **Ispin was murdered.** No beast from the Wastes killed him. **Red Ruin** did, by way of her dragonnel mount, on Soth's word (see below). The wounds were too long and too targeted for a wild animal, which Becklin suspects. Affects [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^bond-ispin|Ispin]].
- **Soth is a nameless legend.** To the world there's only a whispered legend of **the Dread Knight**, a shadow over the old castle of Dargaard in Nightlund, said to be cursed by some ancient evil no one can name, which some say was once a Knight of Solamnia. Nobles bring it up to needle knights. His name and even what he is have been forgotten, and almost nobody believes there's anything there at all, the Order included. Keep the name out of every player-facing text until the catacombs beneath Castle Kalaman. There, the name comes back out of the dark: carved on a forgotten tomb or in the records of the knights buried there, or spoken aloud by the module's skeletal knight Zanas **Sarlamir**, one of the Death Knight's own fallen knights, who still names his lord with pride. "Lord Loren Soth" should land like a story the world forgot it was telling, retold at last.
- **The Greenshield line.** On the night Soth turned his back on the gods and let the Cataclysm come, one knight of his household refused to follow him. That knight was Ispin's ancestor.

As the fire took Dargaard and the people of the valley came to its gates begging for shelter, Soth ordered his knights to bar them out. Ispin's ancestor opened the gates instead. Then he stood in the burning gateway with his shield raised, between the flames and the fleeing crowd, and held it there with his shield and his own body until the last of them was through. He died in that gateway. When the survivors came back at dawn, they found his body burned black, and the shield on his arm without a mark on it. Among the people he had saved was his own young son, who took up the shield and the name Greenshield, and the family has carried both ever since. Soth never forgave it.

His sacrifice became the family's creed: an order is only worth obeying if it serves the people it's meant to protect. Every Greenshield since has been a little bit of a maverick, and Ispin most of all. His hedge-knight ways, his contempt for ceremony and his habit of ignoring orders he thought were wrong were never just eccentricity. They were his inheritance. Ispin was the last of the line. He knew the family story, and he hated talking about it, which is why he went quiet whenever anyone mentioned Dargaard. Becklin is the only one he ever told, which is why she knows "the truth" behind the tale the Bakarises mock (see [[1.00 An Ordinary Day]]).
- **The black rose riders.** Shortly before Ispin left for Vogler, five skeletal riders bearing the **black rose**, the badge of Soth's knights, ambushed him and Edric on the road outside Kalaman. It was Soth's first move against the last Greenshield, and a test. Ispin won, and recognised the badge at once from his family's story. As usual, he told Edric nothing.
- **Why Ispin went to Vogler.** The ambush, together with rumours of war abroad (the Dragonarmies' conquests in the east were reaching even Kalaman), convinced him the old family legend was waking up and might be tied to the coming war. He went to Vogler to consult **Becklin**, the one person he'd ever trusted with the Greenshield story, and to find out what was stirring in Nightlund. He didn't take Edric because he didn't want his squire anywhere near it, after seeing how close those riders came. Edric only knows he went to "look into something". Becklin knows a good deal more, if anyone asks her the right questions.
- **Soth orchestrated his death.** Soth had two reasons. First, he wanted the last Greenshield dead: three and a half centuries of grudge, and a loose end from the night he fell. Second, he wanted the **shield** destroyed. He gave the word through his new allies in the Dragon Army, and Red Ruin carried it out on her dragonnel. Then Gragonis took the shield from the casket in secret, to be sent on to Dargaard (see [[1.02 Arrival in Vogler#The Missing Child and the Stolen Shield]]).
- **Why the shield matters to him: pride.** Soth doesn't care much who learns the truth. What he can't stand is the shield itself. He demanded unquestioning loyalty from his knights, and on the night he betrayed the gods, one of them betrayed *him*, opened his gates to the people he'd shut out, and died holding the fire back from them with that shield. For three and a half centuries it has been a living symbol of defiance against him, carried openly across Solamnia by a family that boasts of saying no to its lord. Every year it survives is an insult. He wants it ended.
- **What the shield really is.** It was blessed by the true gods before Soth's fall, and on the night the first Greenshield died behind it, something of that knight's spirit stayed in it. Every Greenshield since who died with the shield on his arm has stayed too, and now Ispin is in it as well. His habit of talking to his shield was never one-sided, and his spirit, faint and far away, is still there.
- **Why it's so hard to destroy.** It held back the fires of the Cataclysm, the same flames that consumed Dargaard and damned Soth, and was found unburnt on its bearer's arm. Since that night no fire, hammer or curse has marked it, and the blessing burns undead hands, so neither Soth nor his knights can even hold it. That galls him more than anything. So he wants it carried by living hands to Dargaard, where he can keep trying to break it, and where, if he can't, at least no Greenshield or anyone else will ever carry it again.
- **Soth's mockery: Ispin's body.** The funeral boat that carries Ispin's body down the Vingaard (see the module's funeral scene) never reaches the sea. Soth's servants take it from the river downstream, and Soth raises it as a **death knight** of his retinue, wearing the black rose. At around 6th level, use the [[death-knight-aspirant-xmm|death knight aspirant]] stat block. The full [[death-knight-xmm|death knight]] is too strong that early. It's the cruellest joke he knows: the last Greenshield "finally" kneeling to the lord his family defied. But it's only the body. Ispin's spirit is safe in the shield, so the thing Soth raised is hollow, an empty suit of Ispin wearing his face, with none of his humour or his kindness.
  - **The reveal.** In the catacombs beneath Kalaman, the Death Knight's retinue includes a knight whose helm comes off to show Ispin's face. Have Soth introduce it with relish.
  - **The shield knows.** When it gets close, the shield goes cold for the first time. Then it burns hot, and Edric hears, clearly for the first time, Ispin's voice: "That isn't me, lad." That's the shield's first real stirring. Its full power waits for the Temple of Paladine (see "Ispin's Shield").
  - **Laying him to rest.** Destroying the hollow knight is something only Edric should do, and it's the most painful thing he'll ever do as a knight. The shield's blessing could be what finally lets the body fall still, Ispin protecting his squire one last time.
- **Soth's lies.** Soth won't deny the shield, but he'll lie about it. To Edric, he'll say the first Greenshield was a coward who flung open the gates in a panic, let a mob into the keep, and died for nothing, that the family's famous defiance was nothing but treachery dressed up as virtue, and that Ispin, a laughing fool the Order rightly ignored, was cut from the same cloth. He's very convincing, and it lands hardest on a knight who already doubts whether rules or Ispin were right.
- **Red Ruin was only the dagger.** In this campaign Red Ruin is a child of the Dragon Army, taken young and shaped into a weapon who has never been allowed to be anything else. She killed Ispin because she was told to.
  - **Soth pins it on her.** He isn't hiding out of fear. Blaming Red Ruin and denying any part in it is simply more amusing, a way to toy with Ispin's squire and watch what he does with his anger. Every clue leads to Red Ruin and stops there: the dragonnel the party meets in the woods, the belt clasp they find beside it (see [[1.02 Arrival in Vogler#The Missing Child and the Stolen Shield]]), the wounds Becklin noticed. In the catacombs, Soth tells Edric himself that "the Red Ruin's beast" killed his master, as if he were doing him a kindness. For a long time, that's the only answer Edric has.
  - **Reveal it in stages.** First Edric learns Red Ruin killed Ispin, and has someone to hate. Much later, given all the facts, or once Ispin's voice in the shield can finally tell him, he learns who gave the order. Last, he learns why, and that Ispin died for something an ancestor did right three hundred and fifty years ago.
  - **The conflict with Wurm.** Red Ruin is a child raised by the Dragon Army into a weapon who knows nothing but obedience, which is Wurm's own story. Wurm will see himself in her. While Edric wants her dead, Wurm wants to understand her, or spare her, or free her, and for a long time neither of them knows that the real enemy is the one who set them against each other. See [[Wurm (DM)]].
  - **The test.** Whether Edric can see Red Ruin as a victim, the way Ispin once saw a frightened twelve-year-old in a line of captives, may be the truest test of what kind of knight he is.

## Hooks

- **A council of knights.** Plays on [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^bond-order|the Order]] and [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^flaw-doubt|his doubt]]. If the chapterhouse hears about the desertion charge, its knight-commander may want to judge Edric's honour himself, as the Measure says he must ([[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#The Oath and the Measure]]). It's a trial inside the company's trial, and a chance for the Knights' Standing to rise or fall on him alone. Clearing him is worth **+1 Knights**. Being judged against costs **-2**, and the Order asks for his spurs back.
- **Why Edric fights the Dragon Queen.** His reasons deepen as the campaign goes on.
  1. **Duty (from the start).** He's a Knight of the Crown, and the Order is going to war. It's what he swore to do, and what Ispin would have done.
  2. **Vogler (Broken Silence).** He watches Ispin's home village burn and leads its people downriver. After that it's no longer an abstract war. These are people he failed to protect once, and won't fail again.
  3. **Ispin (when the truth comes out).** Learning that Red Ruin murdered Ispin turns duty into something personal and much harder to control. Learning that Soth gave the order turns it into something else again.
  4. **Command (late).** As he rises through the ranks, he's fighting for the soldiers under him, which is his parents' story all over again, and his chance to finally hold the line.

- **Soth and Greenshield's squire.** In the catacombs, the legend turns out to be real. Soth looks past everyone else to Edric, recognises Ispin in the way he fights, and speaks to him as "Greenshield's boy". He knows exactly who Edric is. Grow it over the campaign:
  - **The heir.** Ispin had no children. Edric is the closest thing to an heir the Greenshield line has left, and as far as Soth is concerned, the grudge didn't die with Ispin.
  - **The mirror.** Soth was once the perfect knight Edric is trying to become, who lived by the Measure and fell anyway. He wants to see whether Ispin's squire falls too, and proving him wrong is the other half of the Greenshield story.
  - **The question.** Soth asks him what is worth saving, the line from the intro, and whether the Measure can tell him. It's the same choice Edric's arc is built on, rules or Ispin.
  - **The shield.** When the party defeats Gragonis, Edric gets Ispin's shield back, before it ever reaches Dargaard. From then on he's carrying the one thing Soth most wants destroyed, and Soth will keep sending things to take it. Let the shield wake slowly:
    - At first it's only warm against his arm, or feels a little lighter in a fight.
    - Then come moments when Edric is sure he heard someone humming badly off-key, the way Ispin used to.
    - Then a word, or a warning, once, at the worst possible moment.
    - Much later, if Edric learns to listen, Ispin can tell him the truth Soth buried: who really gave the order.

    That makes the shield how the final reveal arrives. It also gives Edric the means to stand against Soth, if the blessing of the Greenshields is real. Getting it back is the first real thing Edric does for Ispin.
  Plays on [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^bond-ispin|Ispin]], [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^bond-order|the Order]] and [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^flaw-doubt|self-doubt]].
- **Belonging in the Order.** His first major arc is proving he belongs, through hard choices under pressure rather than words. Plays on [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^flaw-doubt|self-doubt]] and [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^bond-order|the Order]].
- **The truth about Ispin.** Learning what really happened turns his story from grief into a much larger confrontation. Plays on [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^bond-ispin|Ispin]] and [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^flaw-grief|grief]].
- **The protector he failed to be.** His strongest drive is becoming the protector he couldn't be for his parents, which makes him both reliable and vulnerable. Plays on [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^bond-parents|his parents]] and [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^flaw-guilt|guilt]].
- **Holding the group together.** His role as the party's steadying figure gets tested whenever the party fractures, panics or faces moral compromise. Plays on [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^ideal-loyalty|Loyalty]] and [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge#^flaw-overprotective|overprotectiveness]].

## Ispin's Shield

Give this to Edric when the party defeats Gragonis. (This replaces the module's scene where Becklin hands the shield over at breakfast. Here it was stolen from the casket instead.)

**Description.** A round shield of old, close-grained wood bound in dark iron, painted a deep mossy green that has never faded or chipped, though no one can remember it ever being repainted. Carved across its face is a broadleaf tree with a long scar down its trunk, a tree that has been struck and kept growing. Ispin called the shield **Old Moss** and talked to it constantly. Turned over, the inside of the rim is cut with a ring of small, worn names, one for every Greenshield who carried it, the oldest almost too faint to read. There's one space left after the last. Ispin's name isn't there yet. If Edric ever carves it, that's a good moment for the shield to wake.

**The cover story.** Ispin told everyone, and the module's characters all remember, that a unicorn gave him the shield in Darken Wood. It's the best of his tall tales, and he told it on purpose, so that nobody would ever ask where the shield really came from.
### Stage 1: Old Moss (from Gragonis)

*Shield, uncommon*

When Edric first gets it, it's exactly what the module says it is, a [[1-shield-xdmg|+1 shield]]. The blessing of the true gods has dimmed in the centuries since they withdrew, and what's left is barely more than stubbornness.
- **+1 Shield.** You gain a +1 bonus to AC while wielding this shield, in addition to the shield's normal bonus.
- **Unburnt.** The shield itself can't be damaged by fire, and undead creatures can't bear to hold it. This is a story detail more than a mechanic, and it's why Soth needs living hands.
- **The Voices.** Nothing yet. You decide when the first stirrings come (warmth, off-key humming). The catacombs and the hollow Ispin are the natural moment.

### Stage 2: Restored (at the Temple of Paladine)

*Shield, very rare (requires attunement by a creature who has sworn a knightly oath)*

When the party reforges the dragonlance at the Temple of Paladine in the City of Lost Names, the old blessing that was laid on the shield before the Cataclysm comes back to it too, as the gods' touch returns to the world. Light runs through the carved tree, the green deepens, and for a moment every name around the rim shines. Ispin's too, if Edric has carved it. From then on it becomes a [[shield-of-the-cavalier-xdmg|Shield of the Cavalier]], with one additional property:
- **Through the Flames.** While you wield the shield, you have resistance to fire damage, and you can't be harmed by the fire of the Cataclysm or any fire like it. That includes Dargaard's undying flames and dragonfire of the gods' own wrath. Exactly what counts is up to the DM.

The Voices stay a story element. Ispin and the Greenshield line speak through the shield when the story needs them to, not as a mechanic.

## Downtime: Raising a Company

Kalaman is full of frightened refugees and green recruits, and Edric can't walk past them. For each workweek he spends drilling them, he makes a Charisma (Persuasion) or Wisdom (Insight) check, his choice:
- **DC 15:** 1 progress.
- **DC 20:** 2 progress.
- **Below 10:** a recruit is badly hurt in drill, or deserts, and Edric blames himself.

| Progress | What he gains |
|---|---|
| 2 | **A loyal few.** Four recruits (use **guard** stats) call themselves his, and will follow him on any mission near Kalaman. |
| 4 | **Renown.** +1 renown with the Knights of Solamnia, as word spreads about the young knight who trains anyone who asks. |
| 6 | **A banner.** The company sews him a banner. When Edric uses his Banneret rallying features while it flies within sight, allies who can see it also have advantage on their next saving throw against being frightened. |
| 8 | **Edric's Company.** About forty soldiers who would die for him. In mass combat (Warriors of Krynn) or the Siege of Kalaman, they count as an extra allied unit, or they can hold one position the party would otherwise have to defend themselves. |
| 10 | **Their captain.** The Order formally gives him command of them. This lines up with the Captain rung of his rank progression, and gives Soth a lot of people to threaten. |

## Level-Up Moments

The gods are almost entirely absent, so any divine touch has to be deniable: a coincidence, an animal, the weather, something in his own body. Never say a god did it. Edric's patron is **Mishakal**, the healer, so his moments come through healing, his hands and his mare. *Divine* moments are the gods nudging; *training* and *earned* moments are just Edric getting better.

- **Level 4: the flotilla downriver** *(divine)*. A fevered child on his boat won't stop shaking. He sits up with her all night. Near dawn the fever breaks, and his hands are warm in a way the river cold can't explain. Then he sleeps properly for the first time since Ispin died.
- **Level 5: downtime in Kalaman** *(training)*. Drilling in the castle yard with Darrett. The recruits start copying how he stands, so he starts teaching them. He learns more from teaching than from drilling.
- **Level 6: after the catacombs** *(divine)*. After facing a fallen knight, he finds he has knelt without meaning to and said the old Knights' prayer he stopped saying after Brackenridge. Nothing answers, but the fear doesn't come back. Ask the player: what did he pray for?
- **Level 7: the Northern Wastes** *(divine)*. In a howling dust storm, the mare stops dead, refuses the path and turns aside. If he follows her, she leads him to someone half-buried in the sand of a dried-out canyon, still breathing, left behind by the Wash. Ask: did he follow the horse or the road?
- **Level 8: the passage to the City of Lost Names** *(earned)*. On night watch, he finally writes the letter to Ispin he never got to send. Then he burns it.
- **Level 9: reforging the dragonlance** *(divine)*. Everyone is watching the lance. Edric looks down and sees the scar across his palm, which he's had since Brackenridge, is gone.
- **Level 10: escaping the Threshold** *(divine)*. He carries the slowest of them out and isn't tired. Outside, his mare is waiting, somewhere no horse could have reached.

## Arc: Rising Through the Ranks

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**.

Edric climbs two ladders at once: **Kalaman's army**, where rank means soldiers, and **the Knights of Solamnia**, where rank means honour. They don't always move together.

**Kalaman's army.** Each step needs the milestone **and** the Military Standing. If the Standing isn't there, the promotion waits.

| Rank | When | Military Standing | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Esquire** (lieutenant's rank) | Arrival in Kalaman | any | Leads the party on missions. No soldiers of his own. |
| **Captain** | After Steel Springs, if he held the Inkwater | Neutral or better | Commands one Kalaman unit in any battle, whatever the party's Standing ([[Skirmish Battles#Who Commands What]]). |
| **Commander** | Back from the Wastes | Trusted or better | Several companies. A seat in Vendri's war room as an officer, not a guest. |
| **Marshal** (of a wall, or the field army) | During the siege | Honoured | Commands a whole section of the defence. |
| **Grand Marshal of Kalaman** | After the siege, **only if Vendri has fallen** and Kalaman stands | Honoured, and the city's trust | Commands all of Kalaman's armies. The war's next chapter is his to fight. |

How the war goes decides how far he gets. If Steel Springs is a rout, or the Wastes go badly, he stays a rank lower. If Vendri dies in the siege and the city holds, the council turns to the knight who held the gate.

**The Knights of Solamnia.** Edric starts as a **Knight of the Crown**. Reaching +10 with the Knights ([[Knights of Solamnia]]) gets him knighted in the **Sword** or the **Rose** on the spot. Raising a Company (his downtime) gets him his forty. A Knight of the Rose who is also Grand Marshal of a Nightlund city is a very unusual man, and Dargaard Keep is in Nightlund.

**If Edric dies,** the promotions go to whoever held the line with him, most likely Darrett, if he's still alive.
