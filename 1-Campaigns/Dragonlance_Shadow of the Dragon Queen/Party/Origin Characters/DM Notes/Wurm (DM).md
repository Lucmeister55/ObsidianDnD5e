## The Truth

What Wurm doesn't know about himself.

- **He is the first draconian.** Decades before the draconian armies were bred, Takhisis made her first attempt at corrupting a stolen good dragon's egg, and it was Wurm. It wasn't any metallic egg. It was a **platinum** egg, of Paladine's own line, the rarest egg on Krynn. Corrupting it was a blow aimed straight at her oldest enemy. It's also why Paladine cares so much about Wurm: he is, in a real sense, one of Paladine's own. She poured far too much of herself into that first egg. What hatched wasn't the mindless, obedient soldier she wanted. It was something that could think and want and choose, with black scales and, deep down, a share of her own hunger for domination. When the hatchling wouldn't obey her, Takhisis flew into a rage and hurled it into the river in the Taman Busuk to drown, a failed experiment and nothing more. Only years later did she feel something tug at the edge of her mind: the thing she'd thrown away was still alive, and the piece of herself she'd poured into it still tied them together. That was when she understood what she'd made. It was a poor soldier, but a perfect **vessel**. So she left him where he was and waited, letting him grow up among mortals, bound to her by that link. In due time, she means to wake it fully and use him as her **avatar** in the world, the body the Dark Queen walks in while the other gods are barred. High Hill was the first time she pulled on it. Every rage since has been her trying it on for size.
- **If Wurm ever confronts her.** Takhisis won't deny it. She'll do something worse and play the loving mother. She had to wear Paladine's face, she'll say, because the humans taught him to fear her name, the same humans who caged him, sold him and beat him, and he'd never have trusted her otherwise. She made him herself, with more of her own heart than any other creature she ever made. He's the only one of her children who is truly hers, she has always loved him, and she cast him out only to keep him safe until he was strong enough to come home. Her war, she'll tell him, is for him: to wipe the cruel humans who hurt him off the face of Krynn, the cultists and the magistrates and the Bakarises and every one like them, so that no one like him is ever owned again. All of it is a lie. He's a tool and a body to her, nothing more. But it's exactly what a lonely creature who has never been loved by anyone except an elf lady he believes is dead would want to hear. Play it warm, patient and devastating.
- **The voice.** The presence that brought him back at High Hill isn't Paladine. It's **Takhisis behind a divine mask**. The grey dragon and the "kill them all" are both hers. Paladine's true form is a platinum dragon, and the grey is the tell: platinum tarnished and seen through smoke, a mask that doesn't quite fit. It's also exactly the colour Wurm's own scales would be if the corruption were only half done. When the real Paladine leaves his quiet signs, they're true, bright silver, so the contrast is there for the players to catch. Affects [[Wurm#^bond-voice|the voice]], [[Wurm#^ideal-balance|Balance]] and [[Wurm#^flaw-divine|trusting divine authority]].
- **The rage is possession.** When Wurm rages, it isn't anger. It's Takhisis pouring a sliver of herself into him through the bond she made at High Hill. It feels holy to him, and it's the closest thing she has to a hand in the material world.
- **Wurm is her spy.** That same bond lets Takhisis see through his eyes and hear through his ears, and she listens constantly. Every war council he stands guard at and every plan the party discusses around him reaches the Dragon Army. Use this to make the players ask *how did they know?*: an ambush waiting on a route only the party knew, a target moved the night before a raid, a commander who answers a plan before it's been tried. Use it sparingly, a few times per chapter at most, so it reads as a pattern and not as the DM cheating. Wurm has no idea.
- **Paladine is trying to warn him.** Like all the true gods, Paladine is barred from the world and can't act directly the way Takhisis does through her bond. All he can do is leave small, strange hints, and only Wurm, or a sharp-eyed player, will notice them:
  - Wurm's reflection in still water sometimes shows his eyes grey, when they've never been grey.
  - Silver things go cold in his hands just before the Dragon Army does something it shouldn't have known to do.
  - He dreams of a war council, seen from somewhere above and behind his own head.
  - A child stops in the street, stares up at him for a long moment, and asks, "Why does your shadow have two heads?" Then they run off. Wurm's shadow looks perfectly normal. Use something just as odd and harmless that only makes sense in hindsight. Never have anyone say the truth out loud.
  - **The befuddled old wizard.** This is Paladine's favourite disguise in canon, **Fizban the Fabulous**: a muddled old man in a battered hat who forgets everyone's name, sets things on fire by accident, and is never quite where you left him. Let him turn up near Wurm a handful of times across the campaign, never for long and never explaining himself.

    **Don't say the name "Fizban" at the table until late in the campaign.** Dragonlance fans will recognise it instantly. He's just "the old man". When anyone asks his name, he can't remember it, or offers a different ridiculous one each time ("Oswald? No. Barnaby, perhaps"). Keep his hat and habits a little vaguer than the famous version, so he doesn't give himself away. Save the real name for a big moment, ideally the Temple of Paladine. Some ways to use him:
    - He shares a fire with Wurm one night, rambles about nothing for an hour, and keeps calling him by the wrong name, "Silverscale" or "Brightwing", before correcting himself. Each name hints at the metallic egg.
    - He fumbles a spell that's supposed to light a candle and instead blows out every light in the room except the one directly behind Wurm, then grumbles that "someone's been reading over your shoulder, my boy, terribly rude" and wanders off. Leave the party to argue over what he meant.
    - He gives Wurm a gift that makes no sense, like a small silver mirror, saying "for when you want to see who's really looking." It's the first time Wurm sees the grey in his own eyes clearly.
    - He's gone before dawn every time, and no one else in camp remembers him arriving.
    - **He becomes Wurm's mentor.** Over the campaign the visits grow into something like a friendship, the first teacher Wurm has had since the elf lady. Fizban never gives orders, and that's the whole lesson. Where the voice commands, he asks questions: "And what do *you* think you should do?" He teaches Wurm silly, small, human things, like how to skip a stone, how to haggle at a market, and how to say no to someone, and then makes him practise saying no to Fizban himself. He's hopeless at fighting lessons and refuses to give them. He can't break Takhisis's hold, because he's barred from acting directly, but he can help Wurm grow into someone who can break it himself. If Wurm takes the Paladine path, it's Fizban's patience, not any miracle, that gets him there. And he never once admits who he is.

    Ispin met the same old man once. It's on the module's table of his funeral stories, "a befuddled old man who disappeared before dawn". If Edric or Becklin recognises the description, that's a lovely thread between the two characters.

  Spread these through the campaign and the level-up moments. The goal is that once the truth comes out, the players can look back and see that every clue was there. Affects [[Wurm#^bond-voice|the voice]] and [[Wurm#^ideal-truth|Truth]].
- **Lohezet**, the cult's patron, shares his name with the module's Lohezet (chapter 6, *City of Lost Names*). If they're the same person, that ties Wurm straight to the campaign's endgame. Your call.

## Hooks

- **Why Wurm fights the Dragon Queen.** This is the cruellest irony in the party: he's fighting her armies while she whispers in his ear.
  1. **The voice (from the start).** "Paladine" tells him to destroy the draconians and protect the worthy, and Wurm obeys. Takhisis is happy to spend a few draconians to win a champion.
  2. **Freedom (early on).** The Bakarises are in Kalaman, and he's still legally theirs (see "The contract" below). Renown with the Knights and the Kalaman military is the only road he can see to having his bond broken.
  3. **Belonging (mid-campaign).** The party and the Knights are the first people who've ever treated him as a person. He fights for them because no one has ever let him fight for anyone before.
  4. **Vrella and Lohezet (late).** The trail to Lohezet, and maybe to Vrella, runs straight through the Dragon Army in the City of Lost Names.

- **The contract.** Wurm's whole servitude rests on one sealed parchment from the magistrate of Draevenfall. Serfdom is still legal around Kalaman, so as long as that contract exists, the law says he's the Bakarises' property, however many draconians he kills. Bakaris the Younger keeps it on him, or in a locked strongbox in the family's Kalaman lodgings, and loves to show it off. It's a ready-made side quest with several ways to solve it:
  - **Steal it.** A job made for [[Milo Geartickle|Milo]]. Burning it in front of the Younger would be one of the most satisfying scenes in the campaign. But a stolen contract can be copied from the Draevenfall records, so it may only buy time.
  - **Buy it.** The Bakarises are short of money since their exile. A big enough sum, perhaps a reward from the Kalaman council, might tempt the Elder, if the Younger doesn't find out.
  - **Pull strings.** Renown with the Knights of Solamnia, Marshal Vendri or Kalaman's ruling council could get the contract voided, or Wurm's freedom granted for service to the city. It's slow but permanent, and it's a good reason to invest in the faction system in [[3.00 Storm Clouds]].
  - **The fine print.** Whoever finally reads it might discover something odd. It names Wurm as "the creature recovered from the Paladine sect", and the magistrate's signature sits beside a second seal that matches a mark tied to **Lohezet**.

  Plays on [[Wurm#^ideal-freedom|Freedom]] and [[Wurm#^flaw-freedom|fear of freedom]]. When the contract is finally gone, Wurm should have to face the question of what he does when no one owns him.

- **The voice's agenda.** Takhisis pushes him toward order through conquest: destroy draconians, protect the "worthy", restore balance through strength. Plays on [[Wurm#^bond-voice|the voice]] and [[Wurm#^flaw-divine|trusting divine authority]].
- **The elf lady is Vrella Farsky.** Wurm never learned her name, and he's certain she's dead. She was "given to an oracle of dragons". Whether she's actually alive, and where, is up to you. If she turns up, she should be recognisable without a name:
  - **The scar.** An old, silvery, crescent-shaped dragonnel bite across the back of her left hand.
  - **The kingfishers.** She still folds paper kingfishers out of habit, leaving them on windowsills and tables wherever she's been.

  Seed the kingfishers before she appears. It's a lovely coincidence that Vogler gives the party a folded paper kingfisher as its reward in the module. Wurm finding one, folded the way she folded them, somewhere it shouldn't be is a perfect first hint that she's alive. Plays on [[Wurm#^bond-vrella|the elf lady]].
- **Breaking from the Bakarises.** With the party and the occasional help of the Knights of Kalaman, Wurm begins a slow, uneven break from Bakaris's control. He is still bound to them socially and legally, but no longer spiritually. He becomes too valuable to discard and too dangerous to ignore. Plays on [[Wurm#^ideal-freedom|Freedom]] and [[Wurm#^flaw-freedom|fear of freedom]].
- **Belonging.** The party and the Knights of Kalaman may be the first authority he has known that doesn't abuse him. Plays on [[Wurm#^bond-party|companions]].
- **Prejudice, worship and a deliberate smear.** As the war comes closer to Kalaman, Wurm's looks become a problem. Make this grow over the campaign.
  - **Suspicion.** To most soldiers and townsfolk, a black-scaled dragonborn looks far too much like a draconian. Expect stares, whispers, doors closed in his face, soldiers refusing to share a watch with him, and eventually open accusations that he's a Dragon Army spy. Some of it comes from people the party trusts.
  - **Worship.** A very small minority goes the other way. Refugees, fanatics, people who saw him rise at High Hill start to treat him as a holy sign, follow him, and leave offerings. For Wurm this is worse than the suspicion, because it's the cult all over again, and it can make him freeze or panic.
  - **The smear.** Takhisis wants Wurm isolated and convinced that humans will never accept him, so the Dragon Army is quietly ordered to feed the rumours. In battle, enemy soldiers and draconians sometimes pointedly ignore him, step around him, or even hold back a blow meant for him, always where the Kalaman troops can see. Use it sparingly, a handful of times across the campaign, and let the party work out the pattern before anyone proves it's deliberate.

  Plays on [[Wurm#^flaw-humans|distrust of humans]], [[Wurm#^flaw-draconians|kinship with draconians]] and [[Wurm#^bond-cult|the cult]], and feeds directly into the voice's argument that humanity is beyond redemption.
- **Red Ruin.** The Dragon Army's dragonnel ace is, in this campaign, a child taken young and shaped into a weapon who has never been allowed to be anything else. That is Wurm's story too. When the party learns she killed Ispin, Edric will want her dead, but Wurm may see himself in her and want to spare her, or free her the way no one ever freed him. The truth is that the Death Knight gave the order and framed her, but nobody knows that for a long time, so this can be a real rift between Wurm and Edric. See [[Ser Edric of Brackenridge (DM)]]. Plays on [[Wurm#^bond-outcasts|captives and outcasts]] and [[Wurm#^ideal-freedom|Freedom]].
- **Who's in the rage.** The "other person" who takes over when Wurm rages is Takhisis herself, a little more of her each time, settling into the vessel she made. The meek Wurm is the one who grew up. The raging one is what she made him for. The more he gives in to the voice, the more the rage-self lingers afterwards, and the less detached Wurm feels from what it did, until one day he isn't horrified by it any more. That's the danger sign. On the Paladine path, the two slowly merge into one Wurm who chooses when to fight.
- **The rage.** Powerful enough to turn a battle, but unstable and emotionally costly. Plays on [[Wurm#^flaw-violence|losing control]].

## Downtime: Learning His Letters

Wurm can barely read. Put more than a few words together and he's lost. He can spend downtime learning, ideally with someone teaching him. A party member, Darrett or the old man would all work, and the teacher matters for the roleplay. For each workweek, he makes an Intelligence check, with advantage if someone teaches him:
- **DC 12:** 1 progress.
- **DC 17:** 2 progress.
- **Below 8:** he gives up for the week in shame, and needs someone to coax him back.

| Progress | What he gains |
|---|---|
| 2 | **Signs and names.** He can read simple signs, notices and names. He learns the names of everyone in the party, written down, and keeps them. |
| 4 | **Letters.** He can read and write simple letters. He writes his first one and doesn't know who to send it to. |
| 6 | **Stories.** He can read a book by himself, slowly. He gains proficiency in one of History or Religion, his choice, from what he reads. |
| 8 | **The contract.** He can finally read his own contract, the one that says he's property. He learns exactly what it says, including the fine print (see "The contract" in Hooks). |
| 10 | **His own name.** He's read enough to choose a name for himself, if he wants one. This is a good moment for that arc to land. |

**On the side, kingfishers.** In any downtime, Wurm can fold paper kingfishers for the refugee children in Kalaman. It has no mechanical effect, but each week he does it, roll a d6. On a 6, a child gives him something back. That slowly makes it harder for the town to believe the spy rumours.

## Level-Up Moments

Wurm is the exception to the rule that the gods stay silent. **Takhisis** speaks to him directly, as "Paladine", and she takes credit for every bit of his growth. Underneath, the real **Paladine** leaves much smaller, deniable signs: silver light, gentleness, kindness Wurm didn't expect. Each moment below has both. Let the player notice the quiet one, or not. *Divine* moments are the gods nudging; *training* and *earned* moments are just Wurm getting better.

- **Level 4: the flotilla downriver** *(divine)*. He rows the Bakarises' boat all night. The voice tells him how weak they are and how easily they'd drown. A refugee child falls asleep against his arm, and he doesn't move until dawn so as not to wake her.
- **Level 5: downtime in Kalaman** *(training)*. For the first time in his life, someone teaches him to fight instead of beating him. Darrett is the natural teacher. Wurm is slow to understand that the bruises aren't punishment. One evening, washing at a trough after drill, he sees his reflection look back at him with grey eyes, and then it's gone.
- **Level 6: after the catacombs** *(divine)*. Near the fallen knight, the voice goes silent for the first time since High Hill. In that silence, Wurm hears his own thoughts, and he becomes aware of something he never noticed before: a faint pressure behind his eyes, like someone leaning close to look over his shoulder, which is suddenly gone. Ask the player: what is the first thing he thinks?
- **Level 7: the Northern Wastes** *(divine)*. He comes across a wounded draconian and doesn't kill it. The voice screams at him. When he stands up, there's a single silver scale lying in the dust where he knelt, and it isn't his. That night he dreams of the party's last war council, seen from somewhere above and behind his own head, and in the dream a grey shape is listening.
- **Level 8: the passage to the City of Lost Names** *(divine)*. A metallic dragon is carved into the passage wall. When Wurm touches it, his black scales reflect for a heartbeat under his hand. It's the first hint of what he really hatched from.
- **Level 9: reforging the dragonlance** *(divine)*. In the Temple of Paladine, the voice is quiet and uneasy, and his dragon-bone axe won't come when he calls it. Ask: what does Wurm pray to, here, when it isn't the voice?
- **Level 10: escaping the Threshold** *(divine or earned)*. If you've decided Lohezet is the cult's patron, this is where Wurm faces the man who took Vrella. If not, he levels by carrying the party's wounded out, and the voice tells him they'll owe him for it.

## Arc

- **Trusts Takhisis:** he ends up betraying humanity, believing it is beyond redemption. In the end the meek Wurm disappears completely, and Takhisis walks the world in his body as her avatar.
- **Rejects Takhisis**, helped by everything Fizban taught him: the real Paladine awakens in him. His weapon changes from the dragon-bone greataxe to a radiant greatsword, and his power becomes his own.
- **Or neither:** he breaks free of both gods' manipulation and defines his own moral path.
