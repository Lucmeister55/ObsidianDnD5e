## The Truth

What Thorn doesn't know, or what the other players shouldn't learn early.

- **His royal blood** is known to Thorn but hidden from everyone else. Keep it out of anything the rest of the table sees until he chooses to reveal it.
- **The Orb's history.** The wizards of Istar entrusted it to Lorac just before the Cataclysm. He sealed it in one of his fortresses, then left for Alhana's birth. The fortress fell in the Cataclysm, and the Orb was never found in the rubble. That makes Alhana about 351, born the night of the Cataclysm.
- **The stranger was Cyan Bloodbane.** The great green dragon of canon (*Dragons of Winter Night*; see [[6-draconomicon|Fizban's]]), in a borrowed elven shape, acting for **Takhisis**. He was the one who dug the Orb out of the rubble, or was handed it by her, and brought it to Silvanost. Lorac's fortune went to fund the invasion. His week-long tour was reconnaissance: he walked every road, river and weak point the Dragonarmies later used, including the hidden way into the city. Cyan can turn up again, in any shape.
- **Takhisis in the Orb.** Through the Orb, with Cyan's voice whispering in it, Takhisis preyed on the two things in Lorac that ran deepest, his hunger for power and his duty to defend his people. Once the invasion began, she worked on his hubris and fear. She made him doubt whether Silvanost could kill every dragon the Orb lured in, whether using it would betray the hidden city, and whether he was strong enough to wield it at all. She convinced him the Orb's mere presence warded the city, and fed him false prophetic dreams of the Dragonarmies breaking on that ward, so he stayed complacent while the forest burned. The more he leaned on it, the worse he ruled, and the more he leaned on it again, and it aged him visibly. That is why he kept Silvanost's forces behind the walls and forbade Eltharion from riding out. Affects [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^bond-lorac|Lorac]].
- **Lorac was a good man.** Without the Orb he is a wise Speaker and a loving father, which is what makes this a tragedy. Thorn saw his father change but has no idea why. He blames Lorac's arrogance and sorcery itself, and has no idea there was a dragon behind it.
- **How Silvanost was breached.** The draconians came in by the hidden way Cyan had been shown. Lorac, already mad, blamed Alhana and House Mystic for betraying the city. The fear he was manipulated with, that the enemy would learn the way in, had already come true.
- **The Orb's blast.** Lorac bends the Orb to destruction during his struggle with Eltharion. The blast wrecks Silvanost, and his madness pours out through the Orb into the land, turning Silvanesti into the land of nightmares from canon and the module. Whether Lorac survived is left open; Eltharion found no sign of him.
- **The Bloodbane name.** Davos's family is the **Bloodbanes**, disgraced for a sin during the Cataclysm. The name comes from a company of humans who served Cyan in the Third Dragon War, and that service was the family's sin. That ties Davos and Thorn together through the dragon who destroyed Thorn's home. See [[Davos Bloodbane (DM)]].
- **Silvanesti refugees in the Northern Wastes.** Some of them may have known the royal family. Affects [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^bond-silvanesti|Silvanesti]] and [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^flaw-shame|shame]].

## Hooks

- **Why Thorn fights the Dragon Queen.** He's the one who needs no persuading, but his reasons can change.
  1. **Vengeance (from the start).** The Dragonarmies destroyed his home and tortured him for months. Every draconian he kills is a debt being paid, especially kapaks.
  2. **The people he didn't save (mid-campaign).** The refugees of Vogler and Kalaman are the first people he's lived among since Silvanost. Protecting them is the closest he'll come to what he failed to do for his own. Plays on [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^ideal-preservation|Preservation]].
  3. **The truth (late).** If he learns there was a dragon behind the Orb, and that Takhisis planted it, his hatred has a new target, and he may finally be able to forgive his father.
  4. **Silvanesti (late).** Meeting the refugees in the Wastes gives him a people to fight *for* again, if he lets himself.

- **Vengeance against the Dragonarmies.** His captivity and mutilation define much of who he is now. Fighting alongside the Knights in Kalaman means repeated contact with draconians and dragon forces. That's strategy and personal retribution at once, which makes him a driven hunter and a liability when restraint is needed. Plays on [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^ideal-retribution|Retribution]] and [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^flaw-dragonkin|hostility toward dragonkin]].
- **Inside knowledge.** He's one of the few people with first-hand experience of the Dragonarmies behind the front lines. Being moved between camps and regions gave him practical insight into their structure, movements and methods.
- **Scout and tracker.** His elven senses and survival conditioning make him an exceptional scout. He's at his best ahead of the main force, finding threats, routes and ambushes, not leading from the front.
- **Exile and shame.** He knows the Silvanesti survived as refugees, but he avoids them. His survival while his people suffered, and his failure to protect them, make him shy away from elven society and from anything that could expose him as a Caladon prince. Plays on [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^flaw-shame|shame]] and [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^flaw-guilt|survivor's guilt]].
- **The refugees of the Northern Wastes.** Later, the party meets Silvanesti refugees in the Wastes, some of whom may have known the royal family. His scars mean they don't recognise him at first. He can reveal himself and reclaim his place, or stay anonymous. Either choice has real political and emotional consequences for him and for the survivors. Plays on [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^bond-alhana|Alhana]] and [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^bond-silvanesti|Silvanesti]].
- **The magic he tore out.** He was once one of the most powerful mages in Silvanost, and he severed himself from magic by force after the Orb. Whether that wound can heal, and whether he'd ever let it, is a long-term thread. A late-campaign multiclass back into wizard would be his story's biggest turn. Magical artifacts, sorcerers in the party (see [[Davos Bloodbane]]) and any Silvanesti magic the party finds all press on that. Plays on [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^flaw-arcane|distrust of the arcane]] and [[Aeltharion Caladon (Thorn)#^bond-zhelsuel|Zhelsuel]].

## Downtime: The Grove

Outside Kalaman's walls there's a patch of old woodland the war has half trampled. Thorn can spend downtime there, alone, tending it the old way: clearing the dead wood, planting, burying his kills according to his rite (see his level 5 moment), and keeping watch. For each workweek, he makes a Wisdom (Survival) or Intelligence (Nature) check:
- **DC 15:** 1 progress.
- **DC 20:** 2 progress.
- **Below 10:** soldiers or refugees cut wood from the grove, and Thorn has to decide what to do about it.

| Progress | What he gains |
|---|---|
| 2 | **His bow.** He carves himself a longbow from yew in the grove, the first weapon he has owned since Silvanost. It counts as a magic weapon for overcoming resistance, and it's his. |
| 4 | **Sanctuary.** A long rest taken in the grove grants each creature resting there temporary hit points equal to Thorn's level, once per downtime period. |
| 6 | **The beasts know him.** Animals around Kalaman are never hostile to him unless magically compelled. Once per long rest, he can cast *speak with animals* or *locate animals or plants* without a spell slot. |
| 8 | **Something stirs.** A dryad, or the spirit of an old tree, wakes in the grove and starts speaking to him, the first of the forest's old powers he's met since Silvanost fell. It knows things about the land around Kalaman that no map does. |
| 10 | **The forest answers.** When the Dragon Army comes for Kalaman, the grove comes too: a treant, or a wave of roots and thorns, stands with the defenders for one decisive scene in the Siege of Kalaman. This time an elf stood with the forest, and the forest stands with him. |

## Level-Up Moments

The gods are almost entirely absent, so any divine touch has to be deniable: an animal, the weather, a sound, a growing thing. Never say a god did it. Thorn has turned from the arcane toward the old elven gods of the wild: **Chislev** (the living land), **Habbakuk** (beasts and the cycle of life and death) and **Branchala** (music), the god he once sang for. His moments come through animals, the earth and song, and each one asks him to give something back. *Divine* moments are the gods nudging; *training* and *earned* moments are just Thorn getting better.

- **Level 4: the flotilla downriver** *(divine)*. He hates the boat and watches the forest bank all night. At dawn a kingfisher lands on the prow beside him and stays there until they reach the bank.
- **Level 5: the Missing Scouts mission** *(divine)*. While they're tracking the scouts, a golden stag steps onto the trail, and Thorn shoots it before he's even thought about it. It doesn't run. It lies down and watches him, almost as if it understands, and dies. Its tracks lead back the way it came: the scouts had followed this beautiful creature off the road and into the draconian ambush that took them, and the trail leads Thorn to the survivors.

Then let the question hang. **Was the stag at fault?** It never meant anyone harm. It was only beautiful, and men followed it to their deaths. That's Thorn's whole story in miniature: a wondrous thing that led good people to ruin, a father who followed it, and a son who killed what he loved before he'd even thought about it. Don't spell that out for the player. Just ask them, in character, whether Thorn thinks the stag deserved to die. Then ask: **what does Thorn do with the carcass?** Bury it, eat it and give thanks, burn it, leave it to the wild. Whatever he chooses becomes his rite for every kill after this one, and his answer to the first question says a lot about whether he can ever forgive Lorac, or himself.
- **Level 6: downtime in Kalaman** *(training)*. He's already a fine shot, but fine isn't enough for him. Every dawn outside the walls he tries the same impossible shot: splitting a hanging thread at two hundred paces, while the city guards gather to make bets on him. Every time, the arrow passes a finger's width wide, and he blames the missing eye. Then one morning he notices he's been drawing in time to something, the old rhythm of a Silvanost court dance he hasn't thought of in years, and that he's been drawing the string back the way he used to pluck a harp, light and sure, listening to it rather than fighting it. He relaxes into it, lets the rhythm carry the draw, and the thread parts. The guards go quiet. He tries again without the rhythm, and misses. Ask the player: does he start again, humming, or walk away?
- **Level 7: the Northern Wastes** *(divine)*. Days after a fight, he passes the spot where a draconian he killed fell. A single green shoot is growing out of the ash, the only living thing for miles. Ask: does he tend it, or walk on?
- **Level 8: the passage to the City of Lost Names** *(divine)*. The dark is total and the party is flagging. Thorn sings for the first time in front of anyone, to keep them moving. The echo comes back in more voices than his own.
- **Level 9: reforging the dragonlance** *(earned)*. He watches magic forge a holy weapon, and for once he can't call it a stain. He tells someone it's beautiful, or he doesn't. Either way, he has thought it. For one moment, deep inside, something that went silent years ago stirs.
- **Level 10: escaping the Threshold** *(divine)*. When the way out is lost, he follows birdsong where no birds should be, and it leads them out.

## Arc

His journey is defined by the tension between vengeance and restoration. It keeps testing whether he stays a weapon shaped by trauma, or can recover the identity of Eltharion Vanyarin of Silvanost: son of Lorac, brother of Alhana, and heir to a broken legacy.
