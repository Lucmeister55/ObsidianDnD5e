> [!abstract] Thorn
> **Stalker** · **Nameless** · **Bites**
> 
> - **You are** Thorn, a 66-year-old Silvanesti elf ranger, scarred, half-blind and lame in one leg.
> - **You were** a singer, a poet and a powerful archmage in Silvanost. You stayed when others fled, and watched sorcery destroy your homeland. Then the Dragonarmies took you captive and dragged you from camp to camp for months, and somewhere in that misery you tore your own magic out of yourself.
> - **Now** you've just escaped the Red Dragon Army's camp west of Vogler. You killed the draconians who chased you with your bare hands, and you've stumbled out of the woods straight into a battle.
> - **You love** killing draconians, more than you'd ever admit. Underneath, you still love music, poetry, and the old, deep study of how the world is woven together, but you won't let anyone see it.
> - **You can't stand** dragons, draconians (kapaks most of all, the only thing that still frightens you), and arcane magic in any form, from sorcerers and wizards to enchanted trinkets.
> - **Play him** silent, watchful and cold, always checking for exits and threats. Around dragonkin, your rage takes over. Sometimes, when you think you're alone, you still hum. You never tell anyone you were a prince.

## Appearance

Thorn is gaunt and hard-faced, the fine Silvanesti bones still showing through what captivity made of him. His hair would be white under the dirt. It hangs matted with mud, old blood and woodsmoke, with one thin braid at the side that he keeps without seeming to notice. He doesn't wash, partly because he doesn't care and partly because a hunter who smells of leaf mould is one the prey never scents. A stained strip of cloth covers the empty socket, and the eye that's left is pale and very hard to hold. A scar runs down his cheek, and the Dragonarmies cut his ears back so far that most people don't realise he's an elf. He walks with a limp he never mentions, wears whatever he has stripped from the dead, and always carries the slender yew wand he broke from the tree by the brook.

## Backstory

### Silvanost

He was born **Eltharion Vanyarin** in 285 AC, the younger child of **Lorac Caladon**, Speaker of the Stars, and brother to **Alhana Starbreeze**. The court of Silvanost prized stillness, and he was its free spirit, singing at the autumn feasts, writing verses for his sister and dancing until the lamps burned low. People said he moved as though the music had been written for him.

There was a scholar under the songs. Like his father, he studied at the Tower of High Sorcery in the Forest of Wayreth, for the joy of reading the threads that hold stone to stone the way he read poetry. Where other apprentices learned the forms by rote, he varied them like melodies, and his masters kept getting back things none of them had seen before. By the time the war came he was an archmage, and people said he would surpass his father. His guide through all of it was **Zhelsuel**, an elder of the court and as near to an uncle as a prince could have, who sat up with him through the long nights of study, laughed at his worst verses, and once told him, half joking, that the hardest spell in any book was knowing when to close it.

The story Lorac told most often, and never happily, was about one of the **Dragon Orbs**, crystal globes the wizards of all three Towers forged in the last great war against the chromatic dragons, each holding a dragon's essence and made to call the great wyrms in and bend them. Just before the Cataclysm, as the mobs of Istar turned on its Tower, the wizards gave one into his keeping. He sealed it in a fortress far from Istar and rode home for Alhana's birth, and he was holding his newborn daughter when the Cataclysm came. The fortress fell, and the Orb was never found in the rubble. Alhana saved his life, he liked to say, smiling, and then the smile would fade, because she was also why he lost it. Of everything he had failed to do in his long life, he said, that was his greatest regret.

Then a stranger came to Silvanost. No one remembered letting him in, and no one could later agree on his face. He saw the Speaker alone, and when he came out, the Orb was back in Lorac's hands. Lorac gave him a fortune that would have bought a human kingdom, and the stranger asked for one thing more: to see the wonders of Silvanost. For a week he was shown everything, the white towers and the gardens, the roads and rivers to the borders, and, as a mark of great favour, the hidden way into the city that no outsider had walked in a thousand years. He asked a great many questions. Then he was gone, and nobody could say when he left.

Eltharion had never seen his father so happy. For a season Silvanost was all feasts. Lorac kept the Orb high in his tower behind careful wards, and took it out now and then just to look at it. Eltharion saw it once, through a half-open door, a ball of crystal no bigger than a child's head with mist turning slowly inside it. It was beautiful, and he never forgot the feeling that something in the mist had turned to look back at him.

### The Fall

The Red and Blue Dragonarmies crossed into Silvanesti late in 349 AC, as the leaves were turning. Silvanost was hidden, as it had been for thousands of years, so they set out to destroy the forest around it until there was nowhere left to hide. Draconians went through the aspen groves with axes and fire, and dammed the rivers with the dead. Red dragons burned whole hillsides in a breath, and blue lightning split the oldest trees from crown to root. Night after night the sky beyond the city glowed red, and the wind smelled of smoke and of something sweeter and worse.

The old powers of the forest rose against them: treants tearing down siege towers, dryads leading companies astray until they starved, river spirits rising in black floods to drown whole columns at the fords. For weeks they fought alone. Standing beside them was the elves' oldest duty, since the Silvanesti and their forest had always lived or died together, but the Silvanesti never came. Over the centuries Silvanost had turned inward, ruled in truth by its most learned, where every question went to council and a scholar outweighed a general. The courtiers argued, the scholars meditated, and everyone waited for the Speaker to decide.

He couldn't. The court begged him to use the Orb, and only he could hope to master it. Could Silvanost's mages bring down every dragon it called? If one escaped, would the city's secret be lost? Was he even strong enough? He would say he needed time, and spend it alone in the tower with the Orb, first his nights and then his days, and come down with his eyes somewhere else. He grew jealous of it, stopped asking for counsel and then stopped hearing it. Then he stopped talking about using it at all. Its presence alone would shield them, he said, and he had dreamed of the Dragonarmies breaking against an unseen wall around the city like a wave on rock. In a single year the Speaker of the Stars went grey, his hands shook, and his face drew in until it looked carved from bone. Eltharion begged again and again to take a company out to fight beside the spirits, and every time Lorac forbade it. One by one the old powers fell and burned.

As the forest died, so did something in its people. Songs at the evening gatherings fell silent half-sung. The old sat for days without speaking, and some lay down under the trees and didn't get up. Children slept and couldn't be woken. That was why **Alhana** led most of the Silvanesti away by ship to Southern Ergoth. Eltharion felt the sickness as much as anyone, and stayed anyway, because it was his father's realm and he couldn't leave it to die alone. He said goodbye to his sister at the edge of the royal grounds, and neither of them said aloud that it was probably the last time.

Then Zhelsuel went too. Lorac, deep in his madness, sent the scholars of House Mystic to search the ruins of Istar for more relics to shield the city, and the court called it a mission of hope. Zhelsuel took Eltharion aside and told him the truth. Hardly anyone dared leave the hidden city any more, for fear of being caught and made to give up the way in, so almost no food was coming in. Sending away old scholars who couldn't fight meant fewer mouths. It was exile in all but name.

The end came in the autumn of 350 AC. Horns sounded from the lower city one night, and draconians poured in by the hidden way. The few defenders fell back street by street, the gardens burned, and dragons circled the Tower of the Stars so close that he could hear their wings over the screaming. He found his father at the top of the tower, raving that someone had betrayed them, cursing Alhana by name, and House Mystic. Lorac took the Orb from its wards and held it to his chest like a child. They would take it and flee, tonight, he muttered. He would not lose it again.

Eltharion knew then that if no one used the Orb tonight, Silvanost would die, and that his father would carry it off into the dark instead. He tried to take it. The fight tore the top of the tower apart. Spells he had only ever read about came to his hands like songs, and his father, grey and shaking, gave ground until Eltharion had him on his knees. Then Lorac reached out one trembling hand, and the Orb flew across the chamber to him. Eltharion watched his father take the thing made to call dragons and wrench it into something that only wanted to destroy. The mist turned to white fire. He had time to throw up the barest, most desperate ward he had ever cast, and then the world went white.

When he woke, the Tower of the Stars was open to the sky, and the white city lay broken around it, with dragons, draconians and Silvanesti among the dead. Beyond the walls, the forest had gone wrong. The Orb had poured his father's madness into the land, and the trees wept blood and twisted into clawed and screaming shapes, and things moved between them that belonged in a nightmare. Of his father there was no sign. Everything Eltharion had given his life to had just been turned against his people by the man he had hoped to become.

### Captivity

The Dragonarmies' reinforcements found him in the rubble and took no other prisoners. They never learned who he was. As far as he knew, everyone who stayed was dead and everyone who left was gone, and there was no one left to protect or fail.

They wanted nothing from him, no questions and no ransom, only cruelty, and that was how he learned what draconians are. They aren't soldiers to reason with or beasts to tame. They're malice in a scaled skin, and they enjoy it. The worst were the **kapaks**, who grinned and laughed while they worked. They scarred his ears, burned out one eye slowly with their acid, and broke his leg and let it heal crooked. Nothing frightens Thorn now except kapaks, and he hates nothing more. For months he was dragged from camp to camp, the Red Army and then the Blue, east to Taman Busuk and back, until he ended up in the Red Dragon Army's camp in the woods west of Vogler.

In chains, he had nothing to do but remember: the white fire, his father's face over the Orb, the bleeding trees. He came to hate everything Lorac stood for, the pride and the towers and the endless reaching for power, and himself almost as much, the prince who studied while the forest burned and the archmage who couldn't stop his father. Somewhere in the worst of it he did something no mage does, and tore his own connection to magic out of himself. He has never told anyone what that felt like. The threads went silent, and he would rather stay deaf to them forever than become what his father became. He has hated sorcery, and everyone who touches it, ever since.

In Brookgreen of 351 AC the camp began to empty, whole companies marching out for reasons nobody told a prisoner. One night the draconian on watch leaned too close to his cage. He bit through its throat, took its keys and ran, limping toward the river, and when the others came after him he killed them with his bare hands.

Deep in the woods he stopped at a brook to drink, under an old yew, and without knowing why he broke off a slender length of it. Something stirred. It wasn't the arcane, which was gone for good. It was greener and older, and it came up out of the earth and the wood instead of down from the moons, and when he reached for it, it answered. He sat a long while with the wand across his knees, listening to the water and the birds. Even in the white towers of Silvanost he had never belonged anywhere the way he belonged beside that brook.

Then he heard steel through the trees, and a horn call he knew too well from the camps, and the peace went out of him. He broke from the treeline just as the Battle of High Hill began.

He calls himself **Thorn** now, because the prince is gone and what's left survives by wounding whatever comes too close. When he thinks no one is listening, he still hums.

## Personality

Thorn says very little to people he doesn't know, and when he does speak it's quiet and exact. His eye is always moving, to the doors, the treeline, the hands of whoever is standing nearest. Captivity taught him to see everyone as a threat first and a person second, and to strike before he is struck.

Hatred is what keeps him going. Around dragons and draconians, or anyone who serves the Dragonarmies, his judgement leaves him and something colder takes its place. Killing them is the one thing that still gives him anything like joy, though he would never admit it. Magic stirs the same fury in him, whether it's a sorcerer, a wizard of the Towers or some enchanted trinket, because to him it is all the same poison that took his father.

He has turned his back on towers and tomes for something older, the ways of forest and beast that he believes his people followed before they grew proud. He was an archmage once. Now he is starting again from nothing, with a yew wand and a crooked leg and one good eye, and whatever skill he has as a ranger he learned from staying alive.

The singer is still in there, though. Music can stop him in his tracks, and something beautiful in the wild can hold him still for a long moment. He will never let anyone see how much he still loves songs and poetry, or the study of the world he once meant to give his life to. His trust is very hard to earn, but once he gives it, he gives it completely.

He never tells anyone who he really is. Part of it is that he has turned his back on everything the name Caladon stood for. Part of it is guilt, because he doesn't believe the last prince of Silvanost deserves the title after letting it burn. But part of it is plain sense. The son of Lorac, alive and loose in a world at war, would be a prize to the Dragonarmies and a problem to half the courts of Ansalon, and Thorn has no wish to wake up with a knife in his back.

**Traits**

- Speaks rarely and quietly, and means every word.
- Always knows where the exits are.
- Unnervingly calm when things go wrong.
- Goes still when he hears music, and pretends he didn't.
- Changes the subject whenever anyone asks where he's from.

## Playing Thorn

Ideas, not rules. Use what fits.
- **Say less.** Answer in as few words as possible, and let the silence finish the sentence.
- **Where he stands.** When the party enters a room, say where Thorn is: by the door, back to the wall, near the window.
- **The hum.** When he thinks he's alone, he hums a tune, never the same one, never finished. If anyone notices, he stops.
- **Kapaks.** Freeze for one heartbeat when you see one. Then go for it. It's the only fear he ever shows.
- **Arcane magic** makes him step back from whoever's casting it, every time.
- **The leg and the eye.** Let them slow him on stairs and in bad light. He turns down help once, and takes it the second time.
- **With the others.** The sorcerer is everything he despises, and the one most likely to make him admit what he used to be. The kender won't stop asking where he's from.
- **The company** means nothing to him. The refugees being fed does. Let that be his reason to stay.

## Unique Mechanics

### Downtime: The Grove

Outside Kalaman's walls, Thorn can tend a patch of old woodland the war has half destroyed, clearing it, planting it, and keeping it safe the old elven way. For each workweek, make a Wisdom (Survival) or Intelligence (Nature) check and tell the DM the result. The more he gives back to the land, the more it gives back.
