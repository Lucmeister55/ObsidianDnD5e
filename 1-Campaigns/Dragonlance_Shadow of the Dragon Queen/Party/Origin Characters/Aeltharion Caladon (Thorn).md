## Appearance

Thorn is gaunt and hard-faced, with the fine bones of the Silvanesti still showing through what captivity made of him. His hair is white, or it would be, somewhere under the dirt. Now it hangs loose and matted, caked with mud and old blood and woodsmoke, with a single thin braid at one side that he keeps without seeming to notice. He doesn't wash it, and he doesn't wash much else either. Partly he doesn't care. Partly it's deliberate, because a hunter who smells of earth and leaf mould is one the prey never scents coming. A strip of stained cloth is bound across his face over the empty socket, and the eye that's left is pale and cold and very hard to hold. A scar runs down across his cheek, and his ears were cut back so far by the Dragonarmies that, under all that matted hair, most people don't realise at first glance that he's an elf at all. He walks with a limp he never mentions. He came out of captivity with nothing but the rags on his back, and since then he has worn whatever he can strip from the dead. The one thing he always carries is the slender yew wand he broke from the tree by the brook.

## Backstory

### Silvanost

He was born Eltharion Vanyarin in 285 AC, the younger child of Lorac Caladon, Speaker of the Stars, and brother to Alhana Starbreeze. If the court of Silvanost could see him now, scarred and silent and killing with his bare hands, few of them would believe it was the same elf. He was once the free spirit of a court that prized stillness, the one who sang at the autumn feasts and wrote verses for his sister and danced until the lamps burned low, and who never made the same thing twice. People said he moved as though the music had been written for him.

There was a scholar beneath the songs as well. Like his father before him, Eltharion went to study at the Tower of High Sorcery in the Forest of Wayreth, and he went for the sheer joy of understanding things. He wanted to learn the hidden grammar of the world, the threads that hold stone to stone and star to star, and to read them the way he read poetry. The other apprentices learned the old forms by rote, while he played with them, turning a spell this way and that the way he would vary a melody, and more than once his masters set him a lesson and got back something none of them had seen before. By the time the war came he was an archmage, with few in Silvanost who could match him, and people had begun to say he would one day surpass his father.

His guide through all of it was **Zhelsuel**, an elder of the court and as near to an uncle as a prince of the Silvanesti could have. It was Zhelsuel who sat up with him through the long nights of study, who laughed at his worst verses and argued with his best ones, and who once told him, half joking, that the hardest spell in any book was knowing when to close it.

All through Eltharion's childhood there was one story his father told more than any other, and never happily. It was about one of the **Dragon Orbs**, globes of etched crystal that the wizards of all three Towers forged together in the last great war against the chromatic dragons, each holding the trapped essence of a dragon and made to call the great wyrms in and bend them to a mage's will. Just before the Cataclysm, as the mobs of Istar turned on its Tower, the wizards gave one into Lorac's keeping. He sealed it in one of his own fortresses, far from Istar, and believed it safe. Then word came that Alhana's birth was near, and he rode for Silvanost to be with her mother. He was there, holding his newborn daughter, when the Cataclysm came, and the fortress didn't survive it. When he went back there was nothing left but rubble, and the Orb was nowhere in it. Alhana saved his life, he liked to say, and he would always smile when he said it. Then the smile would fade, because she was also the reason he lost it. He searched for years and never found so much as a rumour of it. It had been his to keep, and of everything he had done and failed to do in his long life, he said, losing it was his greatest regret.

Then, long before the war, a stranger came to Silvanost. No one at court remembered letting him in, and no one could later agree on his face. He asked to see the Speaker alone, and when he came out, the Orb was back in Lorac's hands. Lorac rewarded him with a fortune, chests of gold and gems and old elven treasures that would have bought a human kingdom, and the stranger took it all with a polite little bow. He asked for only one thing more, which was to be shown the wonders of Silvanost. So for a week he was taken everywhere, through the white towers and the gardens and the halls of the Speaker, and out along the roads and rivers of the forest all the way to the borders, and even, as a mark of the Speaker's great favour, along the hidden way into the city that no outsider had walked in a thousand years, and he looked at everything with great interest and asked a great many questions. Then he was gone, and nobody could remember quite when he had left.

Lorac had always been a good Speaker and a gentle father, wise in council, patient with his son's wildness, and quick to laugh at his verses. Even so, Eltharion had never seen him so happy. For a season Silvanost was full of feasts, and Lorac laughed more than his children could remember him laughing. His old failure had been undone, and he treasured the Orb the way a man treasures a lost heirloom come home. He kept it high in his tower behind careful wards and took it out now and then to simply look at it. Eltharion saw it only once, through a half-open door, a ball of crystal no bigger than a child's head with mist turning slowly inside it. It was beautiful. He never quite forgot the feeling that something in the mist had turned to look back at him.

### The Fall

Then the Dragonarmies came, the Red and the Blue together.

They crossed into Silvanesti late in 349 AC, as the leaves were turning. Silvanost itself was hidden, as it had been for thousands of years, and no army had ever found the way in. So they came for the forest instead, and set about destroying everything around the city until there would be nowhere left for it to hide. Draconians went through the aspen groves with axes and fire, felling trees that had stood since before the first Speaker was crowned, and burning what they couldn't be bothered to fell. They dammed the clear rivers with the bodies of the dead, and fouled the springs. Dragons came after them. Where the red dragons flew, whole hillsides of forest went up in a single breath, and where the blues passed, their lightning split the oldest trees from crown to root. Night after night the sky beyond the city glowed red, and the wind that came into Silvanost smelled of smoke and of something sweeter and worse.

The forest did not die quietly. The old powers of the glades rose against the invaders, as they had in the elder days, with treants walking out of the deep woods to tear down siege towers, dryads leading whole companies astray until they starved, and the spirits of the rivers rising in black floods that tore whole columns of draconians off the fords and drowned them screaming. They were nature's rage given shape, and they fought for nothing but the forest. For weeks they fought on alone, in the burning woods all around Silvanost. Since the first trees were planted, it had been the elves' oldest duty to stand beside them, for the forest's sake and for their own, because the Silvanesti and their forest had always lived or died together.

The Silvanesti never came. Over the long centuries the elves of Silvanost had turned inward, safe behind their white walls and lost in their histories and debates. The Speaker still sat on his throne, but in truth the realm had become an epistocracy, ruled by its most learned, where a scholar's opinion outweighed a general's and every question went to council. Somewhere along the way they had forgotten that the forest was ever their ally. The wild elves of old would have ridden out to meet the enemy beneath the trees. In Silvanost the courtiers argued and the scholars meditated, and nobody did anything drastic, because they were all waiting for their Speaker to decide.

And the Speaker could not decide. As the reports grew worse, the court begged him to use the Orb and call the dragons in to break them, the way it was made to. Only Lorac had any hope of mastering it, so it was his to use or no one's. He would um and ah, and say he needed time to think. Could Silvanost's mages bring down every dragon it lured in? If even one escaped, would the city's secret be lost? Was he even strong enough to wield it? Then he would climb the tower and spend that time alone with the Orb, first his nights and then his days, and come down with his eyes somewhere else and no answers for anyone. The worse the war went, the more he turned to it, and the more he turned to it, the worse the war went.

He grew jealous of the thing the way old men grow jealous of a young wife, and he stopped asking for counsel, and then stopped hearing it. Then he stopped talking about using the Orb at all. It didn't need to be used, he began to say. As long as it stayed in Silvanost, its presence alone would shield them, a ward no dragon could cross, and all they had to do was keep it close and wait out the storm. The dreams told him so. He would come down certain he had seen the Dragonarmies break against some unseen wall around the city like a wave on rock, and nothing anyone said could shake him. Why ride out to die in the woods, when he had seen how it ended? And he aged. Elves do not grow old the way humans do, but in a single year of war the Speaker of the Stars went grey, his hands began to shake, and his face drew in until it looked carved from bone.

Eltharion begged him again and again to let him take a company out, any company, to fight beside the spirits in the woods while there were still woods to fight in. Every time, Lorac forbade it. Silvanost would hold, he said. The Orb would hold them. He had seen it. And one by one the old powers of the forest fell, and were cut down, and burned.

The Silvanesti are bound to their forest in ways humans have never understood, and as the forest died, so did something in them. It came on slowly at first. The songs at the evening gatherings grew thin and fell silent half-sung. The old began to fade, greying and quiet, sitting for days without speaking, and some of them simply lay down beneath the trees and did not get up. Children slept and could not be woken from their dreams. The healers had no name for it and no cure. It was grief, and it was the land, and it was killing them.

That was why Alhana led them away. As the Dragonarmies closed on Silvanost itself, most of the Silvanesti took ship with her for Southern Ergoth, to save what was left of their people before there was nothing left to save. Eltharion would not go. It was his father's realm, and he could not leave it to die alone, and he felt the sickness in himself as much as anyone, and he stayed anyway.

He said goodbye to his sister at the edge of the royal grounds. She begged him to come with her, and he refused, and neither of them said aloud what both of them were thinking, that it was probably the last time. He stood and watched until she was gone.

In the end Zhelsuel left as well. In his growing madness, Lorac sent the scholars of House Mystic out to search the ruins of old Istar for more relics to shield the city with, and the court called it a mission of hope. Before he went, Zhelsuel took Eltharion aside and told him the truth. Hardly anyone dared leave the hidden city any more, for fear of being caught and made to give up the way in, and so almost no food was coming in from outside, so the stores were running low. Sending away a company of old scholars who couldn't fight meant fewer mouths to feed. It was exile in all but name. He gave what farewell he could, and then he was gone too.

So Eltharion was there at the end, a year into the war, in the autumn of 350 AC.

It came without warning. One night the horns sounded from the lower city, and before anyone understood what they meant, draconians were pouring through the hidden way into Silvanost, the way no outsider had walked in a thousand years. The city that had never been found was suddenly full of fire. The few defenders who had stayed fell back street by street through the white stone, the gardens burned, and dragons came down out of the dark to circle the Tower of the Stars, so close that Eltharion could hear their wings over the screaming.

He found his father at the top of the tower, raving. Someone had betrayed them, Lorac shouted. It was Alhana, who had run to the humans, or House Mystic, who had taken the city's secrets with them, and he cursed his own daughter by name. Then he went to the wards, and took out the Orb, and held it to his chest like a child. They had to go, he muttered. They would take it and flee, now, tonight, and find somewhere safe. He would not lose it again. Not this time. Not again.

Eltharion knew then that his father could not be trusted with it. He would carry it off into the dark and leave his people to burn, and if no one used the Orb tonight, Silvanost would die. So he tried to take it from him. The fight that followed tore the top of the tower apart. Spells Eltharion had only ever read about came to his hands as easily as songs, and his father, grey and shaking and half out of his mind, gave ground. For a few moments Eltharion was winning. He was younger and stronger, and Lorac had been wasting away for a year. He had his father on his knees.

But Lorac reached out one trembling hand, and the Orb flew across the chamber into his grasp. He looked down into it, and his face changed, and Eltharion watched his father take the thing that had been made to call dragons and wrench it into something else, something that only wanted to destroy. The mist inside it turned to white fire. Eltharion had time to throw up a single shield, the barest, most desperate ward he had ever cast, and then the world went white.

When he woke, Silvanost was a ruin. The Tower of the Stars had been torn open to the sky, and the white city lay broken around it, with dragons and draconians and soldiers among the dead in its streets, and Silvanesti too, more than he could bear to think of. And beyond the walls, the forest had gone wrong. The Orb's power hadn't stopped at destruction. It had poured his father's madness out into the land itself, and the trees wept blood and twisted into clawed and screaming shapes, and things moved between them that had no right to exist outside a nightmare. Of his father, there was no sign at all.
Everything Eltharion had given his life to, all the deep workings of the world, had just been turned against his people by the very man he had hoped to become.

### Captivity

The Dragonarmies' reinforcements found him there, still lying in the rubble, and they took no other prisoners but him. They had no idea who he was, and he never told them. To them he was just the one elf who had crawled out of the ruins, and as far as he knew, they were right. Everyone who had stayed in Silvanost was dead, and everyone who had left was long gone. There was no one left for him to protect, or answer to, or fail.

They never wanted anything from him. There were no questions and no ransom, only cruelty for its own sake, and that was how he learned what draconians really are. They aren't soldiers who might be reasoned with, or beasts that might be tamed. They are malice in a scaled skin, made to hurt and to rule, and they enjoy it. The worst of them were the kapaks, with their sly grins and their venom, who laughed while they worked. They scarred his ears. They burned out one of his eyes with their acid, slowly, and broke his leg and let it heal crooked. Nothing frightens Thorn now, except the kapaks, and nothing he hates comes close to how much he hates them. For months they dragged him from camp to camp, first the Red Dragon Army and then the Blue, east as far as Taman Busuk and back west again, until at last he was held in the Red Dragon Army's camp in the woods west of Vogler.

Through all those months he had nothing to do but remember. Every night in chains he saw the white fire again, and his father's face over the Orb, and the bleeding trees around the ruins of Silvanost. He came to hate everything Lorac had stood for, the pride and the towers and the endless reaching after power. And he came to hate himself almost as much, the prince who had studied while the forest burned, and the archmage who had matched his father spell for spell and hadn't been able to stop him. Somewhere on that long road, in the worst of it, he did something no mage does. Almost without knowing it, he severed his own connection to the weave of magic, and renounced the arcane entirely. He has never told anyone what it felt like. He only knows that the threads went silent, and that he would sooner stay deaf to them forever than become what his father became. He has hated sorcery, and everyone who touches it, ever since.

Then, in Brookgreen of 351 AC, the camp began to empty. Whole companies marched out over a few days, for reasons no one bothered to explain to a prisoner, until only a handful of guards were left. One night the draconian on watch leaned too close to his cage. He bit through its throat, took its keys, and ran, starving and half-dead, limping toward the river. When the others came after him, he killed them with his bare hands.

Somewhere deep in the woods, when there was no one left chasing him, he stopped by a brook to drink. An old yew leaned over the water, and without quite knowing why, he broke off a slender length of it and closed his hand around it. Something stirred. It wasn't the arcane, which was gone from him for good. It was quieter and greener than that, and older, and it came up out of the earth and the wood instead of down from the moons, and when he reached for it, it answered. For a long time he just sat there with the yew wand across his knees, watching the water run over the stones, listening to the birds and the wind in the leaves. It was such a small, plain place, and it was beautiful. He felt something like peace, and something stranger than that. Even in the white towers of Silvanost, he had never felt he belonged anywhere the way he belonged beside that brook.

Then, somewhere ahead through the trees, he heard steel. There was shouting and the roar of a battle, and over it a horn call he knew far too well from the camps. The peace went out of him as if it had never been there. He would hunt them down, and he would kill every last one of them. He broke from the treeline just as the Battle of High Hill was about to begin.

Whoever came out of those woods, it wasn't the singer of Silvanost. He calls himself **Thorn** now, because the prince is gone and what was left is something that survives by wounding whatever comes too close. And yet, when he thinks no one is listening, he still hums.

## Personality

Thorn says very little to people he doesn't know, and when he does speak it's quiet and exact. His eye is always moving, to the doors, the treeline, the hands of whoever is standing nearest. Captivity taught him to see everyone as a threat first and a person second, and to strike before he is struck.

Hatred is what keeps him going. Around dragons and draconians, or anyone who serves the Dragonarmies, his judgement leaves him and something colder takes its place. Killing them is the one thing that still gives him anything like joy, though he would never admit it. Magic stirs the same fury in him, whether it's a sorcerer, a wizard of the Towers or some enchanted trinket, because to him it is all the same poison that took his father.

He has turned his back on towers and tomes for something older, the ways of forest and beast that he believes his people followed before they grew proud. He was an archmage once. Now he is starting again from nothing, with a yew wand and a crooked leg and one good eye, and whatever skill he has as a ranger he learned from staying alive.

The singer is still in there, though. Music can stop him in his tracks, and something beautiful in the wild can hold him still for a long moment. He will never let anyone see how much he still loves songs and poetry, or the study of the world he once meant to give his life to. His trust is very hard to earn, but once he gives it, he gives it completely.

He never tells anyone who he really is. Part of it is that he has turned his back on everything the name Caladon stood for. Part of it is guilt, because he doesn't believe the last prince of Silvanost deserves the title after letting it burn. But part of it is plain sense. The son of Lorac, alive and loose in a world at war, would be a prize to the Dragonarmies and a problem to half the courts of Ansalon, and Thorn has no wish to wake up with a knife in his back.
