## Appearance

Davos is a lean, good-looking young man with a sun-browned face, an easy half-smile, and the steady eyes of someone who is always working out what you want. His hair falls to his shoulders and is never the same colour two days running, silver one morning and wine-red by evening and black as a cellar when he's angry. He dresses a cut above his station, in a sky-blue shirt and a dark mage's vest stitched with little stars and lined in red, with leather straps across his chest and a knife at his belt that he's rather good with. The phases of the moons are tattooed down his right arm, from wrist to shoulder, and when he casts, they seem to glow.

## Backstory

### Relgoth

Davos grew up in the lower districts of **Relgoth**, outside the old walls, where river labourers and small craftsmen lived on top of one another. Relgoth, capital of the province of Vingaard, sits in a dry valley on the Knight's High Road between Delgaard and Ryn, and it still hasn't finished recovering from the last occupation.

His father died in the city prison when Davos was a boy, and nobody in the family likes to say why. Since then his mother has raised him and his younger brother and sister alone, on whatever honest work she can find. Now and then, half proud and half bitter, she talks about a time when the name **Bloodbane** meant money and influence, before an ancestor committed some terrible sin in the old wars and the Knights of Solamnia stripped the family of everything. The name survived. Not much else did.

As the eldest, Davos learned early that hunger doesn't wait for talent. He watched his mother stretch meals that were too small and hold the household together by stubbornness alone, and he became the man of the house long before he was ready. It made him quick at reading people, seeing trouble coming, and finding the angle that got everyone through to the next day.

### The Guard

One evening at the public bathhouse, a travelling mage left a fine set of robes folded on a bench, and Davos walked out wearing them. He didn't have a scrap of magic in him and didn't need any. With a confident voice and a bag of painted tin, he became a hedge-mage selling "enchanted" charms to anyone who wanted to believe, and for a while it worked.

But the robes had been reported stolen, and a city guard recognised the embroidery on the sleeves. It was the worst guard it could have been. The dock children called him **the Ferryman**, because everyone paid his toll, in coin or some other way. He was a big, smiling man who beat the dock children for fun, and who had once broken Davos's little brother's arm for stealing an apple. When Davos tried to talk his way out and slip off, it all went wrong at once, and he was on the ground with the guard's hand closing on his throat, and the threats pouring out: the lash, the prison that had killed his father, his family paying alongside him.

That night Nuitari was full, the black moon only its own can see, and as the street went dim at the edges, Davos saw it hanging above the rooftops, a perfect circle of nothing where the stars should be, looking at him. Then it was inside him. Cold poured into his chest like river water through a broken dam, and the panic turned into something that wasn't afraid of anything at all. His hand came up on its own and closed on the guard's wrist, and darkness ran out of his fingers like ink into water, up the man's arm and across his neck in black veins. The guard managed only a wet, choking sound. His skin greyed and split, his eyes went dark from the inside, and he folded onto the cobbles like a sack of empty clothes, smoking faintly in the cold.

For a long moment the cold kept coming, and part of Davos wanted it to. Then it drained away, and he was just a boy in a stolen robe, shaking beside a dead man. The plain brown hair he'd had all his life had gone pitch black.

He would have hanged for it, but the owner of the robes had been watching: a White Robe named **Antimodes**, an old archmage and diviner with a famous habit of finding gifted strays. The Mages of High Sorcery leaned on the city with all the quiet weight they had, and the gallows went without him. The price was Davos himself. From that day they held his life in their hands, and they had no intention of letting a prodigy go. He had been claimed, and he has never mistaken it for mercy.

His magic took shape under their watch, and it was strange from the start, answering to all three moons unevenly: the clarity of Solinari, the shifting balance of Lunitari, the dangerous pull of Nuitari. His hair never went back to brown. It shifts with the moons and his mood, paling toward silver when Solinari is strong or he's calm, warming to red under Lunitari or when he's scheming, and going black when Nuitari rises or his temper does. Anyone who knows him well can read his mood off his head before he says a word. The Mages took a far closer interest in that than he could account for.

He has never believed a word of their ideals. The Order is powerful, comfortable, and run mostly by people who have never been hungry, and their talk of balance leaves him cold. But it gave him training, protection, and a wage he sends almost all of back to Relgoth. Without it he'd still be a condemned man.

### Trial of the Barb

On **14 Brookgreen 350 AC**, the Night of the Eye, Davos was sent to the Barb, a ruined spire near Maelgoth that only wakes with magic while the moons are aligned, for his first real step toward the Order.

A Red Robe named **Rovina** met the initiates, talked at length about the three Orders, and asked each of them which they felt closest to. It wasn't binding, she said, only a measure. Then she led them to a sealed chamber with an orb for each of them, to be set on the central pedestal before the night ended, when the chamber would vanish. Anyone who failed would be expelled.

The chamber was round and dim and full of small sounds. Each orb hung around the neck of a young basilisk, blind but very dangerous, that reacted to movement, noise and spellcasting alike. Davos had told Rovina he felt closest to the Red Robes, mostly because the middle seemed the safest place to stand. In the chamber he looked for the angle, as he always had. The basilisks hunted by sound, so he flicked a coin across the floor, and his blundered toward it, straight into another initiate's. While the two creatures tore at each other, Davos crept in and cut his orb free. He never lifted a hand against anything. He also didn't look back to see whether the other initiate got out, and he heard the scream behind him as he left.

On the pedestal his orb turned red, the colour he had claimed. Then a thread of black curled through the heart of it and stayed, and beside it, so faint he almost missed it, a single sliver of white. Rovina congratulated him: red was cleverness and balance, and no colour was ever a failure. She said nothing about the black, or the white. She handed him a sealed scroll to deliver unopened to the mage **Wyhan** in Kalaman, his contact in the Order from now on, who would decide when he was ready for the true Test. She smiled in a way that told him the trial had shown her more than it had shown him.

Kalaman was Antimodes's choice. "Go where the tides run strongest," the old man told him the last time they spoke, "and learn how the world is really run." Davos took it for an old man's riddle, and the posting for a polite kind of exile.

So he set out for Kalaman north of the river, which meant crossing by ferry at Vogler. But Vogler is in the middle of the Kingfisher Festival and getting ready for Ispin Greenshield's funeral, and every ferry is delayed. There are Solamnic Knights in town, though, and Davos has never wasted an opportunity in his life. He has found a seat at a knight's table in the tavern, and a deck of cards, and he's winning, not too much, with a little help.

## Personality

Davos is clever, watchful and deeply cynical. He doesn't trust any system that talks about justice while it protects the comfortable, and he has very little patience for nobles, officials, or anyone who asks for obedience from people who already have the least. He learned young that survival belongs to the quickest mind in the room, and very rarely to the most honourable one.

He keeps himself under tight control on the outside, but he's restless underneath. Authority gets under his skin, especially when it talks about duty and honour, and the Knights of Solamnia make him wary however close he has to get to them. Their ideals sound fine. He's just seen too much to take them at face value.

He isn't cruel, though. He sends nearly every coin he earns home, and his family's welfare is the one thing he takes completely seriously. His cynicism comes from experience, and he wants a better life for the people he loves even if he doesn't believe the world owes anyone one.

He stays with the Order because of what they could do with the truth about the guard, and because the wage feeds his family, and because, if he's honest, he loves the power. His training started late, though, and he still can't fully control it. The moons pull at him hard, at his moods as much as his spells, and he's at his sharpest when the right moon rides high and at his most dangerous when the wrong one does.

So he is caught three ways at once, between his family's needs and the Mages' demands and a war gathering around Kalaman. He knows the river better than most. If Kalaman falls, there's nothing between the Dragon Army and Relgoth but the Vingaard itself, and that would carry them straight to his mother's door. He has the power to matter. He doesn't yet know what kind of mage he's going to be.

## Playing Davos

Ideas, not rules. Use what fits.
- **Every room is a market.** Before he speaks, decide who in the room has something he wants, and what they'd take for it.
- **Count the coin** in front of people, then send most of it home. Let the party catch him writing the letter.
- **Watch the moons.** Look up every night and say which moon is up. It matters to him in a way it doesn't to anyone else.
- **The robes.** Never explain where they came from the same way twice.
- **The temper.** Keep the lid on until someone pushes. When it goes, decide which thing he said he'll regret.
- **With the others.** The elf hates every spell he casts, so cast them politely, and often. The knight preaches honour from a full belly, which Davos can't stand, except that this one shares his rations.
- **The company** is the best con he's ever been part of: paid, protected, and on the side of the law for once. Enjoy it, out loud.

## Unique Mechanics

Davos's magic follows the three moons. See [[Calendar#The Three Moons]] for their cycles.

### Downtime: The Cards

Davos can spend a workweek working Kalaman's gambling dens, from the soldiers' dice games at the Steady Beacon to the guild sons' card tables. Make a Charisma (Deception) or Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check and tell the DM the result. Win, and the money goes home to Relgoth. Win big, and he hears things worth knowing. Lose badly, and someone catches him cheating.

The Mages of High Sorcery take a dim view of magic used to cheat at cards, so be careful how much help the moons give him.

Every card sharp on the docks tells the same story: there's a bigger game somewhere under the lower city, run by the Firefishers, and you can't get a seat unless they ask you. Davos has never turned down a bigger game.
