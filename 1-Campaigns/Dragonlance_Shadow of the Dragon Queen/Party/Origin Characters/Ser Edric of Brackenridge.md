## Appearance

Edric looks older than his twenty-six years. The road and the grief have put lines around his eyes early. He's tall and broad-shouldered, with straw-blond hair swept back from a weathered face, a long drooping moustache he is privately rather proud of, and pale blue eyes that are usually looking at someone else to see if they're all right. He wears his knight's plate polished bright, trimmed in gold over a deep blue tabard, with a grey-blue cloak thrown over one shoulder and pinned with the badge of the Order. He smells, faintly and always, of horse.

## Backstory

### Brackenridge

Edric grew up in **Brackenridge**, a frontier village on the trade roads of eastern Solamnia, where the land climbs toward the Estwilde and the far peaks of the Taman Busuk. It was remote and poor, but quiet enough that the wagons kept coming.

His father, **Darian Vellcrest**, was the cooper and wagonwright who fitted out every caravan before the mountain roads, a careful man whose saying was "prepare for the worst, even on good days". His mother, **Melia**, was the village healer, with herb-stained hands, who patched up the victims of fever and raiders alike and liked to say that "people endure longer than they think they can". From his father Edric learned horses, and found a calm in the stalls he never found anywhere else. From his mother he learned poultices and bandages, and how to keep a wounded man breathing until help came.

In **325 AC**, when he was twelve, steppe raiders came out of **Khur** and Brackenridge burned in a single night. His father died in the street trying to hold them back. His mother died over Edric, shielding him from a volley of arrows.

The raiders roped the boy in a line of captives for the slave markets of Khur, but a company of Solamnic Knights under [[Ispin Greenshield|Ser Ispin Greenshield]] rode them down and cut the prisoners loose. Edric watched them keep their discipline in the chaos, show mercy that wasn't weakness, and spend their strength on strangers, and afterwards he asked Ispin to take him on. Ispin, an odd wandering knight who had never wanted a squire, said no. So Edric followed him anyway, trailing his horse for days, sleeping at the edge of his campfire, mending his tack before he woke and asking again every morning, until one morning Ispin sighed, handed him the reins, and stopped saying no.

### Squire to Ispin

Edric trained as if his life depended on it, to become the kind of man who could stop the next village burning. It never quieted the voice that said he had failed the first one, or the older anger he could never aim at anyone: why his parents had suffered, and where the gods had been that night. He stopped expecting an answer.

Ispin trained him on the road instead of in castle yards, sleeping under open skies and seeing towns only for supplies, more like hedge knights than Knights of Solamnia. He told a new story by the fire every night, each more outrageous than the last: the minotaur pirates who chased him across the Blood Sea for three days, the night in a roc's nest, the gnome who hired him to fetch a weasel she had launched from Mount Nevermind all the way to Sancrist. He called every stranger "old friend" and every innkeeper "my dear", sang badly and constantly, and couldn't cook, so within a year Edric did all the cooking. He carried a round, mossy-green shield carved with a scarred broadleaf tree, which he called **Old Moss**, talked to in a crisis, and claimed a unicorn had given him in Darken Wood. Thrown in a cell in Palanthas for shaving the Lord of Palanthas's prize poodles, he talked his way out with a story and a borrowed lute, and told Edric afterwards that a good tale is worth more than a good sword, most days. He had one saying he never smiled at: "An order's only worth following if it serves the people it's meant to protect." His father had told him that, he said, and his grandfather before that.

There was one subject Ispin would never tell a story about. Whenever anyone mentioned the Dargaard Mountains, the black ruin of Dargaard Keep or the legend of the Dread Knight said to haunt it, he went quiet and changed the subject. Once, when a drunk at an inn wouldn't stop asking, he got up and walked out into the rain. Edric never asked.

On the road near Estwilde they came across a caravan trying to be rid of a mare. Her rider had died in battle, and since then she had refused every saddle and nearly crushed a handler against a wagon wheel. An old burn scar ran across her hide. Edric knew the breed at a glance, a steppe horse from the same plains as the riders who burned his home, and he bought her against all good sense with every coin he and Ispin had earned that season hunting bandits. Ispin laughed for a week, and they ate very little that month. For days Edric didn't try to ride her. He just stayed near, until she stopped fighting him, then stopped running from him, and in the end let no one else near her reins. They've been inseparable since.

Ispin's road kept crossing his old companions', and so Edric came to know [[Becklin Uth Viharin]], a Knight of the Crown, **Cudgel**, a mercenary with a famous grin, and Becklin's squire [[Darrett Highwater]], a year or two younger than him, who became a fast friend over long rides and shared drills.

### Ispin's Death

It started on the last few miles into Kalaman at dusk, not long before the end, when five riders came out of the trees on horses that made no sound. Under the rusted helms there was nothing but bone, and every shield bore the same badge, a single black rose. Edric had never been so afraid. Ispin fought as Edric had never seen him fight, and afterwards stood over the bones for a long time, then picked up a shield, looked at the rose, threw it in the ditch and said they should get on. He never explained.

Soon after, in Kalaman, Ispin said Edric was ready to seek his knighthood, and announced that he himself was going home to Vogler to "retire": no orders from the Order, just old friends like Becklin and the paths of his youth, and, almost in passing, a few things he wanted to look into. He wouldn't say what. He refused Edric's offer to come, kindly but firmly. Edric had his own trials ahead of him in **Maelgoth**, and he had never once won an argument with Ispin. It was the last time he saw him alive.

So Edric rode a hundred and fifty miles west to Maelgoth, spent the winter in the knights' yards, and at the turn of the year swore the Oath before the Council of Maelgoth and rode out a Knight of the Crown. He was back in Kalaman when word came that Ispin had been found dead in the woods near Vogler, killed by some beast down from the Northern Wastes, people said. It was easy to believe, since people go missing up there all the time, and the Order accepted it without question. They had never taken Ispin seriously anyway, with his tall tales and hedge-knight ways. To most of them he was a joke in armour.

Edric couldn't accept it. After fourteen years at Ispin's side, he knew what the Order never saw. Ispin played the fool so well that people forgot to be afraid of him, and he was the most dangerous man in any fight Edric ever saw. Edric is fairly sure he lost more than one tourney on purpose: once to avoid a seat at the high table beside the most boring lord in Solamnia, and once, Edric worked out afterwards, because he'd bet a month's wages on the other man. No beast from the Wastes would have caught him by surprise. And he had gone to Vogler to look into something.

Becklin had loved Ispin too, in the way old companions do, and her letters from Vogler were full of grief and the same doubts. The wounds were wrong, she wrote, and so was the story. Between them they made a plan. Becklin had been asked to escort a pair of Vogler nobles home from their river barge for the Kingfisher Festival, and Edric volunteered to ride with her. Now they're on the road together. The funeral is in Vogler, and so is whatever Ispin went looking for.

## Personality

Edric is the one people lean on. He never decides to be, he just finds himself checking on the wounded and settling the argument and counting heads before the march, making sure nobody gets left behind. In any group, he ends up being the one holding it together.

People tend to like him. He has an easy, open charm that he doesn't seem to know about, the kind that makes innkeepers give him the good room and frightened recruits stand a little straighter. And when things are at their worst, when the line is breaking and everyone is looking at everyone else, Edric is the one who climbs up on the cart and starts talking. He picked that up from Ispin, along with the habit of not quite knowing what he's going to say until he says it. Somehow it usually works.

Inside, he's a young man trying very hard to be what everyone needs him to be. He looks calm because someone has to. He has endless patience for other people, especially the young and the frightened, and almost none for himself, and he still isn't sure whether he deserves to be called a knight or is only copying the men and women he admired.

So he holds on to the Order's ideals with both hands. The Oath and the Measure, the virtues of the Crown, the old codes most knights only half remember, Edric knows them all by heart, because they tell him what a good knight does when he can't trust himself to know. And he means to rise. Ispin was never given the respect he deserved, and Edric has quietly decided that he will earn it for both of them. He'll climb the ranks and become the great knight everyone said Ispin never was, so that one day they'll have to admit who trained him. He doesn't see the irony, that the man he's honouring broke every rule in the Measure and laughed about it.

## The Oath and the Measure

> [!module] Source: **Module** ([[02-chapter-1-character-creation#The Oath and the Measure|ch. 1, "The Oath and the Measure"]] and "Knights of the Crown"). The rules under "What binds him" are **Homebrew** readings of the Measure, for this campaign.

**The Oath.** *Est Sularus oth Mithas*, "My honour is my life." Every knight swears it, and it was never meant as a rule. It's a star to steer by, and when the rulebook runs out, it's what's left.

**The Measure.** The rulebook. Thirty-seven volumes on how a knight must live, from how to address a lord to how to saddle a horse. No Grand Master has been elected in generations, so nobody has the authority to change a word of it. Edric has read more of it than most knights alive. A growing number of knights want it reformed, and he hasn't decided which side he's on.

**What he swore at Maelgoth,** as a Knight of the Crown, before the Council:
- **The Oath**, on his own sword.
- **Loyalty** to the Order, and **obedience** to the lawful orders of a superior knight.
- **To aid** any knight who asks it of him, and any land on the **List of Loyalty**, the Order's roll of its allies.
- **To protect** those who can't protect themselves.

**What binds him,** in practice:
- **A knight's word binds him.** An oath freely sworn can't be broken without dishonour. That includes the one he swore to Vendri in the cells.
- **A knight doesn't leave the field while anyone in his charge is still on it.** That's what the desertion charge says he did. To a knight, nothing worse can be said about him.
- **A knight accused of cowardice answers to his peers.** Only a council of knights can judge a knight's honour. Leaving a city's charge unanswered is a stain of its own, and if the chapterhouse hears of it, they may want to judge him themselves.
- **A knight doesn't lie.** He may keep silent.
- **Quarter is given to any enemy who yields, and a parley is sacred.**
- **Kalaman isn't on the List of Loyalty.** The city struck itself off after the Cataclysm. The Order owes it nothing, and a Knight of the Crown sworn to its marshal is something the Measure never imagined.

Ispin broke half of these rules, laughing. Whenever the Measure and the people in front of him want different things, the player decides which one Edric serves, and the other one remembers.

## Playing Edric

Ideas, not rules. Use what fits.
- **Quote the Measure,** volume and chapter, and then do the right thing anyway. "Volume nine says I shouldn't. Hold my horse."
- **Count heads** out loud after every fight. If someone's missing, he goes back.
- **Talk to the mare** when he can't talk to people. At first she's the only one he tells the truth to.
- **Two oaths.** Salute Vendri like a soldier, then look things up in the Measure in private. The strain between the Order and the company is his best material.
- **Ispin's stories.** When morale is low, tell one of Ispin's tall tales, slightly wrong, and let someone correct him.
- **With the others.** The sorcerer is everything the Measure warns about, and good company. The dragonborn obeys anyone with rank, Edric included. Refusing to give him orders is a scene in itself.
- **Look for** a speech from the back of a cart, a duel he shouldn't accept, and an order that would get someone killed if he obeyed it.

## Unique Mechanics

### Knightly Orders

The Order he serves shapes the character further, through feats you can swap in D&D Beyond.

| Order | Focus / Flavor |
|-------|----------------|
| Order of the Crown | Traditional, honor-bound knights; loyalty, justice, large-scale command. |
| Order of the Sword | Combat specialists; valor-focused, frontline-oriented. |
| Order of the Rose | Courtly and diplomatic knights; focus on noble protection, ceremonies, and investigation. |

### Downtime: Raising a Company

In Kalaman, Edric can spend downtime drilling refugees and new recruits into soldiers. For each workweek, make a Charisma (Persuasion) or Wisdom (Insight) check and tell the DM the result. Over time, the people he trains become *his*, and what they can do grows with them.
