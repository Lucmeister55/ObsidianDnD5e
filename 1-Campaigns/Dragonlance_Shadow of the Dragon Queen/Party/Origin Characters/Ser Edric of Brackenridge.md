## Appearance

Edric looks older than his twenty-six years. The road and the grief have put lines around his eyes early. He's tall and broad-shouldered, with straw-blond hair swept back from a weathered face, a long drooping moustache he is privately rather proud of, and pale blue eyes that are usually looking at someone else to see if they're all right. He wears his knight's plate polished bright, trimmed in gold over a deep blue tabard, with a grey-blue cloak thrown over one shoulder and pinned with the badge of the Order. He smells, faintly and always, of horse.

## Backstory

### Brackenridge

Edric grew up in **Brackenridge**, a frontier village on the trade roads of eastern Solamnia, where the land begins to climb toward the Estwilde and the far peaks of the Taman Busuk. By the standards of the age it was a peaceful place, remote and poor but quiet enough that the wagons kept coming.

His father, **Darian Vellcrest**, was the village cooper and wagonwright, the man who mended the carts and barrels that kept Brackenridge alive and fitted out every caravan that stopped there before the mountain roads. He was careful by nature, and his favourite saying was "prepare for the worst, even on good days." His mother, **Melia**, was the village healer, a calm woman with herb-stained hands who patched up the victims of fever and famine and raiders alike, and her saying was a gentler one: "people endure longer than they think they can."

From his father Edric learned horses. He would spend whole days in the stalls with the draft animals and find a calm there that he never found anywhere else. From his mother he learned poultices and bandages, and how to keep a wounded man breathing until help arrived.

In **325 AC**, when Edric was twelve, the riders came out of **Khur**, steppe raiders as fast as they were merciless, and Brackenridge burned in a single night. His father died in the street trying to hold them back. His mother died over Edric, shielding him from a volley of arrows. By morning there was no village left.

The raiders kept the boy alive, roped in a line with the other captives to be sold as slaves in Khur, but they never got that far. A company of Solamnic Knights under [[Ispin Greenshield|Ser Ispin Greenshield]] rode them down and cut the prisoners loose, and Edric watched them do it with discipline in the middle of the chaos, with mercy that wasn't weakness, spending their strength on strangers. He had never seen anything like it. Afterwards he went to find Ispin and asked to serve him. Ispin said no. He was an odd, wandering sort of knight who had never wanted a squire and never expected to take one, and he told the boy so. So Edric followed him anyway. He trailed after Ispin's horse for days, sleeping at the edge of his campfire, mending his tack before he woke, and asking again every single morning, until one morning Ispin sighed, handed him the reins, and stopped saying no.

### Squire to Ispin

He trained as though his life depended on it, because he wanted to become the kind of man who could stop the next village from burning. It never quite quieted the voice that told him he had failed the first one. Under that voice there was an older anger, too, one he could never aim at anyone. Why had his parents been made to suffer, and where had the gods been that night? He never got an answer, and in time he stopped expecting one.

Ispin was no ordinary knight. He trained his squire on the road instead of in castle yards, so they slept under open skies and ate around campfires and only saw the inside of a town when they needed supplies. They lived more like hedge knights than Knights of Solamnia, and Edric learned more on those roads than any hall could have taught him.

And Ispin himself was a character. He told a new story every night by the fire, each one more outrageous than the last: the minotaur pirates who chased him across the Blood Sea for three days, the night he spent in a roc's nest, the time a gnome hired him to fetch a weasel she had launched from Mount Nevermind all the way to Sancrist. Edric never knew how much of any of it was true, and Ispin never told him. There was only one subject Ispin would never tell a story about. Whenever anyone mentioned the Dargaard Mountains, or the black ruin of Dargaard Keep, or the whispered legend of the Dread Knight said to haunt its ruins, Ispin would go quiet and change the subject, and once, when a drunk at an inn wouldn't stop asking, he simply got up and walked out into the rain. Edric never asked. He called every stranger "old friend" and every innkeeper "my dear". He carried a round, mossy-green shield carved with a scarred broadleaf tree, which he called Old Moss, talked to in a crisis, and claimed had been given to him by a unicorn in Darken Wood. He sang badly and constantly, and he could not cook to save his life, so by the end of his first year as a squire Edric was doing all the cooking. Once, thrown in a cell in Palanthas for shaving the Lord of Palanthas's prize poodles, Ispin talked his way out with nothing but a story and a borrowed lute, and when Edric asked him how, he only winked and said that a good tale is worth more than a good sword, most days. He had one other saying, the only one he ever said without smiling: "An order's only worth following if it serves the people it's meant to protect." He said his father had told him that, and his grandfather before that.

It was on the road near Estwilde that they came across the mare. A caravan was trying to get rid of her. Her rider had died in battle, and ever since she had refused every saddle, kicked anyone who came close, and once nearly crushed a handler to death against a wagon wheel. An old burn scar ran across her hide. Edric knew the breed at a glance, a steppe horse from the same eastern plains as the riders who had burned his home, and where anyone else would have sent her to the knacker, he bought her on the spot against all good sense, with every coin he and Ispin had earned that season hunting down bandits on the roads. Ispin laughed about it for a week, and they ate very little that month. For days he didn't even try to ride her. He just stayed near. Slowly she stopped fighting him, and then stopped running from him, until in the end he was the only person alive she would let near her reins. They've been inseparable ever since.

Ispin's road kept crossing the paths of his old adventuring companions, and that was how Edric came to know [[Becklin Uth Viharin]], a Knight of the Crown, and **Cudgel**, a mercenary with a famous grin, and Becklin's squire, [[Darrett Highwater]]. He and Darrett were the same age, and they became fast friends over long rides and shared drills.

### Ispin's Death

It started on the road, not long before the end. They were riding the last few miles into Kalaman at dusk when riders came out of the trees, five of them on horses that made no sound. Under the rusted helms there was nothing but bone, and on every shield and every tattered surcoat was the same badge, a single black rose. Edric had never been so afraid in his life. Ispin fought like Edric had never seen him fight, and when it was over and the bones lay scattered in the road, he stood over them for a long time without a word. Then he picked up one of the shields, looked at the rose, threw it in the ditch and said they should get on. He never would explain it.

Not long after, in Kalaman, Ispin told Edric he was ready to seek his knighthood, and then announced that he himself was going home to Vogler to "retire". It wasn't an assignment from the Order. He said he wanted to see old friends like Becklin again and walk the paths of his youth, and then he mentioned, almost in passing, that there were a few things he wanted to look into while he was there. He wouldn't say what. The remark stuck with Edric.

Edric asked to come along, and Ispin refused him kindly but firmly. It was a personal errand to a sleepy village, he said, and Edric had his own trials ahead of him. Edric didn't argue, because he had never once been able to argue with Ispin, and it was the last time he saw him alive.

Not long after, Edric passed his test of knighthood. A few weeks later word reached Kalaman that Ispin had been found dead in the woods near Vogler, killed by some beast down from the Northern Wastes, people said. It was easy to believe. Everyone knows the Wastes beyond Vogler are deadly, and people go missing up there all the time. The Order accepted the story without question. They had never taken Ispin very seriously anyway, with his tall tales and his hedge-knight ways and his cheerful disregard for every tradition they held dear. To most of them he was a joke in armour.

Edric couldn't accept it. He had spent fourteen years at Ispin's side, and he knew what the Order never bothered to see. Ispin played the fool so well that people forgot to be afraid of him, and then he was the most dangerous man in any fight Edric ever saw, in any tourney or battle or brawl. Edric is fairly sure Ispin lost more than one tourney on purpose. Once it was because the champion's prize was a seat at the high table beside the most boring lord in Solamnia, and Ispin would sooner take a lance to the face than sit through that dinner. Another time, Edric only worked out afterwards, it was because Ispin had quietly bet a month's wages on the other man. No beast from the Wastes would have caught him by surprise. And he had gone to Vogler to look into something.

He wasn't the only one. Becklin had loved Ispin too, in the way old companions do, and her letters from Vogler were full of grief, and of the same doubts Edric couldn't put into words. The wounds were wrong, she wrote, and so was the story. Between them, over a handful of letters, they made a plan. Becklin had been asked to escort a pair of Vogler nobles home from their river barge for the Kingfisher Festival, and Edric volunteered to ride with her. Now they are on the road together. The funeral is in Vogler, and so is whatever Ispin went looking for.

## Personality

Edric is the one people lean on. He never decides to be, he just finds himself checking on the wounded and settling the argument and counting heads before the march, making sure nobody gets left behind. In any group, he ends up being the one holding it together.

People tend to like him. He has an easy, open charm that he doesn't seem to know about, the kind that makes innkeepers give him the good room and frightened recruits stand a little straighter. And when things are at their worst, when the line is breaking and everyone is looking at everyone else, Edric is the one who climbs up on the cart and starts talking. He picked that up from Ispin, along with the habit of not quite knowing what he's going to say until he says it. Somehow it usually works.

Inside, he's a young man trying very hard to be what everyone needs him to be. He looks calm because someone has to. He has endless patience for other people, especially the young and the frightened, and almost none for himself, and he still isn't sure whether he deserves to be called a knight or is only copying the men and women he admired.

So he holds on to the Order's ideals with both hands. The Oath and the Measure, the virtues of the Crown, the old codes most knights only half remember, Edric knows them all by heart, because they tell him what a good knight does when he can't trust himself to know. And he means to rise. Ispin was never given the respect he deserved, and Edric has quietly decided that he will earn it for both of them. He'll climb the ranks and become the great knight everyone said Ispin never was, so that one day they'll have to admit who trained him. He doesn't see the irony, that the man he's honouring broke every rule in the Measure and laughed about it.
