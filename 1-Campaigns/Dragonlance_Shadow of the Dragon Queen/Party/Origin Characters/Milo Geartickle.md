> [!abstract] Milo Geartickle
> **Tinkerer** · **Fearless** · **Just Browsing**
> 
> - **You are** Milo Geartickle, 31, kender, tinkerer and self-appointed archaeologist. You don't steal relics. You rescue them, for safekeeping, indefinitely.
> - **You were** in a kender resistance band until it got boring. Since then it's been Solamnia, a gnome's workshop, a great many locked doors, and a bronze dragon in the Northern Wastes that nobody believes you saw. A little drone followed you home from up there, and nobody can tell you what it is. You also wear an iron ring that won't come off. As far as you can tell, it won't let you die either. You've checked.
> - **Now** you're in Vogler, because the famous Ispin Greenshield's shield is here, and you would very much like to hold it.
> - **You love** relics, old stories, locks, gadgets, and anything you haven't seen yet.
> - **You can't stand** closed doors, boredom, people who won't let you look, and the Dragonarmies, who melt down ruins older than their grandmothers' grandmothers for scrap.
> - **Play them** cheerful, nosy and fearless. Ask too many questions, pocket things without noticing, and have one mad idea after another. Every so often, one of them is brilliant.

## Appearance

Milo is small even for a kender, round-faced and freckled, with pointed ears and a grin so wide it seems to take up most of their head. Their dark hair is piled up in a messy topknot, with one long braid looping out of it and fastened with a bright bead, and it's usually got something stuck in it, a twig, a pencil, a lockpick. They're wrapped in an enormous mustard-yellow scarf striped with teal, which they never take off and which almost certainly has things hidden in it. Pouches hang off them everywhere. The little drone is never far from their shoulder. And on one finger, always, there's a plain iron ring.

## Backstory

### Kendermore

Milo was born in **Kendermore**, where nobody draws much of a line between a story, a road and a thing you found on the road. Like most kender they had little sense of what belonged to whom and less interest in staying put. What made Milo different was what they loved. Other kender collected shiny things, but Milo collected old things, relics and battlefields and half-forgotten histories, out of fascination rather than greed. Every object seemed to have a story in it, and every story seemed worth keeping.

Their best friend was **Elgo Duckditcher**, and the two of them tumbled around the Goodlund Peninsula together. When the Black Dragon Army occupied Goodlund and Balifor, both joined a kender resistance band, less an army than a cheerful mess of raids, scouting mishaps and opportunistic sabotage. Milo stayed because Elgo cared, a great deal, and Milo didn't want to let him down. They turned out to be very good at it, slipping through lines and spotting paths nobody else noticed, without ever quite meaning to. In the end it was the routine that did it. Memorising patrols and staying alert every day stopped feeling like an adventure and started feeling like a cage, and once Milo stopped worrying about disappointing Elgo, they drifted off with a cheerful remark down whichever road looked most interesting.

### On the Road

There was no big farewell. A short wander turned into a very long one, and Milo ended up in **Solamnia**, mostly by chance, after getting across the **Taman Busuk Mountains** through what can only be called luck. They survived the kender way, by "borrowing", and keep a loose list of everyone they owe, which they fully intend to pay back someday. Without noticing, they also picked up a reputation for slipping into private collections and small museums to examine interesting artifacts, and sometimes to take them away for closer study.

All that borrowing, and every archive rifled and every conversation overheard in inns and war camps, made Milo one of the best-informed people in the region. They have an instinctive grasp of history, politics and old Solamnic lore that came from collecting rather than studying, and they understand machines the same way: locks, traps, siege engines and strange relic-machines make sense the moment they get their hands on them, without a day of schooling. In Milo's own mind they've never been a thief. They're an archaeologist, a rescuer of relics. Wonderful old things shouldn't rot in tombs or gather dust in some lord's cabinet, and someone has to study them properly and keep them safe. That the relics usually end up in Milo's pockets is a matter of safekeeping.

In Kalaman, for a while, they studied with **Tatina Rookledust**, a gnomish inventor whose workshop was a glorious maze of half-working devices and ideas that were only *almost* dangerous.

### The Ring

In the sealed strongroom of a ruined manor in the hills of Estwilde, Milo found a plain iron ring lying alone on a stone shelf. The looters before them had touched everything else in the room and left the ring alone, and Milo didn't wonder why. It seemed to want to be picked up, and then to be worn, and Milo had never said no to anything that interesting.

It hasn't come off since. Soap, grease, string and one very determined blacksmith have all failed, and when anyone pulls on it, it tightens. Milo gave up after a week, because by then they'd found out what it does. Cuts close in an hour, and a broken arm knits overnight, and when a rotten floor dropped them forty feet onto rock, they lay dead for a while, then got up, brushed themselves off, and went looking for the way out.

Even by kender standards Milo was always a bit much, and the ring took away whatever caution was left. They treat danger as a suggestion and death as a short nap. They've walked into trapped tombs to see how the traps worked, eaten mushrooms nobody could name, and once climbed into a gnomish invention described to them, accurately, as a bomb. The ring has brought them back every time. Milo thinks it's the best thing that ever happened to them, and has never once wondered why a ring would want them alive.

### The Wastes

Eventually Milo wandered off again, up into the Northern Wastes. They don't remember much of it. They forget plenty of things, but this is different, a whole stretch of their life missing like a page torn from a book. What's left comes in pieces: days that blistered the skin and nights cold enough to crack stone, canyons that weren't where they had been the day before, and the Wash, the great salt flood that roared through the canyons without warning and left them bone-dry a week later. Somewhere in it there was a forge deep in a canyon wall where the floods couldn't reach, and someone with very large, patient hands who taught them to mend things properly. There were people up there, more than anyone in the south would believe, and when Milo tries to tell anyone, they get the same look as when Milo mentions the dragon.

That memory, at least, is perfectly clear. An enormous dragon passed overhead, its wings flashing bronze in the sun like living metal, and the storm winds parted around it. Milo has told the story in every tavern from there to Kalaman, and people always laugh, because everyone knows the good dragons left the world long ago, if they were ever there at all.

The drone found Milo up there too. One morning it was simply hovering outside their tent, a small construct that clearly thinks for itself and talks in little motions and patterns of light, and it has never left. No dwarven engineer, gnomish artificer or inventor Milo has shown it to can say who made it or why. However lost Milo gets, or however thoroughly they blow themselves up, the drone always finds them again, and hovers by their right hand as if checking they're all still there.

### Vogler

Which is how Milo comes to be in **Vogler** as the village gathers for [[Ispin Greenshield|Ser Ispin Greenshield's]] funeral. They've heard all about Ispin's legendary shield, and would very much like to see it up close, sketch it, measure it, and maybe borrow it for a little while to study the details. It hasn't occurred to them that this might be theft, or in poor taste with the man barely in the ground.

Milo doesn't care much about kings, wars or the Dragon Queen. What they care about is the stories on the road of Dragonarmy soldiers digging up ancient ruins, smashing whatever they can't use and melting priceless old wonders down for metal, which is unforgivable. And if there's going to be a war anyway, someone ought to write it down properly. Heroes need a chronicler, and Milo would very much like to be in the room when the legends get made.

## Personality

Milo is bright and nosy and cheerful, and nothing short of a locked door will discourage them for long, and even then not for long. They ask questions constantly, usually before it would be polite to, and they walk into danger with an alarming lack of fear, partly because they're a kender and partly because the ring has never let them stay dead. The curiosity isn't an act. It's the engine of their whole life.

Milo's mind never sits still. They hop from one wild idea to the next before anyone has caught up with the first, and they abandon half-built plans mid-sentence because something shinier has wandered past. In a party full of grim, driven people, Milo is the one who has forgotten what the mission was and is now very excited about a beetle. They're not stupid, though. Their ideas are mad, but a surprising number of them work, and every so often, in the middle of the nonsense, they say the one thing nobody else had thought of and then carry on talking about the beetle.

## Playing Milo

Ideas, not rules. Use what fits.
- **Pockets.** Once a session, produce something from a pocket that Milo shouldn't have, with no idea how it got there.
- **Ask the question** everyone else is too polite to ask, at the worst possible moment.
- **Fearless, not foolish.** Kender don't feel fear, so Milo walks up to the dragon for a better look. Let the others drag them back.
- **The drone.** Talk to it like a pet. Give it opinions, and argue with them.
- **The ring.** Fiddle with it while thinking. Never quite take it off.
- **Taunts.** When Milo taunts an enemy, say the insult out loud. Specific is funnier.
- **With the others.** The dragonborn is the most interesting person Milo has ever met, so ask him everything. The elf is a wonderful grump, and won't say where he's from, which only makes it more fun.
- **Being a suspect** is the most exciting thing that's happened to Milo all year. Tell people about it.

## Unique Mechanics

### The Drone

A Tiny construct the size of a fist that follows Milo everywhere. It flies, stays within 60 feet of them, can't be made to leave, and can't attack. It communicates only in motions and patterns of light, and no one has ever managed to read them.

**Tinkering.** Milo is the party's technician, and the drone is their favourite puzzle. During downtime, Milo can spend a workweek taking it apart and putting it back together. At the end of the week, make an Intelligence check using tinker's tools. Tell the DM the result, and they'll let you know if you've made progress. Enough progress unlocks new things the drone can do, and maybe tells you something about where it came from.

### The Iron Ring

Works as a *Ring of Regeneration*, with these changes:
- Milo is permanently attuned. The attunement can't be ended, and the ring can't be removed by any means short of *remove curse* (and maybe not even then).
- It keeps working at 0 hit points. Milo doesn't make death saving throws. After 10 minutes at 0 hit points, they regain 1d6 hit points and get back up.
- Only a death that leaves nothing to heal (disintegration, for example) can kill Milo for good.
- Lost body parts regrow as normal for the ring.
