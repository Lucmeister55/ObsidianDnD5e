# Weapon Triangle

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**, built on the 2024 Weapon Mastery rules.

**The point:** a fighter should have a reason to carry more than one weapon, and to look at what the enemy is holding before choosing theirs. The 2024 rules already make swapping cheap (you can equip or unequip one weapon as part of each attack), and variant encumbrance makes carrying a whole armoury expensive. The triangle makes that choice matter.

## The Rule

Fire Emblem style: **swords beat axes, axes beat spears, spears beat swords.** Every melee weapon belongs to one of three **families**:

| Family | Weapons | Beats | Because |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blades** | dagger, shortsword, scimitar, rapier, sickle, longsword, greatsword | **Hafted** | Quick enough to strike inside a heavy swing. |
| **Hafted** | club, greatclub, handaxe, battleaxe, greataxe, light hammer, warhammer, maul, mace, morningstar, flail, war pick | **Polearms** | Smashes past the point and breaks the shaft. |
| **Polearms** | spear, javelin (melee), quarterstaff, trident, glaive, halberd, lance, pike | **Blades** | Reach keeps the swordsman out. |

**When your weapon's family beats the family of the weapon your target is wielding, you have a +2 bonus to attack rolls against it.** That's the whole rule. It works both ways on its own, so the enemy gets the same bonus against you when their weapon beats yours.

- A creature wielding two families uses the one it attacked with most recently.
- Natural weapons (claws, bites, tails), unarmed strikes, ranged weapons, spells and whips are **outside the triangle**: no bonus either way.
- The triangle is for heroes. Units in [[Skirmish Battles]] use their own troop types instead.

## Why It's Interesting Here

The Dragon Army is mostly **blades**, which means hafted weapons have the edge against it, and the Kalaman garrison's longswords have no edge at all. That's a story the players can discover and act on:

| Enemy | Weapon | Beat it with |
|---|---|---|
| Baaz draconian | shortsword (blade) | **Hafted**, and see Death Throes below |
| Kapak draconian | dagger (blade) | **Hafted** |
| Sivak draconian | serrated sword (blade) | **Hafted** |
| Bozak draconian | trident (polearm) | **Blades** |
| Dragon Army soldier | longsword (blade) | **Hafted** |
| Dragon Army officer | vicious lance (polearm) | **Blades** |
| Hobgoblin warrior | longsword (blade) | **Hafted** |
| Goblin warrior | scimitar (blade) | **Hafted** |
| Kalaman soldier, knight | longsword or greatsword (blade) | (allies) Vulnerable to hafted enemies like ogres |

**Draconian Death Throes** add a second reason to change weapons. A baaz turns to stone when it dies and can trap the weapon that killed it. Rule it simply: a **blade or polearm** that kills a baaz is stuck until someone makes a DC 13 Strength check as an action. **Hafted** weapons and ranged attacks never get stuck. A kapak's acid and a bozak's explosion punish anyone who kills them up close, which rewards reach and ranged weapons.

## Where Each Weapon Shines

Some weapons are just worse versions of others under the normal rules: the club next to the mace, the spear next to the trident. Here, every weapon has **a place where it's the best tool going**. There's nothing to track: the **scene** decides. When the party walks into one of these situations, say so ("You're in the Wash up to your chest, so trident rules apply"), and let the players reach for the right weapon.

| Situation | Shines | Effect | Suffers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Underwater** (the Wash, the Blue Phoenix Shrine, a sunk hold) | **Harpoon** (see below), trident, spear, javelin, dagger, crossbow | No penalty, and a harpoon or trident hit can **reel the target 10 feet** toward you. | Every other weapon attacks with **disadvantage**. Bludgeoning deals half damage. |
| **Tight spaces** (tunnels 5 feet wide, crypts, ship holds, the citadel's foundations) | Dagger, shortsword, club, handaxe, light hammer, sickle | **+2 to attack rolls.** | Two-handed and reach weapons attack with **disadvantage**. |
| **On the walls** (siege ladders, ramparts, cliff edges) | Glaive, halberd, pike, spear, quarterstaff | A hit can **shove the target off the ladder or edge** (Strength save against your attack's damage, minimum DC 10). | |
| **Against fliers** (dragonnels, kapaks gliding, the death dragons swooping) | Javelin, pike, lance, crossbow | On a hit, the target makes a **DC 13 Strength save** or drops 10 feet, or is **unseated** if mounted. | |
| **Mounted** (Edric's mare, borrowed dragonnels) | Lance, spear, longsword, flail | **+1d6 damage** if the mount moved at least 20 feet this turn. | Two-handed weapons other than the lance have **disadvantage**. |
| **Bone and stone** (skeletons, death dragons, Soth's skeletal knights, baaz statues) | Club, greatclub, mace, maul, warhammer, morningstar | **+1d4 bludgeoning damage.** | Piercing weapons deal half damage. |
| **Taking prisoners** | Club, quarterstaff, unarmed strike | A creature dropped to 0 is **knocked out, never killed**. Knocked-out draconians **don't trigger Death Throes**, and can be bound and questioned. The Military pays a bounty for live ones. | |
| **Breaking things** (doors, barricades, ballistae, Istarian drones, golems) | Greatclub, maul, war pick, greataxe | **Double damage** to objects and constructs. | Blades deal half damage to objects. |
| **Bad footing** (ice, the Wash flats, floating rocks, a deck in a storm) | Quarterstaff, spear | **Advantage** on saves and checks against falling prone or losing your footing. | |
| **Boarding and rigging** (ship fights, the crossing to the Wastes) | Scimitar, handaxe, dagger, sickle | **Swing on a rope** as part of your move (30 feet, no check). Handaxes and sickles cut grapples and lines as a bonus action. | Reach weapons have disadvantage in the rigging. |
| **In the city** (see the Peace-Tie below) | Club, quarterstaff, sickle, dagger, light hammer, handaxe, sling | **Never peace-tied**, so always to hand. | Martial weapons take an action to free. |
| **In the Wastes** | Sickle, handaxe | **+2 to foraging checks**, and a handaxe makes shelter without a check. | |

**The harpoon** is a new simple weapon, sold by Vogler's fishers (if the Smallfolk are Trusted) and in Kalaman's harbour: 1d8 piercing, thrown (20/60), with 30 feet of line. It counts as a polearm in the triangle. On a hit you can let go, or keep hold of the line and pull: the target makes a Strength save against your attack's damage or is pulled 10 feet toward you. It's the best weapon in the Wash, and a fine way to drag a kapak out of the sky.

### The Peace-Tie

Kalaman is a city at war, and armed strangers make it nervous. **Inside Kalaman's walls, in castles, temples and noble houses, and anywhere the Military is Wary or worse,** martial weapons must be peace-tied, strapped into their scabbard or slings. Drawing one takes an action, and it draws the guards. **Simple weapons that are also tools** (club, quarterstaff, sickle, dagger, light hammer, handaxe, sling) are never peace-tied. They're a walking stick, a farm tool, a knife. When the sivaks strike in the castle at night, the one carrying a club is the one who's armed.

## Optional Layer: Armour

*Try this only once the basic triangle feels natural.*

| Damage type | Against heavy armour | Against light or no armour |
|---|---|---|
| **Bludgeoning** | +2 damage | normal |
| **Piercing** | normal | normal |
| **Slashing** | -2 damage (minimum 1) | +2 damage |

A creature counts as wearing heavy armour if its stat block lists plate, splint, chain mail or ring mail (Dragon Army soldiers, officers, Kalaman soldiers, knights). Draconian scales count as light.

## Things to Watch

- Do fighters actually carry a second weapon, and switch it mid-fight?
- Does +2 feel noticeable without being mandatory? If nobody notices, raise it to advantage on the first attack each turn. If it feels mandatory, drop it to +1.
- Is anyone tracking it besides the DM? Mark each enemy's family on the initiative tracker ("Baaz [B]") to keep it visible.
- Do players start packing for the situation ("we're going into tunnels, bring the short stuff")? That's the goal of "Where Each Weapon Shines". If they don't, foreshadow the next location's situation a session early.
- Does the Monk or Rogue feel left out? Unarmed strikes sit outside the triangle on purpose, so their reliability is the payoff.
