# Combat Extras

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. Small combat mechanics that are each too small for a note of their own, bundled together: the weapon triangle, combo attacks, and slopes and high ground. The weapon triangle is built on the 2024 Weapon Mastery rules, and combos are a round-by-round version of the "one-turn" combo play in [[Session 1.5]].

| Extra | What it's for |
|---|---|
| [[#Weapon Triangle]] | Choosing a weapon by the enemy and the job: swords beat axes, axes beat spears, spears beat swords, plus a niche for every weapon. |
| [[#Combo Attacks]] | Rewarding the party for fighting together: two or more characters spend their actions on one move. |
| [[#Slopes and High Ground]] | Making terrain matter: a charge downhill hits harder, climbing is slow, and an archer on a hill shoots better. |

## Weapon Triangle

**The point:** a fighter should have a reason to carry more than one weapon, and to look at what the enemy is holding before choosing theirs. The 2024 rules already make swapping cheap (you can equip or unequip one weapon as part of each attack), and variant encumbrance makes carrying a whole armoury expensive. The triangle makes that choice matter.

### The Rule

Fire Emblem style: **swords beat axes, axes beat spears, spears beat swords.** Every melee weapon belongs to one of three **families**:

| Family | Weapons | Beats | Because |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blades** | dagger, shortsword, scimitar, rapier, sickle, longsword, greatsword | **Hafted** | Quick enough to strike inside a heavy swing. |
| **Hafted** | club, greatclub, handaxe, battleaxe, greataxe, light hammer, warhammer, maul, mace, morningstar, flail, war pick | **Polearms** | Smashes past the point and breaks the shaft. |
| **Polearms** | spear, javelin (melee), quarterstaff, trident, glaive, halberd, lance, pike | **Blades** | Reach keeps the swordsman out. |

**When your weapon's family beats the family of the weapon your target is wielding, you have a +2 bonus to attack rolls against it.** That's the whole rule. It works both ways on its own, so the enemy gets the same bonus against you when their weapon beats yours.

- A creature wielding two families uses the one it attacked with most recently.
- Natural weapons (claws, bites, tails), unarmed strikes, ranged weapons, spells and whips are **outside the triangle**: no bonus either way.
- The triangle is for heroes. Units in [[Skirmish Battles]] use their own troop types instead.

### Why It's Interesting Here

The Dragon Army is mostly **blades**, which means **polearms** have the edge against it. The Kalaman garrison's longswords are blades too, so they beat **hafted** enemies (ogres and their clubs) but lose to spears. That's a story the players can discover and act on:

| Enemy | Weapon | Beat it with |
|---|---|---|
| Baaz draconian | shortsword (blade) | **Polearms**, and see Death Throes below |
| Kapak draconian | dagger (blade) | **Polearms** |
| Sivak draconian | serrated sword (blade) | **Polearms** |
| Bozak draconian | trident (polearm) | **Blades** |
| Dragon Army soldier | longsword (blade) | **Polearms** |
| Dragon Army officer | vicious lance (polearm) | **Blades** |
| Hobgoblin warrior | longsword (blade) | **Polearms** |
| Goblin warrior | scimitar (blade) | **Polearms** |
| Kalaman soldier, knight | longsword or greatsword (blade) | (allies) They beat hafted enemies like ogres, and are vulnerable to polearm enemies. |

**Draconian Death Throes** add a second reason to change weapons. A baaz turns to stone when it dies and can trap the weapon that killed it. Rule it simply: a **blade or polearm** that kills a baaz is stuck until someone makes a DC 13 Strength check as an action. **Hafted** weapons and ranged attacks never get stuck. That sets up a choice against baaz: the polearm has the +2, but it can stick, while a hafted weapon is safe but fights at the disadvantage. Take the kill with a ranged attack or a second weapon and nothing sticks. A kapak's acid and a bozak's explosion punish anyone who kills them up close, which rewards reach and ranged weapons.

### Where Each Weapon Shines

Some weapons are just worse versions of others under the normal rules: the club next to the mace, the spear next to the trident. Here, every weapon has **a place where it's the best tool going**. There's nothing to track: the **scene** decides. When the party walks into one of these situations, say so ("You're in the Wash up to your chest, so trident rules apply"), and let the players reach for the right weapon.

| Situation | Shines | Effect | Suffers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Underwater** (the Wash, the Blue Phoenix Shrine, a sunk hold) | **Trident**, spear, javelin, dagger, crossbow | No penalty, and a trident hit can **pull the target 10 feet** toward you (Strength save against your attack's damage). | Every other weapon attacks with **disadvantage**. Bludgeoning deals half damage. |
| **Tight spaces** (tunnels 5 feet wide, crypts, ship holds, the citadel's foundations) | Dagger, shortsword, club, handaxe, light hammer, sickle | **+2 to attack rolls.** | Two-handed and reach weapons attack with **disadvantage**. |
| **On the walls** (siege ladders, ramparts, cliff edges) | Glaive, halberd, pike, spear, quarterstaff | A hit can **shove the target off the ladder or edge** (Strength save against your attack's damage, minimum DC 10). | |
| **Against fliers** (dragonnels, kapaks gliding, the death dragons swooping) | Longbow, shortbow, all crossbows, javelin, pike, lance | **Bows and crossbows** (anything that shoots an arrow or a bolt) deal **+1d6 damage** to a flying or gliding target. **Javelin, pike and lance:** on a hit, the target makes a **DC 13 Strength save** or drops 10 feet, or is **unseated** if mounted. | |
| **Mounted** (Edric's mare, borrowed dragonnels) | Lance, spear, longsword, flail | **+1d6 damage** if the mount moved at least 20 feet this turn. | Two-handed weapons other than the lance have **disadvantage**. |
| **Bone and stone** (skeletons, death dragons, Soth's skeletal knights, baaz statues) | Club, greatclub, mace, maul, warhammer, morningstar | **+1d4 bludgeoning damage.** | Piercing weapons deal half damage. |
| **Taking prisoners** | Club, quarterstaff, unarmed strike | A creature dropped to 0 is **knocked out, never killed**. Knocked-out draconians **don't trigger Death Throes**, and can be bound and questioned. The Military pays a bounty for live ones. | |
| **Breaking things** (doors, barricades, ballistae, Istarian drones, golems) | Greatclub, maul, war pick, greataxe | **Double damage** to objects and constructs. | Blades deal half damage to objects. |
| **Bad footing** (ice, the Wash flats, floating rocks, a deck in a storm) | Quarterstaff, spear | **Advantage** on saves and checks against falling prone or losing your footing. | |
| **Boarding and rigging** (ship fights, the crossing to the Wastes) | Scimitar, handaxe, dagger, sickle | **Swing on a rope** as part of your move (30 feet, no check). Handaxes and sickles cut grapples and lines as a bonus action. | Reach weapons have disadvantage in the rigging. |
| **In the city** (see the Peace-Tie below) | Club, quarterstaff, sickle, dagger, light hammer, handaxe, sling | **Never peace-tied**, so always to hand. | Martial weapons take an action to free. |
| **In the Wastes** | Sickle, handaxe | **+2 to foraging checks**, and a handaxe makes shelter without a check. | |

#### The Peace-Tie

Kalaman is a city at war, and armed strangers make it nervous. **Inside Kalaman's walls, in castles, temples and noble houses, and anywhere the Military is Wary or worse,** martial weapons must be peace-tied, strapped into their scabbard or slings. Drawing one takes an action, and it draws the guards. **Simple weapons that are also tools** (club, quarterstaff, sickle, dagger, light hammer, handaxe, sling) are never peace-tied. They're a walking stick, a farm tool, a knife. When the sivaks strike in the castle at night, the one carrying a club is the one who's armed.

### Optional Layer: Armour

*Try this only once the basic triangle feels natural.*

| Damage type | Against heavy armour | Against light or no armour |
|---|---|---|
| **Bludgeoning** | +2 damage | normal |
| **Piercing** | normal | normal |
| **Slashing** | -2 damage (minimum 1) | +2 damage |

A creature counts as wearing heavy armour if its stat block lists plate, splint, chain mail or ring mail (Dragon Army soldiers, officers, Kalaman soldiers, knights). Draconian scales count as light.

## Combo Attacks

**The point:** the party should be rewarded for fighting *together*, not just next to each other. A combo is two or more characters spending their actions on one move that's better than the sum of its parts.

### The Rule

**Combos are freeform.** On your turn, pitch a combo with one or more allies within 30 feet who can see you and still have their reactions. Usually it's just one. Say what each of you does and what you want out of it. If it makes sense, it happens:
1. Each ally spends their **reaction** to do their part.
2. You take your **action** to do yours.
3. The DM picks an **effect** using the scale below.

The menu further down is **inspiration, not a list to choose from**. It shows the players what kind of thing works, and gives the DM a benchmark for how strong an effect should be.

#### Improvising the Effect

Pick one size, based on how good the idea is and how well it uses the scene:

| Size | When | Pick one or two |
|---|---|---|
| **Small** | It's sensible and it works. | Advantage on the attack · +1d6 damage · the target can't take reactions · move 10 feet free |
| **Big** | It's clever, uses the terrain or both characters' strengths, or makes the table cheer. | Small, plus one of: +2d8 damage · the target is knocked prone, pushed 15 feet, grappled or disarmed · ignores cover and shields · an ally gets a free attack |
| **Wild** | Once a session at most: the best idea of the night. | Big, plus a scene effect: the bridge collapses under the ogre, the dragonnel throws its rider, the whole enemy line breaks and runs (a Morale check in [[Skirmish Battles]]). |

**If it's a stretch,** call for one check from whoever's doing the hard part, at a fair DC (12–16). Even on a failure, everyone still takes their actions, and the combo works as **Small**.

**Three or more people** spend that many reactions, so let it count: a three-person combo is at least **Big**, and a whole-party one is a good candidate for **Wild**.

**Limits:**
- Each character can be part of **one combo per round**, whether they start it or join it.
- The ally's part uses their reaction, so they can't make opportunity attacks or cast reaction spells until their next turn.
- Both of you must be able to do your part. If one of you is grappled, incapacitated or out of reach, it doesn't happen, and nothing is spent.

### Inspiration

Examples to show the players, and benchmarks for the DM. Each of these is roughly **Big**.

| Combo | The ally's part (reaction) | Your part (action) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | Boost you up: they must be Medium or larger and within 5 feet of you. | Leap up to 20 feet as part of the action (no extra movement), then make one melee attack. | The attack has **advantage**, ignores half cover and shields, and deals an **extra 1d8** damage. |
| **Hammer and Anvil** | Shove the target 5 feet toward you, or knock it prone, with a Strength (Athletics) check against its Athletics or Acrobatics. | Attack the target. | If the shove worked, your first attack this turn is a **critical hit on a 19–20**. |
| **Pin and Strike** | Grapple the target, or make an attack that would apply Topple, Push or Slow. | Attack the same target. | If the grapple or mastery worked, the target **can't take reactions** until the start of its next turn, and your attack deals an **extra 1d6**. |
| **Spell-Strike** | Cast a cantrip that deals damage, onto your weapon instead of at a target. | Make a weapon attack. | On a hit, add the cantrip's damage to your attack. Both of you are hit by any rebound (Davos's wild magic loves this). |
| **Covering Fire** | Make one ranged attack at the target. | Move up to your speed; you don't provoke opportunity attacks from that target, then attack it or take the Dash, Disengage or Help action. | If the ranged attack hit, the target has **disadvantage** on its next attack roll. |
| **Distraction** | Make a Charisma (Performance or Deception) check against the target's passive Insight. Insults, juggling and fake surrender are all fine. | Attack the target. | If the check worked, the target is **distracted**: your attack has advantage, and it can't target the distracter until its next turn. |
| **Shield Wall** | Raise a shield beside you; they must be holding one and be within 5 feet of you. | Take the Dodge or Ready action, or attack. | Until the start of your next turn, you both have **+2 AC** and three-quarters cover against ranged attacks from the front. |

**When the players invent something good,** write it into this table with the effect you gave it. Over time, this becomes the party's own list of moves.

### Pairings

A combo takes **two people or more**: players, or players and allied **NPCs**. Usually it's two. These pairings aren't extra rules, just ideas built on who these people are to each other. Drop one as a hint ("Wurm's right there, and he's very strong...") when a player is stuck.

#### With NPCs

An allied NPC can join a combo if they're on the battlefield and it fits what they'd do. You decide on the spot whether they're willing.
- **Cudgel:** she holds a door, a line or an ogre still while someone else does the hitting.
- **Darrett:** he's earnest and a little stiff, and he'll take any knightly two-person manoeuvre Edric suggests. If Wurm taught him to fight, the two of them have their own.
- **Tatina:** a gadget for every occasion, and none of them reliable. Anything with her is automatically **Big**, then roll a d6: on a 1, it goes off in the wrong direction.
- **Clystran:** anything involving dragonnels, ropes or climbing.
- **Vogler militia or Kalaman soldiers:** in a [[Skirmish Battles|skirmish]], a PC can combo with an allied unit. The unit's part is a shove, a volley or a charge, and the PC's attack gets the effect.

## Slopes and High Ground

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. Terrain that rewards players who use it.

**The point:** a hill should be a place players want to hold or avoid. The rules are four lines, and the DM only has to say that a slope is there.

### What counts as a slope

A **slope** is ground steep enough that the scene would call it a hill: a cliff path, a ramp up to a rampart, a canyon wall, a siege mound. Roughly 10 feet of rise over 30 feet of run. A gentle rise is just terrain. There's nothing to track: the DM says "that's a slope, up is that way" when the map has one, and points the arrow on the battlemap.

### The Rules

| Situation | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Moving downhill** | Every foot of downhill movement costs **half a foot** of speed, so you cover **double the distance** down a slope. |
| **Moving uphill** | Every foot of uphill movement costs **2 feet** of speed, so you cover **half the distance** up a slope. |
| **Downhill charge** | If you moved at least **20 feet downhill in a straight line towards the target** this turn, your **first melee weapon hit** this turn deals **one extra damage die** of the weapon's type (a longsword adds 1d8, a greatsword or a handaxe adds 1d6). |
| **High ground, ranged** | **+2 to attack rolls with ranged weapon attacks** while you're at least **10 feet higher** than the target and can see it. |

- **It works both ways.** A Dragon Army soldier charging down at the party gets the same bonus, so the first question when a map has a hill is who is standing at the top.
- **Mounted.** A rider's downhill movement is doubled too. The downhill charge die and the Mounted situation's +1d6 ([[#Where Each Weapon Shines]]) **don't stack**: take whichever is bigger.
- **Difficult terrain** (scree, mud, snow) doesn't stack with the uphill cost: use whichever costs more.
- **Units get slopes too.** The weapon triangle is for heroes, but a unit in [[Skirmish Battles#Slopes and High Ground|a skirmish]] charges downhill for double damage and takes half when it holds the high ground.
- **Spells** are unchanged. The high-ground bonus is for ranged weapon attacks, not spell attacks.

### Where It Comes Up

- **[[1.04 The Battle of High Hill]]:** the villagers start on the hill, so they hold the high ground against the mercenaries below, until the mercenaries pour up it with real spears.
- **[[2.01 Back in Vogler#Encounter: Soldiers on the Cliffs]]:** the clifftop and the slope on the village side. Whoever holds the top shoots down with the bonus.
- **Any map with a hill.** Name the slope when you describe the scene, and tell the players which way is up.

## Things to Watch

- **Weapon Triangle.** Do fighters actually carry a second weapon, and switch it mid-fight?
- **Weapon Triangle.** Does +2 feel noticeable without being mandatory? If nobody notices, raise it to advantage on the first attack each turn. If it feels mandatory, drop it to +1.
- **Weapon Triangle.** Is anyone tracking it besides the DM? Mark each enemy's family on the initiative tracker ("Baaz [B]") to keep it visible.
- **Weapon Triangle.** Do players start packing for the situation ("we're going into tunnels, bring the short stuff")? That's the goal of "Where Each Weapon Shines". If they don't, foreshadow the next location's situation a session early.
- **Weapon Triangle.** Does the Monk or Rogue feel left out? Unarmed strikes sit outside the triangle on purpose, so their reliability is the payoff.
- **Combo Attacks.** Do combos happen naturally, or only when the DM reminds people? If the latter, put the menu on a card in the middle of the table.
- **Combo Attacks.** Is the reaction cost real? If everyone always combos, consider raising it to "the ally also loses 10 feet of movement on their next turn".
- **Combo Attacks.** Spell-Strike and Launch are the strongest entries. Watch them first.
- **Combo Attacks.** Does combat slow down? Each combo should take under a minute to agree. If it takes longer, call it Small and move on.
- **Slopes.** Do players use a hill on purpose (run down it, shoot from the top), or do they ignore it? If nobody does, point at the slope in the first round. Is the downhill charge so strong that the party refuses to fight uphill? If so, cut it to the extra die only on a Strength (Athletics) DC 10 check, or to a flat +1d6.
