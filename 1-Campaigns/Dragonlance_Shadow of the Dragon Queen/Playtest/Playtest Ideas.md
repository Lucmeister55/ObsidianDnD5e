## Introduction Schedule

Every system in the campaign, and the moment it first appears. The rule of thumb: **at most one new system per session**, and never a new one in the same session as a big set piece.

| # | When | New system | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Prologue, from the first scene** ([[0. Premonition]]) | Variant encumbrance | Already switched on in D&D Beyond. Nobody needs to be told yet, and it won't bite until the Wastes. |
| 2 | **Prologue: the festival** ([[1. An Ordinary Day]]) | **Faction Standing**, one track only | Show the villagers' number move once, without explaining the system. |
| 3 | **Prologue: High Hill** | **Objective fights** | Not a new rule, just a new habit: the fight is about the villagers. |
| 4 | **Prologue: the pursuing draconian or the cliffs** ([[2. Broken Silence]]) | **One-Turn Resolution** | The first offer, explained in two sentences at the table. Small and low-stakes, and the players can say no. |
| 5 | **Prologue: the Battle of Vogler** | ***Warriors of Krynn*** | Give it its own session if you can. The wharf fight after it is a plain objective fight. |
| 6 | **Session 1.5**, after the prologue ([[Session 1.5]]) | **The house rules, explained** | The first time the players get it all in writing: encumbrance, factions, large-scale battles, one-turns, last stands, and what they can change about their characters. Everything they met in the prologue gets its name here. |
| 7 | **Kalaman: arrival** ([[3. Storm Clouds]]) | **All six factions** | The big one. Hand out a one-page summary of the six, and nothing else new this session. |
| 8 | **Downtime 1** | **Personal downtime activities** | Each player's own, from their sheet. No Bastion yet. |
| 9 | **Mission 1: Rookledust** | **Mission check-ins** and the **Weapon Triangle** | Check-ins take ten seconds. The goblins are all blades, so let someone notice the hafted edge. |
| 10 | **Downtime 2** | **The Bastion** and **Working a Faction** | The party has just been given the Bastion. Both are choices on the same downtime list. |
| 11 | **Mission 2 or 3** | **Combo Attacks** | Once the party knows each other's tricks. Hint with a pairing. |
| 12 | **Mission 3: the Blockade** | **Supply** (the stolen food) | The word, and the idea that food can be banked for later. No rules yet. |
| 12b | **Mission 4: Missing Scouts** | **Harvesting and Crafting** | The wyrmlings are the first kill worth taking apart. The Hammerstrike Forges can craft what they yield in Downtime 5. |
| 13 | **Steel Springs** | **Skirmish Battles** | The first skirmish is the Inkwater crossing. Wheelwatch before it is a normal fight. |
| 14 | **The catacombs** | **The Deadline** | Soth's taunt. The countdown only starts in the Wastes. |
| 15 | **The Wastes, days 1–3** ([[4. Duty - Survival]]) | **Survival**, in stages | Day 1: Supply, Water and exhaustion only. Day 2: add navigation and the Wastes Day table. Day 3: add the Wash and foraging. |
| 16 | **The Wastes** | **Weapon situations** | Underwater (the Wash, the shrine) and tight spaces (Bluemaw) are where "Where Each Weapon Shines" first matters. |
| 17 | **The siege** ([[6. Conquest]]) | **The Siege Ledger** | Not a new rule, just the payoff of every faction track. |

**Last Stands** can be offered whenever a character falls, even in the prologue, before Session 1.5 has explained them. In the prologue, explain it in the moment.

**Is it paced out enough?** Mostly, yes, but with two crunch points to watch:
- **Arrival in Kalaman** (rows 7–10) puts the full faction system, downtime, check-ins, the triangle and the Bastion within two or three sessions. Spread them as above, and resist adding anything else there.
- **The start of the Wastes** (rows 14–16) stacks the deadline, the crossing set piece and survival together. That's why survival comes in over three days, and why the deadline is only *told* in the catacombs.

Elsewhere there's plenty of room: nothing new arrives between the Wastes and the siege except things the players already know.

## Balance Checks

> [!homebrew] Source: **Homebrew**. Run with the `balance-sim` skill. Results as of October 2026.

| System | Target | Result | Change made |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Factions** | Pleasing everyone: mostly Trusted, none Honoured. Favourites: two or three Honoured, the rest Wary or Hostile. | At -3 to +5, all six were Trusted or better **87%** of the time. | Scale widened to **-5 to +10**, rival costs on almost every choice, projects cost a whole downtime. Now: everyone Trusted only about 9% of the time, and Honoured is rare without focus. |
| **Knights and Mages** | As many chances to move as the others | About a sixth of the options of the Military or Refugees | Three more briefing options. Still worth adding Mages events. |
| **Wastes supply** | Carry the longest water gap, Encumbered but not Heavily | About 10 days carried against gaps of about 4 | Fine. "Travel light" added. |
| **Wastes encounters** | Near the combat budget | 13–15 random fights | Cut to 40% a day (10–12), and most are "fight or avoid" choices. |
| **Deadline** | Some pressure on rest days | About 45 days against 25–30 needed | Left lenient on purpose. |
| **Skirmish clashes** | 2–5 rounds | 4–6 at 4 × HP | HP cut to **3 ×**. Units no longer have disadvantage against heroes. |
| **Weapon Triangle** | Noticeable, not mandatory | +17–22% damage | Fine. |
