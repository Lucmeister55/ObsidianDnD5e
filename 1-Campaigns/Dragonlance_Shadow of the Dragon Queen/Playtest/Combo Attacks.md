# Combo Attacks

*Source: **Homebrew**, a round-by-round version of the "one-turn" combo play in [[Session 1.5]].*

**The point:** the party should be rewarded for fighting *together*, not just next to each other. A combo is two or more characters spending their actions on one move that's better than the sum of its parts.

## The Rule

**Combos are freeform.** On your turn, pitch a combo with one or more allies within 30 feet who can see you and still have their reactions. Usually it's just one. Say what each of you does and what you want out of it. If it makes sense, it happens:
1. Each ally spends their **reaction** to do their part.
2. You take your **action** to do yours.
3. The DM picks an **effect** using the scale below.

The menu further down is **inspiration, not a list to choose from**. It shows the players what kind of thing works, and gives the DM a benchmark for how strong an effect should be.

### Improvising the Effect

Pick one size, based on how good the idea is and how well it uses the scene:

| Size | When | Pick one or two |
|---|---|---|
| **Small** | It's sensible and it works. | Advantage on the attack · +1d6 damage · the target can't take reactions · move 10 feet free |
| **Big** | It's clever, uses the terrain or both characters' strengths, or makes the table cheer. | Small, plus one of: +2d8 damage · the target is knocked prone, pushed 15 feet, grappled or disarmed · ignores cover and shields · an ally gets a free attack |
| **Wild** | Once a session at most: the best idea of the night. | Big, plus a scene effect: the bridge collapses under the ogre, the dragonnel throws its rider, the whole enemy line breaks and runs (a Morale check in [[Skirmish Battles]]). |

**If it's a stretch,** call for one check from whoever's doing the hard part, at a fair DC (12–16). Even on a failure, everyone still takes their actions, and the combo works as **Small**.

**Three or more people** spend that many reactions, so let it count: a three-person combo is at least **Big**, and a whole-party one is a good candidate for **Wild**.

**Limits:**
- Each character can be part of **one combo per round**, whether they start it or join it.
- The ally's part uses their reaction, so they can't make opportunity attacks or cast reaction spells until their next turn.
- Both of you must be able to do your part. If one of you is grappled, incapacitated or out of reach, it doesn't happen, and nothing is spent.

## Inspiration

Examples to show the players, and benchmarks for the DM. Each of these is roughly **Big**.

| Combo | The ally's part (reaction) | Your part (action) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | Boost you up: they must be Medium or larger and within 5 feet of you. | Leap up to 20 feet as part of the action (no extra movement), then make one melee attack. | The attack has **advantage**, ignores half cover and shields, and deals an **extra 1d8** damage. |
| **Hammer and Anvil** | Shove the target 5 feet toward you, or knock it prone, with a Strength (Athletics) check against its Athletics or Acrobatics. | Attack the target. | If the shove worked, your first attack this turn is a **critical hit on a 19–20**. |
| **Pin and Strike** | Grapple the target, or make an attack that would apply Topple, Push or Slow. | Attack the same target. | If the grapple or mastery worked, the target **can't take reactions** until the start of its next turn, and your attack deals an **extra 1d6**. |
| **Spell-Strike** | Cast a cantrip that deals damage, onto your weapon instead of at a target. | Make a weapon attack. | On a hit, add the cantrip's damage to your attack. Both of you are hit by any rebound (Davos's wild magic loves this). |
| **Covering Fire** | Make one ranged attack at the target. | Move up to your speed; you don't provoke opportunity attacks from that target, then attack it or take the Dash, Disengage or Help action. | If the ranged attack hit, the target has **disadvantage** on its next attack roll. |
| **Distraction** | Make a Charisma (Performance or Deception) check against the target's passive Insight. Insults, juggling and fake surrender are all fine. | Attack the target. | If the check worked, the target is **distracted**: your attack has advantage, and it can't target the distracter until its next turn. |
| **Shield Wall** | Raise a shield beside you; they must be holding one and be within 5 feet of you. | Take the Dodge or Ready action, or attack. | Until the start of your next turn, you both have **+2 AC** and three-quarters cover against ranged attacks from the front. |

**When the players invent something good,** write it into this table with the effect you gave it. Over time, this becomes the party's own list of moves.

## Pairings

A combo takes **two people or more**: players, or players and allied **NPCs**. Usually it's two. These pairings aren't extra rules, just ideas built on who these people are to each other. Drop one as a hint ("Wurm's right there, and he's very strong...") when a player is stuck.

### With NPCs

An allied NPC can join a combo if they're on the battlefield and it fits what they'd do. You decide on the spot whether they're willing.
- **Cudgel:** she holds a door, a line or an ogre still while someone else does the hitting.
- **Darrett:** he's earnest and a little stiff, and he'll take any knightly two-person manoeuvre Edric suggests. If Wurm taught him to fight, the two of them have their own.
- **Tatina:** a gadget for every occasion, and none of them reliable. Anything with her is automatically **Big**, then roll a d6: on a 1, it goes off in the wrong direction.
- **Clystran:** anything involving dragonnels, ropes or climbing.
- **Vogler militia or Kalaman soldiers:** in a [[Skirmish Battles|skirmish]], a PC can combo with an allied unit. The unit's part is a shove, a volley or a charge, and the PC's attack gets the effect.

## Things to Watch

- Do combos happen naturally, or only when the DM reminds people? If the latter, put the menu on a card in the middle of the table.
- Is the reaction cost real? If everyone always combos, consider raising it to "the ally also loses 10 feet of movement on their next turn".
- Spell-Strike and Launch are the strongest entries. Watch them first.
- Does combat slow down? Each combo should take under a minute to agree. If it takes longer, call it Small and move on.
