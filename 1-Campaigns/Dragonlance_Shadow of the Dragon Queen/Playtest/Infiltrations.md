# Infiltrations

> [!info] Source: **Community: John Harper, *Blades in the Dark*** ([[John Harper - Blades in the Dark - Core Rulebook.pdf]], ch. 4, "The Score"). The plan, detail, engagement roll and flashbacks are as written. **Homebrew:** Stress cut to 4, the two clocks, and everything else left to normal D&D checks.

**The point:** no hour of planning. The players pick a plan and one detail, one roll decides how it starts, and the clever preparation comes in flashbacks once they see the trouble they're in. Everything inside is ordinary D&D: checks, spells, and combat if it comes to that.

**Use it for** any job where getting in matters more than fighting:
- **Wheelwatch Outpost** (the first time, [[3.11 Mission 5 - Wheelwatch Outpost]]).
- **Bakaris's contract** from the Bakaris house ([[3.10 Downtime 5]]).
- **Hetch Warehouse**, against the Firefishers ([[Kalaman Guilds]]).
- **Breaking someone out of Castle Kalaman** ([[4.03 The Trial - Verdicts#If They Are Arrested]]).
- **The storehouse or the cages at Camp Carrionclay** ([[4.13 Camp Carrionclay]]).
- **The flying citadel's sublevels**, if the party sneaks in ([[6.06 The Flying Citadel]]).

## 1. The Plan

The players choose **one plan** and give **its detail**. That's all the planning there is. The characters did the rest off-screen. If the players start on guard rotations, stop them: that's what flashbacks are for.

| Plan | Detail | For example |
|---|---|---|
| **Assault** | The point of attack | "The back gate, at the change of watch." |
| **Deception** | The method | "We're a Dragon Army supply detail." |
| **Stealth** | The way in | "Over the north wall, where the tower is dark." |
| **Magic** | The spell or method | "Davos walks in invisible and opens the gate." |
| **Social** | The contact | "The cook owes Milo a favour." |
| **Transport** | The route and means | "Up the river in a fishing boat, under the nets." |

- **Don't know the detail?** Find it out first: a day watching the place, a Gather Information scene, a contact.
- **A diversion** ("Cudgel starts a fight at the front while we go over the back") is just a **Help** from whoever makes it. If it fails, the engagement roll gets -1d.

**Load.** Each player says how much they carry, not what:
- **Light:** **1** "of course I packed it". Nobody looks twice at you.
- **Normal:** **3**.
- **Heavy:** **5**, and disadvantage on Stealth.

An "of course I packed it" is any ordinary item they could have (rope, a crowbar, a Dragon Army tabard, oil). They name it when they need it.

## 2. The Engagement Roll

**Roll d6s and take the highest.** Start with **1d** for luck, and add or remove dice for big factors only:

| +1d | -1d |
|---|---|
| The plan is bold or daring | The plan is complicated, or hangs on many things going right |
| The detail hits a weak spot (the change of watch, the drunk sergeant) | The target is strongest against this plan, or ready for it |
| A friend or contact helps (a map, an inside man, a faction favour) | An enemy or rival is interfering |
| A soft target (a sleepy warehouse) | A hard target (Castle Kalaman, the citadel) |

At **0d**, roll 2d and take the **lowest**.

| Roll | They start | In D&D terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Two 6s** | **Past the first obstacle,** in control | Already inside. Advantage on the next check. |
| **6** | **In control** | At the first obstacle, unnoticed. Advantage on the first check there. |
| **4–5** | **At risk** | At the first obstacle. Normal checks. |
| **1–3** | **In trouble** | At the first obstacle, and something's going wrong right now: a guard turning round, a ward on the lock. The enemy acts first. |

Then **cut straight to the first obstacle**. The roll covers the approach, so never play out walking up to the walls, and the players can't back out and pick another window.

> [!tip] A bad roll isn't the party's fault. Show how capable the enemy is ("this place is better guarded than you thought"), and expect a flashback.

## 3. Stress

Each character has **4 Stress** for the job. It clears when the job is over and they've had a long rest.

**Flashbacks.** A player can call for a scene from before the job that changes things now. The DM sets the cost:
- **0:** ordinary, easy to have done ("I bribed the stable boy").
- **1:** complex, or an unlikely chance ("I hid my sword in the laundry cart").
- **2:** elaborate, with special chances or contingencies ("We found the old smugglers' tunnel in the city records").
- **Something that took downtime** (a forgery, a hired distraction) costs **gold** instead of Stress: 10 to 50 gp.
- If it needed skill, make the check in the flashback. A flashback can't undo what just happened in front of everyone: "we bribed the guard" works, "the guard was never there" doesn't. The best time for one is right after a bad engagement roll.

**Spend 1 Stress to:**
- **Push:** advantage on a check, by sheer nerve.
- **Resist:** something bad happens one step less badly: caught becomes suspicious, a hit does half damage, a dropped key lands on a rug.

**Breaking.** Someone who needs Stress with none left can still spend it, but they **break**: a level of exhaustion, and they're out of the job. They bolt, freeze or get caught.

## 4. Two Clocks

Draw two circles and cut them into segments. That's the only tracking.
- **Alarm (6).** Tick 1 for a failed Stealth or Deception check, a loud action, or something bad nobody resisted. Tick 2 for a body found or a noisy fight. **At 6, the alarm goes up**: horns, bells, the whole garrison. Switch to a normal objective fight or an escape.
- **The job (4–6).** Tick 1 for each real step: the yard crossed, the keys taken, the door open. **When it's full, they've done it.** Getting out counts, unless the plan says otherwise.

## 5. After the Job

Standing moves as the chapter says. A clean job (the Alarm never passed 3) earns **one extra +1** with the faction that sent them.

## Example: Wheelwatch

- **Engagement:** 1d, **+1d** for the survivor's sketch (a friend's help), **+1d** if they spent 8 hours watching the patrols (module) and strike at the change of watch. A fort that's expecting no one is neither soft nor hard.
- **Alarm:** the **alarm horns** in the towers. If the party destroys or guards them (module), the Alarm can't go past 5 until someone reaches a horn.
- **The job (5):** in, the prisoners found, the keys taken, the gate control reached, the gate open. The 10 Kalaman soldiers come in when it opens, and from there it's the module's battle.
- **Good flashbacks:** a Dragon Army tabard taken off a body at the farmstead (0), Cudgel's people starting a fight at the front gate on cue (1), a note slipped to a prisoner by a "peddler" the week before (2).

## Things to Watch

- **Did it skip the planning without losing the fun of being clever?** If they still plan for twenty minutes, be firmer.
- **Was 4 Stress enough?** If nobody ever breaks, try 3. If everybody breaks, try 5.
- **Did flashbacks feel like cheating?** They should feel like a heist film. If one strains belief, it costs 2 and needs a check.
